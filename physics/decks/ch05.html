The moment of a force

What is a moment?

Key term · moment

The turning effect of a force about a hinge or pivot is called its moment.

moment = force × perpendicular distance from the pivot
moment = F × d

Perpendicular means at right angles.

QuantityUnit
momentnewton metre (N m)
force Fnewton (N)
distance dmetre (m)
UnitsIf the distance is in cm, the moment is in N cm.

The moment of a force

How to get a bigger moment

  1. Use a bigger force. Bigger F → bigger F × d.
  2. Push further from the pivot. Door handles are far from the hinge. A long spanner loosens a tight nut more easily.
  3. Push at 90° to the lever. Then d is as big as it can be.

The principle of moments

When the turning effects on each side cancel out, the object balances and does not turn.

Physics only
Balance

When does something balance?

Principle of moments

For an object in balance: sum of anticlockwise moments = sum of clockwise moments

In simple wordsThe turning one way cancels the turning the other way, so nothing turns.
ACW: 300 × 1 = 300 N m · CW: 150 × 2 = 300 N m → level

Worked example

How hard must B pull?

A pushes with 200 N at 20 cm from the hinge. B pulls at 80 cm. What F balances the door?
BalanceB’s moment = A’s moment
SubstituteF × 80 = 200 × 20
Work outF = 4000 ÷ 80
F = 50 N
Note50 N only balances the door. To open it, B needs more than 50 N.

The long load arm reaches across the site.

The blocks are close to the pivot, so they must be very heavy.

Exam method

Solving any moments question

  1. Sketch it. Mark the pivot, each force and its distance.
  2. Sort the turns. Which forces turn clockwise? Which anticlockwise?
  3. Balance. Sum of anticlockwise moments = sum of clockwise moments. Solve.
  4. Check units. Same distance unit on both sides (all cm or all m).

Centre of gravity and stability

Where an object’s weight acts, and why some objects are hard to knock over.

Physics only
Centre of gravity

Where does the weight act?

Key term · centre of gravity

The point through which the whole weight of an object appears to act.

  • Also called the centre of mass.
  • Support it here: no turning moment, so it balances.
  • A uniform ruler balances at its middle.
Spec 1.31P know that the weight of a body acts through its centre of gravity

Centre of gravity

Pivot not at the centre of gravity

The object’s own weight now makes a moment about the pivot.

Here the weight is to the right of the pivot, so it turns the ruler clockwise.

Include it!Put the object’s weight in your moments calculation.

Worked example

What mass balances the ruler?

A uniform ruler (0.12 kg) is pivoted at 40 cm. A brass mass at 20 cm balances it. Find the mass.
Ruler’s CoG50 cm mark, 10 cm right of the pivot
Balance(m × g) × 20 = (0.12 × g) × 10
g cancelsm × 20 = 1.2
m = 0.06 kg
Using cm is fine because both sides use cm.
Stability · extension

Stable or unstable?

Stable

Hard to push over. When tilted and let go, it returns to its original position.

Low CoG + wide base

Stable, e.g. a ship’s decanter. Its weight pulls it back.

High CoG + narrow base

Unstable: a small tilt makes it fall over.

When tilted, the weight of a stable object acts inside its base and gives a moment that pulls it back upright.

Forces on a beam

How the upward forces from the supports change as a heavy load moves along a beam.

Physics only
Forces on a beam

A boy on a plank bridge

A boy (400 N) stands on a light beam supported at both ends. The beam is still, so total upward force = total downward force.

In the middle

Equal: half each.

At one end

That support takes it all.

A quarter along

Near support ¾, far one ¼.

The beam's own weight is ignored.

Forces on a beam

The key idea

Key ideaAs a load moves along a beam, the force at the nearer support increases. The force at the further support decreases.

Always

The two upward forces add up to the total weight.

Spec 1.33P understand how the upward forces on a light beam, supported at its ends, vary with the position of a heavy…
Forces on a beam

How to calculate the support forces

  1. Take moments about one support. That support’s force has no moment there.
  2. Moment of the load = moment of the other support’s force. Find that force.
  3. Subtract: other force = total weight − force just found.
  4. Beam’s weight counts? Add it acting at the beam’s centre of gravity (the middle of a uniform beam).

Why step 1?

A force through the pivot has d = 0, so it drops out.

CheckUpward forces must add up to the downward forces.

Forces holding up a shelf

2 m shelf, 10 N book 50 cm from Q. Find the forces at P and Q.
Momentsabout P (F at P has no moment)
SubstituteF(Q) × 2 = 10 × 1.5
F(Q) = 7.5 N
SubtractF(P) = 10 − 7.5
F(P) = 2.5 N
Q takes more force because the book is closer to Q.

Common mistakes

Don’t lose easy marks

Slanting distance

Use the perpendicular distance to the line of action, not the length along a slanting lever.

Forgetting the ruler

Add the beam’s or ruler’s own weight at its centre if it is not pivoted there.

Mixing cm and m

Use the same unit on both sides of the equation.

Don’t confuse!Moment (N m) is not momentum (kg m/s).
