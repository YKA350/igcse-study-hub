Two kinds of energy resource

Some resources will run out. Others are topped up by nature.

Physics only
Key words

Non-renewable or renewable?

Key term · non-renewable

Effectively cannot be replaced once used – it will run out.

Key term · renewable

Will not run out – it is continually replaced.

Why it matters

Electricity has to be generated

  • World energy demand keeps rising as the population grows.
  • Fossil fuels are being used up too quickly, and burning them damages the environment.
  • We must use energy efficiently and develop more renewables.
Common mistakeElectricity is not an energy resource. It must be generated from other resources.

Power stations and non-renewables

Coal, oil, gas and nuclear power stations all boil water to make steam.

Physics only
Power stations

How a thermal power station works

  1. Fuel burns – or uranium nuclei split.
  2. The heat boils water in a boiler.
  3. High-pressure steam spins the turbine.
  4. The turbine turns the generator.
  5. The generator makes electricity.
Energy storychemical or nuclear → thermal → kinetic → electrical
Coal, oil, gas and nuclear stations all work like this. Only the source of the heat is different.

Non-renewable

Fossil fuels: coal, oil and gas

Made slowly

From dead plants and tiny creatures, over millions of years.

Used quickly

Can’t be replaced on a human timescale – so they are non-renewable.

Too useful to burn?

Raw materials for chemicals, dyes and plastics – lost forever when burned.

The fuel

A particular isotope of uranium. Its nuclei split (fission) and release heat.

Why non-renewable?

Only a little is needed – but uranium was formed before the Solar System, so the supply is limited.

Nuclear power

Nuclear: the good and the bad

+ Advantages

  • “Clean”: no greenhouse or polluting gases
  • Huge energy from a little fuel → low cost per unit
  • Very reliable

− Disadvantages

  • Expensive to build and to decommission
  • Risk of accidents releasing radioactive material
  • Radioactive waste stays dangerous for a very long time
  • Slow to start up; long pay-back time
Common mistakeNuclear is “clean” – but it is still non-renewable.

Extension

Going further: CO₂ and nuclear waste

Carbon capture

Some companies are researching ways of storing CO₂ underground, so it does not reach the air.

Fast breeder reactors

Make more fuel (plutonium) than they use – but plutonium is extremely dangerous and used in nuclear weapons.

Storing waste

In concrete deep underground (e.g. the proposed Yucca Mountain site) for 300–1000 years.

Renewables · water

Tidal power

kineticmoving water
→
turbine + generator
→
electrical

A dam (barrage) across an estuary. Turbines turn as the tide flows in and again as it flows out. E.g. La Rance, France.

+ Advantages

  • Clean
  • Tides are predictable

− Disadvantages

  • Few suitable sites
  • Changes water levels → damages habitats
  • Not continuous
The energy comes from the gravitational pull of the Moon and the Sun, which causes the tides. A newer idea is underwater tidal-stream turbines.

Wave energy

kineticwaves
→

Waves (from tides and wind) push water up a tube. Rising water pushes air past a turbine; falling water sucks it back.

+ Advantages

  • Clean and renewable
  • No waste

− Disadvantages

  • Depends on the waves
  • Must survive storms
  • Limited output
This is an oscillating water column. The turbine drives a generator.

Solar

Solar heating panels

Matt black

Best absorber of infrared (IR).

Copper

Excellent conductor – passes heat to the water.

Glass + trapped air

Less heat lost by convection; traps IR.

Insulation + shiny foil

Less loss by conduction and radiation.

Energy transfer: light and infrared from the Sun → thermal energy of the water. The glass also traps IR by the greenhouse effect.

Solar

Solar power towers

Many curved mirrors, controlled to track the Sun, focus heat onto a boiler in a central tower.

  1. Sunlight is focused on the boiler.
  2. Water boils → steam.
  3. Steam spins a turbine.
  4. The generator makes electricity.

Key term · geothermal

Thermal energy stored deep inside the Earth.

Made by the decay of radioactive elements like uranium. In Iceland, steam and hot water drive turbines and heat homes.

+ Renewable, no pollution, reliable

− Only in certain (volcanic) areas

Geothermal has little environmental impact. Volcanoes themselves are not a safe resource.

Looking ahead

The future? Fusion

+ The hope

Huge energy without long-lived nuclear waste.

− The catch

No fusion reactor yet produces power on the scale we need.

Don’t mix them upFission = splitting a big nucleus. Fusion = forcing light nuclei together.
