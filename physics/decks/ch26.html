Moving in circles

Why do things go round?

  • A ball on a wire only goes round if it is pulled towards the centre all the time.
  • Let go, and it flies off in a straight line.
In spaceFor planets, moons and satellites, the pull is gravity.

Newton's gravity

Every mass pulls on every mass

Gravitational force

Any two masses attract each other.

  • Bigger masses: stronger force.
  • Bigger distance: weaker force.
  • Double the distance: ¼ of the force. Treble: 1⁄9.
Why we notice itOnly when one object is very massive: a moon, planet or star.

Comets

What is a comet?

Comet

A large rock-like lump of ice that orbits the Sun.

Extension: the tail

Near the Sun, gases evaporate and make a shining tail, always pointing away from the Sun.

Halley's comet returns every 76 years (last seen 1986).

A comet's orbit

Comets: stretched-out orbits

  • Sometimes very close to the Sun, sometimes at the edge of the Solar System.
  • Speed changes a lot: fastest near the Sun, slowest far away.
  • Most easily seen when close to the Sun.

Elliptical

Stretched out like a long oval, with the Sun off-centre.

Gravitational field strength

How hard each world pulls, and what that does to weight.

Gravitational field strength

How strong is gravity there?

Gravitational field strength, g

The strength of gravity on a planet or moon. Unit: N/kg.

  • Earth: 10 N/kg. Each kilogram is pulled with 10 N.
  • Moon: 1.6 N/kg, about ⅙ of Earth's.

In simple words

g tells you how hard a world pulls on each kilogram.

Orbital speed

Where the equation comes from

Start with

speed = distance ÷ time

  • One orbit is once round a circle.
  • Distance = circumference = 2πr.
  • Time = the time period, T.
v = 2πr ÷ T
2πr in mT in s

Key equation

Orbital speed

orbital speed = 2 × π × orbital radius ÷ time period
v = 2πr ÷ T
v in m/sr in mT in s

Rearranged

T = 2πr ÷ v  ·  r = vT ÷ 2π

  • r is measured from the centre of the planet.
  • T is the time for one complete orbit.
  • Works for planets too: r = average distance from the Sun.
UnitsChange km to m (× 1000) and hours or minutes to seconds.
