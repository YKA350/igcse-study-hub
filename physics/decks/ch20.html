What does a magnet pull?

Magnetic – attracted

iron · steel · nickel · cobalt

e.g. needles, paper clips, drawing pins, safety pins, steel nails.

Non-magnetic – not attracted

Plastic, wood, paper, rubber, glass – and most metals, such as copper and aluminium.

e.g. plastic pen lids, erasers, matches, rubber bands.

Poles

Like poles repel, unlike poles attract

Key term · poles

The strongest parts of a magnet are its poles – north (N) and south (S).

Magnets attract and repel other magnets. They only attract magnetic materials.

Two types of magnet

Permanent magnets and electromagnets

Permanent magnet

Has a magnetic field all the time. Its strength and direction are hard to change.

Electromagnet

A field only when switched on. Strength and direction are easy to change.

A scrapyard crane uses an electromagnet.

Magnetic fields

The invisible region around a magnet – and how we draw and map it.

Magnetic fields

The invisible region around a magnet

Key term · magnetic field

The region around a magnet where a magnetic material or another magnet feels a force.

  • We can’t see it.
  • Iron filings or plotting compasses show its shape.
Spec 6.4 understand the term 'magnetic field line'
Field lines

What field lines tell you

  1. Shape of the field
  2. Direction – from N to S (add arrows!)
  3. Strength – closer lines = stronger field
RememberStrongest at the poles. Field lines never cross.

Practical

Mapping the field with iron filings

  1. Magnet between two books, paper on top.
  2. Sprinkle iron filings.
  3. Tap gently – the filings line up with the field.
SafetyFilings irritate skin. Never blow them off – they hurt eyes.

Practical

Mapping the field with plotting compasses

  1. Put a compass near the N pole.
  2. Pencil dot where the needle points.
  3. Move the compass to the dot. Repeat.
  4. Join the dots. Add an arrow N → S.
ThenRepeat with two bar magnets about 5 cm apart.

Overlapping fields

Two magnets close together

N facing S

Lines run across the gap from N to S. The magnets attract.

N facing N

Lines push away from each other. At X the fields cancel – no field.

Uniform fields

A uniform magnetic field

Key term · uniform field

Its strength and direction are the same everywhere.

Drawn as straight, parallel, equally spaced lines with arrows from N to S.

Spec 6.4 understand the term 'magnetic field line'
Uniform fields

How to make a uniform field

  1. Put two bar magnets in a line, opposite poles (N and S) facing, a short distance apart.
  2. The field in the gap is uniform – best near the middle.
  3. Or use the poles of a U-shaped magnet.
Used inElectric motors, loudspeakers and generators.

Induced magnetism

Iron becomes a magnet – for a while

Key idea · induced magnetism

A magnetic material placed in a magnetic field becomes a magnet.

  • The end nearest the S pole becomes an N pole.
  • So induced magnetism always causes attraction.
Spec 6.5 know that magnetism is induced in some materials when they are placed in a magnetic field

Electromagnetism: fields round wires, coils and solenoids.

Electromagnetism

A current makes a magnetic field

Key term · electromagnetism

When there is a current in a wire, a magnetic field is created around it.

  • Oersted (1819) saw a compass needle move near a wire with a current.
  • The field is weak and circular.

Straight wire

Circles round a straight wire Physics only

  • Concentric circles, closer near the wire.
  • Right-hand grip rule: thumb = current, curled fingers = field.
  • Reverse the current → the field reverses.

Exam tip

Compass above and below a wire

The field goes round the wire, so a compass above points the opposite way to one below.

Reverse the current → both compasses flip.

Stronger fieldMore current, or coil the wire.

Solenoid

A long coil acts like a bar magnet Physics only

Key term · solenoid

A long coil of wire. The fields of all the turns add together.

  • Outside: the same shape as a bar magnet.
  • Inside: strong and nearly uniform.
  • Reverse the current → the poles swap.

Finding the poles of a solenoid Physics only

Stronger field

Coil the wire, or increase the current.

Electric bell

How an electric bell rings Physics only

  1. Push → current flows.
  2. Core magnetised → pulls the iron armature.
  3. Hammer hits the bell.
  4. Gap at the contact screw → current stops.
  5. Spring pulls the armature back → repeats.

Circuit breaker

A switch that turns itself off Physics only

  1. Current gets too large.
  2. Electromagnet strong enough to pull the iron catch.
  3. Contacts open → circuit breaks.
  4. Fix the fault, then press reset.

Common mistakes

Four ways to lose marks

Mistake“All metals are magnetic.”
Only iron, steel, nickel, cobalt.
MistakeField lines with no arrows, or arrows S → N.
Arrows go N → S.
MistakeField lines that cross or touch.
They never cross.
MistakeA uniform field with uneven spacing.
