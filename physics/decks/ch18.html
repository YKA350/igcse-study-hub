What is density?

How much mass is packed into a given volume — and the units you need.

Density

Density: how tightly is the mass packed?

Key idea · density

Density tells you how much mass is packed into a given volume.

density = mass ÷ volume
ρ = m ÷ V
ρ in kg/m³m in kgV in m³
Don't mix upρ (Greek “rho”) = density. Small p = pressure.
A hot-air balloon rises because the hot gas inside has a low density.

Practical: measuring density

Measure the mass and the volume of a sample, then calculate ρ = m ÷ V.

Practical · regular solid

Density of a regular solid

volume = length × width × height
V = l × w × h
l, w, h in m
  1. Measure the mass on a balance.
  2. Measure l, w and h with a ruler (e.g. a half-metre rule).
  3. Calculate ρ = m ÷ V.
  4. Repeat and take a mean.
Spec 5.4 practical: investigate density using direct measurements of mass and volume
Practical · irregular solid

Volume by displacement

  1. Fill a displacement (eureka) can up to the spout.
  2. Gently lower the object in.
  3. Collect the water pushed out in a measuring cylinder.
Key factVolume of water displaced = volume of the object.

Practical · accuracy

Reading the measuring cylinder

  1. Stand the cylinder on a level surface.
  2. Put your eye level with the liquid and look straight on.
  3. Read the bottom of the meniscus.
Parallax errorLooking from above or below makes the reading wrong.

Pressure under solids

How a force is spread over an area.

Pressure

What is pressure?

Definition · pressure

Pressure is the force per unit area.

pressure = force ÷ area
p = F ÷ A
p in PaF in NA in m²
Pascal1 Pa = 1 N/m² · 1 kPa = 1000 Pa
Spec 5.5 know and use the relationship between pressure, force and area: pressure = force ÷ area, p = F/A
Pressure

Rearranging p = F ÷ A

Find pressure

p = F ÷ A

Find force

F = p × A

Find area

A = F ÷ p

Bigger force → bigger pressure

Bigger area → smaller pressure

Spec 5.5 know and use the relationship between pressure, force and area: pressure = force ÷ area, p = F/A
Pressure

Small area or large area?

Small area → high pressure

Drawing pins, sharp knives, scissors and bolt cutters.

Large area → low pressure

Tracks, wide tyres, skis and camel feet — less sinking.

Pressure in liquids and gases

In a still fluid, pressure pushes in every direction.

Fluids

Pressure acts in all directions

Key rule

In a liquid or gas at rest, the pressure at a point acts equally in all directions.

Not just downwards — sideways and upwards too. A submerged object feels pressure on all its surfaces.

Spec 5.6 understand how the pressure at a point in a gas or liquid at rest acts equally in all directions
Fluids

Atmospheric pressure

Air pressure ≈ 100 000 Pa

Gases push on everything around them. About 100 kPa — it varies a little from day to day.

Why don't we feel it?

The pressure inside our bodies is similar, so the pushes balance.

Deep down

A deep-sea submersible needs a very strong hull — water pressure is huge at depth.

Fluids

The Magdeburg hemispheres

1654, Magdeburg

Otto von Guericke pumped the air out of two large metal bowls. Two teams of horses could not pull them apart!

With the air removed, pressure acts only from outside. Let air back in and they separate.

Pressure and depth

The deeper you go, the bigger the pressure: p = h × ρ × g.

Depth

Pressure increases with depth

What you see

Water spurts further from the lower holes.

Why

The deeper you go, the more liquid is pressing down from above — so the pressure is bigger.

Depth

The pressure difference equation

pressure difference = height × density × g
p = h × ρ × g
p in Pah in mρ in kg/m³g = 10 N/kg
Any fluidWorks for any liquid or gas if you know its density. Always use h in metres!
Water 10 cm deep. What is the pressure difference?
Write downh = 0.1 m, ρ = 1000 kg/m³, g = 10 N/kg
Equationp = h × ρ × g
Substitutep = 0.1 × 1000 × 10
p = 1000 Pa
Spec 5.7 know and use the relationship for pressure difference: pressure difference = height × density ×…
Extra · not examined

Where p = h × ρ × g comes from

  1. volume = A × h
  2. mass = A × h × ρ
  3. weight F = A × h × ρ × g
  4. p = F ÷ A = h × ρ × g
Big ideaThe area cancels — the width of the container doesn't matter, only the depth.
ExamLearn the equation; you don't need the proof.

Looking ahead

Air pressure and the weather

Blaise Pascal

The unit is named after him. He carried a barometer up a mountain: air pressure decreases with height.

Weather maps

Air flows from high to low pressure → wind. Close isobars = very strong winds.
