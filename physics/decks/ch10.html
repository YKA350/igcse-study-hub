What is a wave?

Waves carry energy. There are two kinds: transverse and longitudinal.

Waves basics

Waves carry energy, not matter

Key idea

Waves transfer energy and information from place to place without transferring matter.

Stone in a pond

Circular ripples carry energy to the edge. The water itself does not travel there.

Drop a stone in a pond: ripples spread out and carry energy, but a leaf just bobs up and down. Like a Mexican wave in a stadium.

Transverse waves

Transverse: vibrations at 90°

Transverse wave

The vibrations are at right angles to the direction the wave (energy) travels.

Examples

Light and all electromagnetic waves, waves on water, waves on a rope.

Shake a rope up and down: the wave moves along, each bit of rope only moves up and down. A slinky moved side to side also makes a transverse wave.

Longitudinal waves

Longitudinal: vibrations along the wave

Longitudinal wave

The vibrations are along (parallel to) the direction the wave travels.

Squash and stretch

Compressions = squashed together. Rarefactions = spread out.

Example

Sound waves.

Push and pull one end of a slinky spring along its length. Sound is longitudinal: air particles move back and forth.

Wavefronts

Drawing waves as wavefronts

Wavefront

A line joining points that are vibrating in phase and are the same distance from the source, e.g. all the crests.

DirectionThe wave travels at right angles to its wavefronts.
A point source like a stone gives circular wavefronts. A straight vibrating bar gives straight, plane wavefronts.

Frequency and period

Frequency = 1 ÷ time period

frequency = 1 ÷ time period
f = 1 ÷ T
f in HzT in s

Turn it round

T = 1 ÷ f. Short time for one wave → lots of waves each second → high frequency.

A source has a frequency of 200 Hz. Find the time period.
Write downf = 200 Hz, T = ?
EquationT = 1 ÷ f
SubstituteT = 1 ÷ 200
T = 0.005 s (5 ms)

The wave equation

Wave speed = f × λ

wave speed = frequency × wavelength
v = f × λ
v in m/sf in Hzλ in m

Rearranged

λ = v ÷ f   ·   f = v ÷ λ

All wavesWorks for sound, light, water waves…
If 4 waves pass each second and each is 3 m long, the waves travel 4 × 3 = 12 m every second, so v = 12 m/s.

Worked example · sound

A tuning fork

A tuning fork makes a 170 Hz note. The speed of sound is 340 m/s. Find the wavelength.
Write downf = 170 Hz, v = 340 m/s, λ = ?
Equationλ = v ÷ f
Substituteλ = 340 ÷ 170
λ = 2 m
Sound is a wave, so v = f × λ works for it. Cover λ on the triangle: you are left with v over f.

Speed stays the same

Refraction

All waves can be refracted

Refraction

The change in direction of a wave when it passes from one material into another.

Why?

The wave changes speed. Light travels more slowly in water and glass than in air.

Like a shopping trolley rolling at an angle from a smooth floor onto carpet: one wheel slows first, so it turns.

The Doppler effect

Why a siren sounds higher as it comes towards you and lower as it goes away.

The Doppler effect

When the source moves

Still source

Wavefronts are evenly spaced. Everyone hears the same frequency.

Moving source

Wavefronts are squashed in front and stretched behind.

Each new wavefront starts a little further along, so they bunch up in front and spread out behind.

Watch out

Common mistakes

"The water moves with the wave"No. Waves transfer energy, not matter. The water moves up and down.
"The source changes its frequency"No. Only the observed frequency changes, because the wavefronts are squashed or stretched.
Amplitude crest to troughNo. Measure from the rest position to a crest.
