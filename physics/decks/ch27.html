Brightness

Why brightness is tricky

Car headlights

A bright headlight far away can look dimmer than a weak one close by.

  • Its distance from the Earth.
  • Its luminosity: the energy it gives out each second.

Luminosity depends on what the star is made of and its nuclear reactions.

Spec 8.11P understand how the brightness of a star at a standard distance can be represented using absolute magnitude
Brightness Physics only

Three brightness words

1 · Apparent magnitude

How bright the star looks from Earth.

Easiest to measure, but distance changes it.

2 · Absolute magnitude

How bright the star would look at a standard distance from Earth: 10 parsecs (32.6 light years).

Lets scientists compare stars fairly.

3 · Luminosity

The total energy emitted as light from the star's surface every second.

Spec 8.11P understand how the brightness of a star at a standard distance can be represented using absolute magnitude
Apparent and absolute Physics only

Apparent vs absolute

Apparent

How bright it looks from Earth. Depends on its distance and luminosity.

Absolute

How bright it would look at 10 parsecs. Depends only on the star itself.

Birth

From nebula to star

Key term · nebula

A cloud of dust and gas, pulled together by gravity over a long time.

  • Nuclear fusion of hydrogen starts: a young star.
  • Leftover material may form planets, moons and comets.

Main sequence

A balance of forces

Main sequence

Gravity pulling in is balanced by the outward pressure from the hot gas.

  • The hot gas is heated by fusion energy.
  • A stable stage that lasts millions or billions of years.

The Sun is here now

Stars like the Sun

The red giant stage

  1. The hydrogen runs out in the core and fusion stops.
  2. Gravity compresses the star.
  3. It heats up and helium fusion starts.
  4. It expands hugely and cools a little: a red giant.

Stars like the Sun

White dwarf, then black dwarf

White dwarf

When the helium is used up, the star contracts and heats up. White/blue and extremely dense.

Black dwarf

The white dwarf slowly cools until it stops glowing.

Red supergiant

After the main sequence, a star much more massive than the Sun becomes a red supergiant.

Supernova

It contracts, becomes unstable and explodes. Dust and gas are thrown into space and can form a new nebula.

After a supernova

Neutron star or black hole

Neutron star

The core left behind becomes a very dense neutron star.

Black hole

If the remaining mass is about 3 times the Sun's or more, it collapses into a black hole. Not even light can escape.

Extension

Stars make the elements

Fusion in stars

Makes elements up to iron.

Supernovae

The only place that makes elements heavier than iron.

Key fact · supernova

The largest explosion in space, caused by the core of a massive star collapsing under gravity.

HR diagram Physics only

Key features

Axes

Luminosity up the side (up = brighter). Temperature or class along the bottom.

Backwards axis

Hot (O, blue) on the left, cool (M, red) on the right.

Main sequence

Diagonal band: most stars, fusing hydrogen.

Giants and supergiants: top-right. Cool but very bright, because they are huge.

White dwarfs: bottom-left. Hot but dim, because they are tiny.
