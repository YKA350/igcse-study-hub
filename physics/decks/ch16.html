Work done

When a force moves something, work is done and energy is transferred.

Key ideas

Energy and work

Key idea · energy

Energy is the ability to do work.

RememberWork done = energy transferred. When a force moves something, energy moves from one store to another.

The joule (J)

Unit of energy and work. Named after James Joule, who showed kinetic energy can be transferred to heat.

The watt (W)

Unit of power. Named after James Watt, famous for the steam engine.

Potential and kinetic energy

Energy stored by height (GPE) and energy stored by movement (KE).

Gravitational potential energy

GPE = m × g × h

gravitational potential energy = mass × gravitational field strength × height
GPE = m × g × h
GPE in Jm in kgg in N/kgh in m
  • g ≈ 10 N/kg on Earth.
  • Lifted up against gravity → GPE increases.
  • Falls → GPE decreases.
  • We usually take GPE = 0 at the ground.

Gravitational potential energy

Where does mgh come from?

To lift mass m you need a force equal to its weightF = m × g
→
Lift it through height h: work done = F × dW = mg × h
→
The work done becomes GPE gainedGPE = m × g × h
Same ideaGPE = mgh is just W = F × d with F = mg and d = h.
Exam tipGiven a mass in kg? Multiply by g (10 N/kg) to get the weight in N.

The mountain reservoir

A reservoir holds 2 × 10⁸ litres (2 × 10⁸ kg) of water, 800 m above a power station. Find its GPE.
Write downm = 2 × 10⁸ kg, g = 10 N/kg, h = 800 m

Kinetic energy

KE = ½ × m × v²

Key term · kinetic energy

Kinetic energy (KE) is the energy stored by moving objects.

kinetic energy = ½ × mass × speed²
KE = ½ × m × v²
KE in Jm in kgv in m/s
Speed is squaredDouble the speed → 4 × the KE.

The meteorite

A meteorite of mass 500 kg travels at 1000 m/s. What is its kinetic energy?

Linking work, GPE and KE

Conservation of energy: what goes up as GPE comes back down as KE.

Conservation of energy

A falling object: GPE → KE

As it falls it loses GPE and gains KE. The total stays the same (ignoring air resistance).

On landing

KE → heat, sound and squashing (deforming) the object and the ground.

Conservation of energy

Loss of GPE = gain in KE

The book says

The loss of GPE is equal to the gain in KE.

The linkwork done lifting = GPE gained = KE just before landing
Spec 4.15 understand how conservation of energy produces a link between gravitational potential energy, kinetic…
Key equation

Falling or rising: mgh = ½mv²

GPE lost = KE gained (no energy wasted)
mgh = ½mv²

1 · Mass cancels

Divide both sides by m:

gh = ½v²

2 · Find speed

Rearrange for v:

v = √(2gh)

3 · Find height

Rearrange for h:

h = v² ÷ 2g

ExtensionThe same result comes from v² = u² + 2as with u = 0, a = g and s = h.

Worked example 4

The rollercoaster

A rollercoaster falls 17 m (g = 10 N/kg). How fast is it going at the bottom?
Assumeall GPE → KE
Equationv² = 2gh
Substitutev² = 2 × 10 × 17 = 340, v = √340
v = 18.4 m/s (≈ 66 km/h)

The real speed is a little less: some GPE becomes heat and sound.

Worked example

The catapult: going up

A 0.04 kg stone is fired straight up with 48 J of KE. How high does it go?
At the topall KE → GPE, so KE = m × g × h
Substitute48 = 0.04 × 10 × h
Rearrangeh = 48 ÷ 0.4
h = 120 m
Simple wordsGoing up is falling in reverse: KE turns back into GPE.
Worked example

The dropped coin

A coin is dropped from 80 m. How fast is it going when it lands?
Write downh = 80 m, g = 10 N/kg
Equationv = √(2gh)
Substitutev = √(2 × 10 × 80) = √1600
v = 40 m/s
Exam tipAlways state: "all the GPE is transferred to KE" (no air resistance). Real answers are a bit less.

Power

Power tells us how fast energy is transferred.

Power

Power = rate of doing work

Key term · power

Power is the rate of transfer of energy or the rate of doing work.

power = work done (energy transferred) ÷ time taken
P = W ÷ t
P in watts (W)W in Jt in s
Units1 W = 1 J/s (one joule per second) · 1 kW = 1000 W

Worked example 5

The lift motor

A lift and passengers weigh 4000 N and rise at a steady 3 m/s. What useful power does the motor give?
Forcesteady speed, so upward force = weight = 4000 N
Each secondW = F × d = 4000 × 3 = 12 000 J
PowerP = W ÷ t = 12 000 ÷ 1
P = 12 000 W (12 kW)
This is useful power only: the motor also wastes energy as heat and sound.

Practical

Working out stair power

A 40 kg student runs up 20 steps, each 0.2 m high, in 12 s. What is their power?
Weight40 × 10 = 400 N
Height20 × 0.2 = 4 m
Work400 × 4 = 1600 J
PowerP = 1600 ÷ 12
P ≈ 133 W
power = weight × n × d ÷ time
P = weight × n × d ÷ t
Why it worksRunning up the stairs, you do work against your weight. That work becomes GPE.
