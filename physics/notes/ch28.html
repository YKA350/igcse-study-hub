Chapter in a nutshell

  • Doppler effect: a moving source changes observed f and λ
  • Approaching: higher f, shorter λ. Away: lower f, longer λ
  • Away = red-shift. Towards = blue-shift
  • Further galaxies: bigger red-shift, faster away
  • Δλ ÷ λ₀ = v ÷ c, with c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s
  • Red-shift shows expansion, so the Big Bang
  • CMB: stretched afterglow from all directions
  • Dark energy: expansion may be accelerating

Evidence 1 · Red-shift

Light from galaxies is stretched. The further away the galaxy, the bigger the stretch.

The Universe is expanding.

The cosmic microwave background: the “afterglow” of the Big Bang, detected in all directions.

1The Doppler effect
Waves from a moving source
Key term · Doppler effect

When a wave source moves relative to an observer, the observed frequency and wavelength change.

  • Described by Christian Doppler in 1842.
  • It is a property of all waves: sound and light.
RememberThe car makes the same sound all the time. Only what the observer hears changes.

Light does it too

Stationary star

We see its normal wavelength.

Moving away

Light is stretched: longer wavelength, towards the red end. Red-shift.

Moving towards us

Light is squashed: shorter wavelength, towards the blue end. Blue-shift.

Red-shift and blue-shift

Red-shift

Source moving away. Lines move towards the red end.

Blue-shift

Source moving towards us. Lines move towards the blue end.

Common mistakeRed-shift does not mean the galaxy looks red. Its lines have moved.
Faster means a bigger shift

Speed

The faster the source moves, the greater the change in frequency and wavelength.

Spectra tell us

We cannot see galaxies move in a telescope. Their spectra show how fast and which way.

3The Doppler equation
The Doppler (red-shift) equation
change in wavelength ÷ reference wavelength = velocity of galaxy ÷ speed of light
Δλ ÷ λ₀ = v ÷ c
Δλ = λ − λ₀v in m/sc = 3 × 10⁸ m/s

λ₀

Reference wavelength from a stationary source, e.g. the Sun.

λ

Observed wavelength from the galaxy.

Find speed

v = (Δλ ÷ λ₀) × c

Reading the equation

λ bigger than λ₀

Red-shift. Moving away.

λ smaller than λ₀

Blue-shift. Moving towards us.

Units cancelΔλ and λ₀ are both wavelengths. Use the same unit (nm) for both.
How fast is the galaxy?
The Sun’s line is at λ₀ = 434 nm. In a galaxy it is at λ = 482 nm. c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s. Find v.
ChangeΔλ = 482 − 434 = 48 nm
Equationv = (Δλ ÷ λ₀) × c
Substitutev = (48 ÷ 434) × 3 × 10⁸
v = 3.32 × 10⁷ m/s

Direction

λ is longer than λ₀: red-shift. The galaxy is moving away.

Check

33 200 km/s. Much slower than light.

Half the speed of light
The Sun’s line is at 600 nm. In a galaxy the lines are shifted by 300 nm. Find v.
KnowΔλ = 300 nm, λ₀ = 600 nm
Substitutev = (300 ÷ 600) × 3 × 10⁸
Work outv = 0.5 × 3 × 10⁸
v = 1.5 × 10⁸ m/s

Meaning

Half the speed of light. Shifted towards red, so moving away.

Common mistakeMixing up λ and λ₀. λ₀ is the reference value.

4CMB radiation
The CMB radiation
  1. A Big Bang would release huge energy as EM waves.
  2. As the Universe expanded, the waves were stretched to much longer wavelengths.
  3. They were predicted to now be microwaves.
  4. Detected in the 1960s from all directions.

Explaining the evidence
  1. Light from distant galaxies shows red-shift: lines moved to longer wavelengths.
  2. So the galaxies are moving away from us (Doppler effect).
  3. More distant galaxies have greater red-shift, so they move faster: the Universe is expanding.
  4. So in the past everything was in one place: the Big Bang.
  5. CMB from all directions is the stretched afterglow of its energy.

Looking ahead: dark energy

Big Crunch?

Gravity could slow the expansion and pull everything back. More mass means more slowing.

Accelerating

Hubble telescope observations of very distant galaxies suggest the expansion is speeding up.

Unknown causeScientists cannot explain it yet. They call it dark energy.
