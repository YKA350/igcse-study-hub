Energy Resources

Physics · Unit 4 · Energy resources and energy transfers · Last-minute notes · Book pages 158–167

Chapter in a nutshell

  • Non-renewable: coal, oil, gas, nuclear
  • Renewable: wind, water, solar, geothermal, wood (if replanted)
  • Thermal station: heat → steam → turbine → generator
  • Fossil fuels: CO₂ (global warming), SO₂ (acid rain)
  • Nuclear: clean, but waste, accidents and cost
  • Hydro: fastest response + pumped storage
  • Wind and solar: clean but unreliable
  • Solar panel: matt black, copper, glass, insulation
  • Gas and hydro respond quickly to demand
  • Pay-back time = time for income to cover building cost
1Two kinds of energy resource
Non-renewable or renewable?
Key term · non-renewable

Effectively cannot be replaced once used – it will run out.

Key term · renewable

Will not run out – it is continually replaced.

Electricity has to be generated
  • World energy demand keeps rising as the population grows.
  • Fossil fuels are being used up too quickly, and burning them damages the environment.
  • We must use energy efficiently and develop more renewables.
Common mistakeElectricity is not an energy resource. It must be generated from other resources.

How a thermal power station works
Fossil fuels: coal, oil and gas

Made slowly

From dead plants and tiny creatures, over millions of years.

Used quickly

Can’t be replaced on a human timescale – so they are non-renewable.

Too useful to burn?

Raw materials for chemicals, dyes and plastics – lost forever when burned.

Why we still use fossil fuels

+ Advantages

  • Reliable – available whenever needed
  • Gas power stations start up quickly
  • Relatively cheap to build

− But…

  • They will run out
  • They all release carbon dioxide

The fuel

A particular isotope of uranium. Its nuclei split (fission) and release heat.

Why non-renewable?

Only a little is needed – but uranium was formed before the Solar System, so the supply is limited.

Nuclear: the good and the bad

+ Advantages

  • “Clean”: no greenhouse or polluting gases
  • Huge energy from a little fuel → low cost per unit
  • Very reliable

− Disadvantages

  • Expensive to build and to decommission
  • Risk of accidents releasing radioactive material
  • Radioactive waste stays dangerous for a very long time
  • Slow to start up; long pay-back time
Common mistakeNuclear is “clean” – but it is still non-renewable.
Going further: CO₂ and nuclear waste

Carbon capture

Some companies are researching ways of storing CO₂ underground, so it does not reach the air.

Fast breeder reactors

Make more fuel (plutonium) than they use – but plutonium is extremely dangerous and used in nuclear weapons.

Storing waste

In concrete deep underground (e.g. the proposed Yucca Mountain site) for 300–1000 years.

About 15% efficient – but improving and getting cheaper. Used in PV farms, on satellites (e.g. Hubble) and far from the mains.

+ Advantages

  • Renewable
  • No greenhouse gases

− Disadvantages

  • Large areas spoil the countryside or use farmland
  • No output at night
  • Low efficiency
Solar heating panels
Getting the most from solar heating

Face the Sun

In the northern hemisphere, panels face south, angled to catch the Sun directly.

Good value

Solar heating panels are more cost-efficient than PV cells.

Energy transferlight / IR from the Sun → thermal energy of the water
Solar power towers

Many curved mirrors, controlled to track the Sun, focus heat onto a boiler in a central tower.

  1. Sunlight is focused on the boiler.
  2. Water boils → steam.
  3. Steam spins a turbine.
  4. The generator makes electricity.

Cost and pay-back time
Key term · pay-back time

The time for the income to cover the building cost.

Nuclear

Cheap fuel, but very expensive to build, run safely and decommission → very long pay-back.

Wind

Cheap to set up, no fuel cost – but low output → also long.

The future? Fusion

+ The hope

Huge energy without long-lived nuclear waste.

− The catch

No fusion reactor yet produces power on the scale we need.

Don’t mix them upFission = splitting a big nucleus. Fusion = forcing light nuclei together.
Mistakes that lose marks
Too vagueNot “bad for the environment” – say “releases carbon dioxide, a greenhouse gas, causing global warming”.
“Clean”means it makes no greenhouse or polluting gases while generating.
Nuclearis clean but non-renewable. Long-lasting dangerous waste → nuclear.
Location mattersHydro – mountains and rain · tidal – an estuary · geothermal – volcanic areas · wind – windy sites · wave – the coast.
