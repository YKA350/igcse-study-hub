1The motor effect
Moving charges get pushed Physics only
The demonstration: the wire jumps

What happens

Close the switch → current flows → the wire jumps upwards. This is the motor effect.

Reading the symbol

A dot ● means current comes out of the page. A cross × means into the page.

Why? Two fields overlap
  1. The magnet makes a straight field (N → S). The current makes a circular field round the wire.
  2. Same direction → fields add → strong. Opposite → they partly cancel → weak.
  3. The wire is pushed from the strong side towards the weak side.
Make the force bigger — or flip it

Bigger force

  • Larger current
  • Stronger magnetic field

Reverse the force

  • Reverse the current
  • Or swap the poles
  • Reverse both → force goes back to the first direction
RememberThe force depends on the size and direction of both the current and the field.
Your left hand finds the force

Hold your thumb, first and second fingers of your LEFT hand at right angles.

  2. seCond finger → Current (+ to −)
  3. thuMb → Motion (force)
Watch outOnly works when the current is at right angles to the field.

2Loudspeakers and d.c. motors
The moving-coil loudspeaker
  1. A changing current (the signal, e.g. from a radio) flows in the coil.
  2. The coil sits in the magnet's field → a motor-effect force.
  3. The current keeps changing size and direction → the coil moves in and out.
  4. The paper cone vibrates → pushes the air → sound waves.
How a simple d.c. motor turns
  1. Current flows opposite ways in the two sides → one side is pushed up, the other down → the loop turns.
  2. At the vertical position its momentum carries it past.
  3. The split-ring commutator reverses the current every half turn → it keeps turning the same way.

Carbon brushes press on the split ring to connect the supply while it spins.

Faster, backwards, and real motors

Turn faster (more force)

  • More turns of wire (a coil)
  • Stronger magnetic field
  • Larger current

Turn the other way

Reverse the current, or swap the magnet's poles.

Uses

Electric trains, drills, fans, washing machines — anything that turns electricity into movement.

Extension: real motors

  • Curved electromagnets → very strong field
  • Several coils on one axle → more powerful, smoother
  • Coils on a plastic-coated soft iron core → more efficient

Mains electricity comes from huge generators in power stations. Building sites use portable generators.

A · Move a wire across a field

Move the wire at right angles to the field → a voltage is induced (made) in it.

In a complete circuit a current flows — the ammeter needle moves.

Key term

This is called electromagnetic induction.

B · Push a magnet into a coil

Push it in

Field lines cut the coil → current flows.

Pull it out

Current flows the opposite way.

Hold it still

No current at all.

The rule — and how to get more
The rule

A voltage is induced when a conductor cuts through magnetic field lines, or when field lines cut through a conductor (the field through a coil changes).

Bigger induced voltage

  • Move the wire or magnet faster
  • Use a stronger magnet
  • Use a coil with more turns
RememberNo movement → no cutting → no voltage.
Extension · Faraday's lawThe induced voltage is directly proportional to the rate of cutting of field lines.
4Generating electricity
A · Spin a magnet in a coil: the dynamo

A dynamo is a simple generator.

  1. Pedalling turns the wheel → a small magnet spins.
  2. Its field lines cut the coil → a voltage is induced.
  3. A current flows to the bike lights.
So…Pedal faster → magnet spins faster → brighter lights.
B · Spin a coil in a field: the alternator
  1. The coil turns between the poles.
  2. Each side moves up, then down, through the field.
  3. So the current changes direction every half turn → alternating current.
  4. Slip rings and brushes connect the turning coil to the circuit.
Key term · alternator

A generator that produces alternating current.

d.c. flows one way — a.c. keeps reversing
Frequency

Number of complete cycles per second, in hertz (Hz). UK mains: 50 Hz.

ExampleCoil turns twice a second → 2 cycles each second
frequency = 2 Hz
Power-station generators are much bigger

Here the magnets stay still and the coils rotate. They make a much bigger voltage than a bike dynamo because they have:

Stronger magnets

Much stronger field

More turns

Many more turns on the coil

Faster spin

Coils spin much faster

Two ways to generate — learn both

  • Rotate a magnet inside a coil (dynamo)
  • Rotate a coil inside a magnetic field (alternator)

Bigger voltage

Faster rotation, stronger magnet, more turns on the coil — plus a soft iron core.

5Transformers and the National Grid
Two coils on one iron core Physics only
  1. a.c. in the primary coil makes a magnetic field that keeps changing.
  2. The soft iron core carries this changing field to the secondary coil.
  3. The changing field cuts the secondary turns → an alternating voltage is induced.
Step-up or step-down — a.c. only Physics only

Step-up

ns > np → voltage up

Step-down

ns < np → voltage down

a.c. onlyd.c. makes a steady field → no voltage is induced.
Making transformers efficient Physics only

Thick copper wire

For the coils: low resistance, so less heating and less energy wasted.

Soft iron core

Made of thin plastic-coated layers, which also keeps energy losses small.

RememberA 100% efficient transformer has power in = power out.
The turns-ratio equation Physics only
input (primary) voltage ÷ output (secondary) voltage = primary turns ÷ secondary turns
Vp / Vs = np / ns
Vp, Vs in volts (V)np, ns = number of turns

Exam tip · 3-mark answerStep-up transformers increase the voltage and so decrease the current in the cables. A smaller current causes less heating, so less energy is wasted. Step-down transformers then reduce the voltage to a safe level (≈ 230 V) for homes.
