Chapter in a nutshell

  • Gravity pulls moons to planets, and planets and comets to the Sun
  • Bigger masses and shorter distances: stronger force
  • Planets: nearly circular. Moons: circular. Comets: very elliptical
  • Close planets: faster, shorter orbits
  • g is larger for more mass, smaller for a larger radius
  • W = m × g: mass stays, weight changes
  • v = 2πr ÷ T

1The Solar System and gravity
Eight planets round one star

The planets orbit the Sun in nearly circular paths. The asteroids lie between Mars and Jupiter.

Galaxies and the Universe

The Universe

A large collection of billions of galaxies.

A galaxy

A large collection of billions of stars.

Our home

The Solar System is in the Milky Way galaxy.

Why do things go round?
  • A ball on a wire only goes round if it is pulled towards the centre all the time.
  • Let go, and it flies off in a straight line.
In spaceFor planets, moons and satellites, the pull is gravity.
Every mass pulls on every mass
Gravitational force

Any two masses attract each other.

  • Bigger masses: stronger force.
  • Bigger distance: weaker force.
  • Double the distance: ¼ of the force. Treble: 1⁄9.
Why we notice itOnly when one object is very massive: a moon, planet or star.
Close planets feel a stronger pull
not to scale
The Sun

Over 99% of the Solar System's mass. Its gravity pulls every planet.

Close planets

Stronger pull: more curved path, faster, shorter orbit.

Far planets

Weak pull: least curved path, very long orbit time.

2Moons, satellites and comets
Moons: natural satellites
not to scale
Satellite

An object that orbits a planet. Natural satellites are called moons.

  • Moons have circular orbits.
  • Our Moon is the 5th largest in the Solar System.
  • Mars has 2 moons. Jupiter and Saturn have more than 60 each.
Satellites we launched

Launched since the late 1950s. Held in orbit by gravity.

High orbits

Communications: phone, video, internet. Slower.

Low (polar) orbits

Watch Earth's surface: ocean temperatures, forest fires. Faster.

Geostationary satellites
not to scale
Geostationary

A satellite that takes 24 hours to orbit the Earth.

So it stays

Earth also turns once a day, so the satellite stays over the same spot.

Used for

Communications: phone, video and the internet. About 35 786 km up, at 3.07 km/s.

What is a comet?
Comet

A large rock-like lump of ice that orbits the Sun.

Extension: the tail

Near the Sun, gases evaporate and make a shining tail, always pointing away from the Sun.

Halley's comet returns every 76 years (last seen 1986).

Comets: stretched-out orbits
  • Sometimes very close to the Sun, sometimes at the edge of the Solar System.
  • Speed changes a lot: fastest near the Sun, slowest far away.
  • Most easily seen when close to the Sun.

Elliptical

Stretched out like a long oval, with the Sun off-centre.

3Gravitational field strength
How strong is gravity there?
Gravitational field strength, g

The strength of gravity on a planet or moon. Unit: N/kg.

  • Earth: 10 N/kg. Each kilogram is pulled with 10 N.
  • Moon: 1.6 N/kg, about ⅙ of Earth's.

In simple words

g tells you how hard a world pulls on each kilogram.

Two things change g
MassLarger mass gives a larger g.
RadiusLarger radius gives a smaller g at the surface.

Where the equation comes from
Start with

speed = distance ÷ time

  • One orbit is once round a circle.
  • Distance = circumference = 2πr.
  • Time = the time period, T.
v = 2πr ÷ T
2πr in mT in s
Orbital speed
orbital speed = 2 × π × orbital radius ÷ time period
v = 2πr ÷ T
v in m/sr in mT in s

Rearranged

T = 2πr ÷ v  ·  r = vT ÷ 2π

  • r is measured from the centre of the planet.
  • T is the time for one complete orbit.
  • Works for planets too: r = average distance from the Sun.
UnitsChange km to m (× 1000) and hours or minutes to seconds.
A low-orbit satellite

The problem

200 km above Earth. One orbit takes 1 h 24 min. Earth's radius is 6400 km. Find v.

1. Radius from the centre

r = 6400 + 200 = 6600 km
= 6 600 000 m

2. Time in seconds

T = (60 + 24) × 60 = 5040 s

Now substitute
orbital speed
v = 2πr ÷ T

3. Substitute

v = 2 × π × 6 600 000 ÷ 5040
v = 41 500 000 ÷ 5040

4. Answer with the unit
v ≈ 8200 m/s

That is about 8.2 km/s.

Checkr is from the centre, not the surface.
A geostationary satellite

The problem

Height 35 786 km, speed 3.07 km/s. How long is one orbit?

1. Radius

r = 35 786 + 6400 = 42 186 km

2. Rearrange and substitute

T = 2πr ÷ v = 2π × 42 186 ÷ 3.07

About 24 hours
3. Answer with the unit
T ≈ 86 300 s

That is about 24 hours.

SoIt orbits once a day, the same time Earth turns once, so it stays over the same point.
UnitsUsing km and s together is fine if you stay consistent.
