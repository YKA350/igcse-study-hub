Properties of Waves

Physics · Unit 3 · Waves · Last-minute notes · Book pages 97–105

Chapter in a nutshell

  • Waves transfer energy and information, not matter
  • Transverse: vibrations at 90° (light, water)
  • Longitudinal: vibrations parallel (sound)
  • Amplitude = rest position to crest
  • v = f × λ for all waves
  • f = 1 ÷ T (T in seconds)
  • Reflection: angle i = angle r, from the normal
  • Refraction: speed change → direction change
  • Doppler, approaching: shorter λ, higher f
  • Doppler, moving away: longer λ, lower f

The parts of a wave

Wavelength λ

From one point on a wave to the same point on the next wave, e.g. crest to crest.

Amplitude A

From the rest position up to a crest.

Common mistakeAmplitude is not crest to trough.

Turn it round

T = 1 ÷ f. Short time for one wave → lots of waves each second → high frequency.

A source has a frequency of 200 Hz. Find the time period.
Write downf = 200 Hz, T = ?
EquationT = 1 ÷ f
SubstituteT = 1 ÷ 200
T = 0.005 s (5 ms)
Finding T from a graph
  1. Find one complete wave (crest to crest).
  2. Read its time: that is T.
  3. Change to seconds, then use f = 1 ÷ T.
Write downT = 5 ms = 0.005 s
Substitutef = 1 ÷ 0.005
f = 200 Hz

A tuning fork
Sound in water
Sound travels through water at 1500 m/s. The wavelength is 1.5 m. Find the frequency and the time period.
Write downv = 1500 m/s, λ = 1.5 m
Equationf = v ÷ λ = 1500 ÷ 1.5
f = 1000 Hz

Step 2: time period

T = 1 ÷ f = 1 ÷ 1000

T = 0.001 s

Exam tipUsing one answer in the next equation is very common.
Using v = f × λ for light and radio
A radio wave has a wavelength of 1500 m. Radio waves travel at 300 000 000 m/s. Find the frequency.
Write downv = 300 000 000 m/s, λ = 1500 m
Equationf = v ÷ λ
Substitutef = 300 000 000 ÷ 1500
f = 200 000 Hz

Same equation

Light and radio waves are electromagnetic waves. v = f × λ works for them too.

CheckHuge speed ÷ wavelength in m → frequency in Hz.

Common mistakes
"The water moves with the wave"No. Waves transfer energy, not matter. The water moves up and down.
"The source changes its frequency"No. Only the observed frequency changes, because the wavefronts are squashed or stretched.
Amplitude crest to troughNo. Measure from the rest position to a crest.

Test yourself

1. Is sound a transverse or a longitudinal wave?Longitudinal: the vibrations are parallel to the direction of travel.
2. What is the amplitude of a wave?The maximum movement of particles from their rest position (rest to crest).
3. A wave has f = 50 Hz and λ = 4 m. Find its speed.v = f × λ = 50 × 4 = 200 m/s
4. Name two waves that are transverse.Any two of: light (any EM wave), water ripples, waves on a rope.
5. A wave has a time period of 0.02 s. What is its frequency?f = 1 ÷ T = 1 ÷ 0.02 = 50 Hz
6. Why does a wave refract when it enters glass?It changes speed (slows down), so it changes direction.
7. An ambulance drives away from you. What happens to the pitch you hear?Lower pitch: the wavefronts are stretched, so λ is longer and f is lower.
8. A wave hits a barrier at 30° to the normal. What is the angle of reflection?30°, because angle of incidence = angle of reflection.
