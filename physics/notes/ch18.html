Density and Pressure

Physics · Unit 5 · Solids, liquids and gases · Last-minute notes · Book pages 173–180

Chapter in a nutshell

  • ρ = m ÷ V, in kg/m³ (or g/cm³)
  • Solids usually densest, gases least dense
  • Mass on a balance; volume by l × w × h or displacement
  • Read at eye level, bottom of meniscus
  • p = F ÷ A, in Pa (1 Pa = 1 N/m²)
  • Small area → high p; large area → low p
  • Still fluid: pressure acts equally in all directions
  • p = h × ρ × g — depth matters, area doesn't
  • Air pressure ≈ 100 000 Pa
  • Barometer: air pressure · manometer: gas pressure
1What is density?
Density: how tightly is the mass packed?
Key idea · density

Density tells you how much mass is packed into a given volume.

density = mass ÷ volume
ρ = m ÷ V
ρ in kg/m³m in kgV in m³
Don't mix upρ (Greek “rho”) = density. Small p = pressure.
A hot-air balloon rises because the hot gas inside has a low density.
Rearranging ρ = m ÷ V

Find density

Find mass

m = ρ × V

Find volume

V = m ÷ ρ

Typical densities

Water: 1000 kg/m³

1 m³ of water has a mass of 1000 kg.

Iron: 7800 kg/m³

Almost 8 times as dense as water.

Mercury: 13 600 kg/m³

A liquid — but denser than iron!

The density of iron
A piece of iron has mass 390 kg and volume 0.05 m³. Find its density.
Write downm = 390 kg, V = 0.05 m³
Equationρ = m ÷ V
Substituteρ = 390 ÷ 0.05
ρ = 7800 kg/m³

Check it

7800 kg/m³ matches the typical value for iron.

UnitsMass in kg and volume in m³ → density in kg/m³.
The density of a liquid
Cylinder + 50 cm³ of liquid = 146 g. The empty cylinder = 100 g. Find the density of the liquid.
Liquid mass146 − 100 = 46 g
Equationρ = m ÷ V
Substituteρ = 46 ÷ 50
0.92 g/cm³ = 920 kg/m³
Watch outSubtract the container — use the mass of the liquid only.
2Practical: measuring density
Density of a regular solid
volume = length × width × height
V = l × w × h
l, w, h in m
  1. Measure the mass on a balance.
  2. Measure l, w and h with a ruler (e.g. a half-metre rule).
  3. Calculate ρ = m ÷ V.
  4. Repeat and take a mean.
Volume by displacement
  1. Fill a displacement (eureka) can up to the spout.
  2. Gently lower the object in.
  3. Collect the water pushed out in a measuring cylinder.
Key factVolume of water displaced = volume of the object.
Reading the measuring cylinder
  1. Stand the cylinder on a level surface.
  2. Put your eye level with the liquid and look straight on.
  3. Read the bottom of the meniscus.
Parallax errorLooking from above or below makes the reading wrong.

3Pressure under solids
What is pressure?
Definition · pressure

Pressure is the force per unit area.

pressure = force ÷ area
p = F ÷ A
p in PaF in NA in m²
Pascal1 Pa = 1 N/m² · 1 kPa = 1000 Pa
Rearranging p = F ÷ A

Find pressure

p = F ÷ A

Find force

F = p × A

Find area

A = F ÷ p

Bigger force → bigger pressure

Bigger area → smaller pressure

Small area or large area?

Small area → high pressure

Drawing pins, sharp knives, scissors and bolt cutters.

Large area → low pressure

Tracks, wide tyres, skis and camel feet — less sinking.

Elephant vs woman
Elephant: weight 40 000 N on feet of total area 0.1 m².
Equationp = F ÷ A
Substitutep = 40 000 ÷ 0.1
400 000 Pa (400 kPa)
Woman: weight 600 N on shoes of area 0.0015 m².
Equationp = F ÷ A
Substitutep = 600 ÷ 0.0015
400 000 Pa (400 kPa)
Same pressure!A small area can give as big a pressure as a huge force.
Walking, boots and cm²
An 850 N person walks. One boot has an area of 210 cm². Find the pressure.
Area210 ÷ 10 000 = 0.021 m²
Equationp = F ÷ A
Substitutep = 850 ÷ 0.021
p ≈ 40 500 Pa
WalkingAll your weight is on one foot at some point — use the area of one boot.

On a plank

Plank: 0.3 m², 70 N.
(850 + 70) ÷ 0.3 ≈ 3070 Pa — much lower!

4Pressure in liquids and gases
Pressure acts in all directions
Key rule

In a liquid or gas at rest, the pressure at a point acts equally in all directions.

Not just downwards — sideways and upwards too. A submerged object feels pressure on all its surfaces.

Atmospheric pressure

Air pressure ≈ 100 000 Pa

Gases push on everything around them. About 100 kPa — it varies a little from day to day.

Why don't we feel it?

The pressure inside our bodies is similar, so the pushes balance.

Deep down

A deep-sea submersible needs a very strong hull — water pressure is huge at depth.

The Magdeburg hemispheres

1654, Magdeburg

Otto von Guericke pumped the air out of two large metal bowls. Two teams of horses could not pull them apart!

With the air removed, pressure acts only from outside. Let air back in and they separate.

The force on the hemispheres

5Pressure and depth
Pressure increases with depth

What you see

Water spurts further from the lower holes.

Why

The deeper you go, the more liquid is pressing down from above — so the pressure is bigger.

The pressure difference equation
pressure difference = height × density × g
p = h × ρ × g
p in Pah in mρ in kg/m³g = 10 N/kg
Any fluidWorks for any liquid or gas if you know its density. Always use h in metres!
Water 10 cm deep. What is the pressure difference?
Write downh = 0.1 m, ρ = 1000 kg/m³, g = 10 N/kg
Equationp = h × ρ × g
Substitutep = 0.1 × 1000 × 10
p = 1000 Pa
Where p = h × ρ × g comes from
  1. volume = A × h
  2. mass = A × h × ρ
  3. weight F = A × h × ρ × g
  4. p = F ÷ A = h × ρ × g
Big ideaThe area cancels — the width of the container doesn't matter, only the depth.
ExamLearn the equation; you don't need the proof.
The mercury barometer

Air pressure on the bowl holds up the mercury column. Height h tells you the air pressure.

h = 0.74 m, ρ = 13 600 kg/m³, g = 10 N/kg. Find the air pressure.
Equationp = h × ρ × g
Substitutep = 0.74 × 13 600 × 10
p = 100 640 Pa
The manometer: measuring gas pressure

The gas pushes one side down. Gas off → the levels become equal.

Level difference = 10 cm of water. Air pressure = 100 000 Pa. Find the gas pressure.
Difference0.1 × 1000 × 10 = 1000 Pa
Gas100 000 + 1000
101 000 Pa
Air pressure and the weather

Blaise Pascal

The unit is named after him. He carried a barometer up a mountain: air pressure decreases with height.

Weather maps

Air flows from high to low pressure → wind. Close isobars = very strong winds.
