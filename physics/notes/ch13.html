Chapter in a nutshell

  • Sound is longitudinal, made by vibrations: compressions and rarefactions
  • Sound can be reflected (echoes, sonar) and refracted
  • Sound needs particles: none in a vacuum
  • Speed of sound in air ≈ 340 m/s (echo method)
  • Echoes: the sound goes there and back, so use × 2 or ÷ 2
  • Microphone + oscilloscope: read T, then f = 1 ÷ T
  • Higher frequency → higher pitch
  • Bigger amplitude → louder
  • Human hearing: 20 Hz to 20 000 Hz
  • v = f × λ, e.g. 340 ÷ 85 = 4 m
1How sound is made
Every sound starts with a vibration
  1. The loudspeaker cone vibrates back and forth.
  2. It pushes air particles together: a compression.
  3. It spreads them apart: a rarefaction.
  4. These travel as a longitudinal wave to your ear.
  5. Your eardrum vibrates. The vibrations become electrical signals for your brain.

Not very accurateThe time is very short, so your reaction time causes a big error.
A better way: use echoes Physics only
  1. Stand at least 50 m from a large wall or building.
  2. Bang two pieces of wood together and listen for the echo.
  3. Clap again each time you hear the echo, in a steady rhythm.
  4. A friend times 20 claps.
  5. Distance = distance to wall × 2 × 20. Then v = distance ÷ time.
Finding the speed of sound
A girl stands 100 m from a building. 20 echoes take 11.7 s. Find the speed of sound.
Distance100 m × 2 × 20 = 4000 m
Equationv = s ÷ t
Substitutev = 4000 ÷ 11.7
v = 342 m/s
Remember the × 2Each echo goes to the wall and back.

Speed of sound in air

About 340 m/s. It changes a little with temperature.

Echo sounding: how deep is the sea?
  1. The ship sends sound waves down to the seabed.
  2. Some are reflected back up to the ship.
  3. Equipment detects the echo and measures the time.
  4. Depth = (speed × time) ÷ 2

SONAR = Sound Navigation And Ranging

Finding the depth of the sea
An echo is heard 4 s after the pulse is sent. Sound travels at 1500 m/s in water. How deep is the sea?
Equationdistance = speed × time
Substitute1500 × 4 = 6000 m (down and back)
Halve it6000 ÷ 2
depth = 3000 m
depth = (speed × time) ÷ 2
d = (v × t) ÷ 2
Common mistakeForgetting to divide by 2. The time is for the round trip.
Sound can be refracted

Warm vs cool air

Sound travels faster in warm air than in cool air. If parts of a wave are in air at different temperatures, the wave changes direction.

Across a lake

Sound going up (A) is bent back down (C). The listener gets this and the direct sound (B), so it is louder and clearer.

Microphone + oscilloscope Physics only
  • The microphone turns sound into an electrical signal.

CarefulThe trace looks transverse, but the sound wave is still longitudinal.

5Pitch, loudness and hearing
Pitch depends on frequency Physics only

Higher frequency (more waves on the screen) → higher pitch.

Small objects (violin strings) vibrate fast → high pitch. Large objects (cello strings) vibrate slowly → low pitch.

Loudness depends on amplitude Physics only

Larger amplitude (taller wave) → louder sound.

Hit a drum hard: more energy, bigger vibrations → loud. Hit it gently: small amplitude → quiet.

Don't mix them up! Physics only

Pitch ↔ frequency

How close together the waves are.

Loudness ↔ amplitude

How tall the waves are.

Loud, high noteTall waves packed close together.
We hear from 20 Hz to 20 000 Hz Physics only

This is the audible range (hearing range). 20 000 Hz = 20 kHz.

It differs a little between people and gets narrower with age.

Show it with a signal generator and a loudspeaker.

Extension: above 20 000 Hz is ultrasound, below 20 Hz is infrasound.
