1Momentum and force
Newton’s cradle

What happens

One ball hits the row. One ball swings out at the far end, as high as the first was released.

Pull back two?

Two balls swing out.

WhyThe moving ball has momentum, and momentum is conserved in the collision.
Hard to stop = lots of momentum
Key term · momentum

A measure of how difficult it is to stop something that is moving.

  • Only moving masses have momentum.
  • More mass → more momentum.
  • Faster → more momentum.
Everyday wordsA car rolling downhill is “gaining momentum” as it speeds up.

Momentum has a direction

A vector

Momentum has size and direction – the direction of the velocity. The other way is negative.

Don’t confuse!Momentum (kg m/s) is not a moment. A moment is the turning effect of a force, in newton metres (N m) – Chapter 5.
A force changes momentum

An unbalanced force changes momentum in the direction of the force.

Rate of change of momentum ∝ force. Double the force → momentum changes twice as quickly.

2Conservation of momentum
Momentum is conserved
Law of conservation of momentum

Total momentum before = total momentum after – for collisions and explosions, if no external forces (e.g. friction) act.

Equal and opposite forces

Each ball pushes the other with an equal and opposite force (Newton’s 3rd law) for the same time.

So F × t is equal but opposite: B gains exactly the momentum A loses. The total is unchanged.

Snooker balls and friction

Friction is an external force. It slows the balls and they stop.

The “lost” momentum is gained by the table. Its mass is huge, so we can’t notice.

RememberMomentum is still conserved overall.
How to solve a collision
  1. Choose a positive direction. Velocities the other way are negative.
  2. Write: momentum before = momentum after.
  3. Each object: p = m × v. Not moving → zero momentum.
  4. Stuck together? Use the combined mass after.
  5. Solve, and give the direction of the answer.
AlwaysState the direction in your answer.

3Explosions, rockets and car safety
Things fly apart

An explosion releases energy and makes things fly apart. Momentum is still conserved.

Start at rest → total momentum = 0. Pieces get equal momentum in opposite directions.

But…There is a huge increase in kinetic (movement) energy.
How rockets work

Rockets do not push on the ground. They work in space, where there is nothing to push against.

The motor is a controlled explosion. Fast gas goes out the back; the rocket gains equal momentum forwards.

Make the stopping time longer
force = change in momentum ÷ time
F = (mv − mu) ÷ t

In a crash the change in momentum is fixed (moving → stopped).

Key ideaLonger time → smaller force on the people inside.
Crumple zones in numbers

A car at 20 m/s stops. Passenger mass 50 kg. Change in momentum = (50 × 20) − (50 × 0) = 1000 kg m/s.

Stops in 0.02 s (no crumple zone)
EquationF = (mv − mu) ÷ t
SubstituteF = 1000 ÷ 0.02
F = 50 000 N
Crumple zones stretch the time to 1 s
Samechange in momentum = 1000 kg m/s
SubstituteF = 1000 ÷ 1
F = 1000 N
Result50 times smaller force – far more survivable!
Crumple zones and seat belts

Crumple zones

Front and back collapse, so the car takes longer to stop → smaller deceleration and force. The passenger cell stays intact.

Seat belts

Stretch slightly so you slow down with the car. Without one you hit something hard → very short time → huge force.

Air bags and escape lanes

Air bags

Inflate fast with gas, then deflate as you hit them. Longer stopping time, and the force is spread out.

Escape lanes

Deep, soft sand slows runaway lorries over a longer time → smaller force.

One ideaEvery feature makes stopping take longer.
“Explain how ___ reduces injury”
  1. The feature increases the time taken for the person to stop (for the momentum to change).
  2. The change in momentum is the same (same mass, same starting speed, ends at rest).
  3. F = change in momentum ÷ time, so a longer time means a smaller force → less serious injury.

4Newton’s laws of motion
Newton’s 1st and 2nd laws

1st law

An object stays at rest or keeps moving in a straight line at steady speed unless a resultant force acts on it.

2nd law

Bigger resultant force → bigger acceleration. Bigger mass needs a bigger force. F = m × a comes from this.

Learn it by name!
Newton’s third law

For every action there is an equal and opposite reaction.

When you push something, it pushes you back just as hard in the opposite direction.

X pushes Y, Y pushes X back. Both move apart.

The 3rd law in action

Sitting down

Your weight pushes down on the seat. The seat pushes up on you with an equal force.

Car on the road

The car pushes down on the ground through the wheels. The ground pushes up equally, so it doesn’t sink.

Collisions

A pushes on B, and B pushes equally back on A. This is why momentum is conserved.

Rockets

The rocket pushes gases backwards. The gases push the rocket forwards.

What is a law? An idea tested many times by careful, independent experiments. Newton’s laws fail near the speed of light (Einstein), but work very well for everyday motion.

Common mistakes

Momentum ≠ moment

Momentum = m × v (kg m/s). A moment is a turning effect (N m).

No direction

Pick a + direction. State the direction of your answer.

Forgot to add masses

Stuck together? Use 5000 + 10 000 = 15 000 kg.

Wrong units

60 g = 0.06 kg. 180 km/h ÷ 3.6 = 50 m/s.

“Safety features reduce momentum”

No! Same change in momentum, longer time → smaller force.

3rd-law pair on one object

A 3rd-law pair always acts on two different objects.
