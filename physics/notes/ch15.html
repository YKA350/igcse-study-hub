Thermal Energy

Physics · Unit 4 · Energy resources and energy transfers · Last-minute notes · Book pages 139–149

Chapter in a nutshell

  • Heat flows from hotter to colder
  • Conduction: vibrating particles; metals best
  • Convection: warm, less dense fluid rises
  • Radiation: IR waves, works in a vacuum
  • Matt black: best absorber and emitter
  • Shiny or white: best reflector, poor emitter
  • Hotter objects radiate more energy per second
  • Trapped air is an excellent insulator
  • Cavity walls, double glazing, loft insulation
  • Clothes, blankets and huddling cut heat loss
1What is thermal energy?
What is thermal energy?
Key idea · thermal energy

Thermal (heat) energy is stored in “hot” matter. It comes from the kinetic energy of the particles.

RememberAnything above absolute zero (the lowest possible temperature) has some thermal energy.
Golden ruleHeat always flows from hotter to colder.
Three ways heat moves

Conduction

Vibrating particles pass energy on. Mainly in solids.

Convection

Warm fluid rises, cool fluid sinks. Liquids and gases only.

Radiation

Infrared waves. Needs no particles – crosses a vacuum.

In simple wordsSolids → conduction. Liquids and gases → convection. Empty space → only radiation.
2Conduction
Conduction: passing on the jiggle
Key term · conduction

Conduction is the transfer of thermal energy through a substance by the vibration of its particles – the substance itself does not move.

RememberParticles stay in place. Only the energy travels.

Like a queue

People pass a push along the line. Nobody moves along – the push does.

How conduction works
  1. Particles at the hot end have more kinetic energy – they vibrate more.
  2. They pass energy to their neighbours, which vibrate more.
  3. Energy is passed on, particle to particle.
  4. Metals do this much faster: free electrons move through, carrying energy.
ExtensionGood heat conductors are usually good electrical conductors too – both use free electrons (copper has about 10²⁹ per m³).
Conductors and insulators

Good thermal conductors

Metals: copper, steel, aluminium. A metal kebab skewer gets hot all along.

Good insulators

Wood, plastic, air, trapped water, wool, glass fibre.

Comparing conduction in metals
  1. Heat one end of each rod.
  2. Jelly melts – pins drop off in order.
  3. Copper’s pins fall first → copper is the better conductor.
Fair testSame rod diameter, same pin spacing, same heat source.
SafetyRods get hot enough to burn – and stay hot.
Why does metal feel colder?

Question

A metal bench and a wooden bench are at the same temperature. Why does the metal feel colder?

Answer

Metal is a good conductor – it carries heat away from your hand quickly. Wood is an insulator, so it doesn’t.

Same ideaA metal spoon in tea conducts heat out, so the tea cools faster.
3Convection
Why does warm fluid rise?
Key term · convection

Convection is the transfer of thermal energy through fluids (liquids and gases) by the upward movement of warmer, less dense regions of fluid.

  1. Heated particles gain kinetic energy and move further apart.
  2. The fluid expands, so its density decreases.
  3. Less dense fluid floats up; cooler, denser fluid sinks.
Seeing a convection current
  1. Drop a potassium manganate(VII) crystal at the side of a beaker of water. Heat gently under it.
  2. Warm water expands, becomes less dense and rises – the purple shows the path.
  3. At the top it cools, becomes denser and sinks.
SafetyAvoid skin contact with the crystals and solution.
Convection in air

A box with two chimneys and a candle under one of them.

  • Warm air rises up the chimney above the candle.
  • Cool air is drawn down the other chimney.
  • The smoke shows the path of the current.
Convection at home
  • Heaters: warm air rises from the top – the whole room warms.
  • Ovens: element at the bottom, so top shelves are hotter. Fan ovens even it out.
  • Kettles: element at the bottom so all the water circulates.
  • Freezers: cooling unit at the top so cold air sinks.
Sea breezes
Day: land heats faster. Warm air over land rises; cool air flows in from the sea.
Night: land cools faster. The breeze blows from the land out to sea.
AlsoGliders and birds ride rising warm air (“thermals”). Convection in thunderclouds builds up charge for lightning.
4Thermal radiation
Heat that travels as infrared
Key term · thermal radiation

Thermal radiation is the transfer of energy by infrared (IR) waves.

  • Part of the EM spectrum.
  • Travels in straight lines.
  • Can be reflected and absorbed, like light.
CarefulThis is not nuclear radiation!
Using infrared

Thermal imaging cameras

The army and emergency services see people by their body heat in the dark.

Medicine

Some illnesses can be found from skin temperature.

Instant heat

You feel a bathroom heater at once – conduction and convection are far slower.

Heaters use a shiny curved (parabolic) reflector to direct IR forwards.

Which surface radiates best?
ResultThe thermometer facing the black side rises faster → black radiates better.
Fair testIdentical thermometers, same height and distance from the bulb.
SafetyThe bulb can burn skin.
Surfaces in real life

Shiny kettle

Radiates less, so it stays hot longer.

Solar panels

Matt black, so they absorb the most IR.

Heat sinks

Matt black metal with fins – radiate and convect heat away from transistors.

Fire-fighter suits

White or shiny, so they reflect IR.

In simple wordsKeep heat in (or out)? Go shiny. Soak up or give out heat? Go matt black.
Temperature matters too

Hotter objects radiate much more energy per second. Cool objects give out mostly infrared.

A tungsten filament at about 3000 °C glows white hot.

5Energy-efficient houses
Why insulate a house?

Heating costs

Heating is the main use of energy at home. Most comes from burning fuels.

Carbon dioxide

Burning fuels makes carbon dioxide – a greenhouse gas that adds to global warming.

Insulation

Reduces the rate of energy transfer between inside and outside.

Hot countriesHere the aim is the opposite – keeping heat out. The same methods work.
Where does a house lose heat?

Main route

Conduction through walls, windows, doors and roof.

Also

Convection (draughts and air currents) and radiation.

Cavity walls and cavity insulation

Cavity wall

Outer brick + inner thermal brick with an air gap. Air is a poor conductor.

Cavity insulation

Foam or glass fibre traps air so it can’t circulate. Foil reflects IR.

Double glazing and loft insulation

Two sheets of glass trap a thin layer of air or low-pressure gas. Too thick a gap → convection currents form.

A thick layer of fibre traps air. Reflective foil reflects IR back into the house.

The greenhouse effect
  1. Short-wavelength IR from the Sun passes through glass and warms the ground.
  2. The ground re-radiates longer-wavelength IR that can’t pass back out – heat is trapped.
  3. Carbon dioxide in the atmosphere acts like the glass.
6Insulating people and animals
How clothes keep you warm
  • Clothes trap air. Trapped air can’t circulate and is a very poor conductor.
  • Wool traps air in its fibres.
  • A wetsuit traps a thin layer of water, which the body warms.
  • Much heat is lost from the head – a hat is like loft insulation!
Wind chill
  • Wind causes forced convection – air moves past the skin and carries heat away fast.
  • Sweat evaporates quickly → rapid cooling.
  • Wear a wind-proof outer layer.
DangerLosing heat too fast → hypothermia (body temperature falls). It can kill.
Emergency blankets and animals

Shiny blankets (mountain rescue, marathons): the inside reflects IR back; the shiny outside is a poor radiator.

Penguins huddle and animals curl up → less exposed surface. Birds fluff up feathers → thicker trapped air.

Conduction, convection or radiation?
SituationExplanation
A lid keeps tea hotStops convection currents (and evaporation) carrying heat away.
Polished copper kettlesCopper conducts heat quickly to the water; polished so it radiates less.
Paper on a brass/wood cylinder scorches on the wood onlyBrass conducts heat away from the paper; wood is an insulator.
Computer vents top and bottom, no fanWarm air rises out of the top; cool air drawn in at the bottom.
Fire at the bottom of a Roman mine shaftHot air rises up the shaft, drawing fresh air through the mine.
Common mistakes

✗ “In conduction the particles travel along the bar.”

✓ They vibrate in place and pass energy on.

✗ “Convection happens in solids.”

✓ Only in fluids – liquids and gases.

✗ “Heat rises.”

✓ Warmer, less dense fluid rises, carrying the heat.

✗ “Thermal radiation is the nuclear kind.”

✓ It is infrared waves.

✗ “Shiny surfaces are good radiators.”

✓ A good reflector is a poor radiator. Matt black emits best.

✗ “A big air gap insulates best.”

✓ Air must be trapped. If it can move, convection carries heat across.
