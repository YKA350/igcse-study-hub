Fission and Fusion

Physics · Unit 7 · Radioactivity and particles · Last-minute notes · Book pages 250–254

Chapter in a nutshell

  • Fission, fusion and decay release energy because mass is lost
  • Slow neutron + U-235 → U-236 → two radioactive daughters + 2–3 neutrons + γ
  • Energy is mostly kinetic energy of the daughter nuclei
  • Chain reaction: released neutrons split more nuclei; critical mass needed
  • Moderator slows neutrons; control rods absorb them
  • Steel and about 5 m of concrete shield the workers
  • Nuclear power: no CO₂, but long-lived radioactive waste
  • Waste is sealed and buried deep in stable, dry rock
  • Fusion joins light nuclei and powers the stars
  • Fusion needs huge temperature and pressure to beat repulsion
1Nuclear fission
Three nuclear energy sources

Fission

A big nucleus splits into smaller ones.

Fusion

Small nuclei join to make a bigger one.

Radioactive decay

Keeps the Earth's core hot (uranium, thorium, potassium).

Where does the energy come from?In fission and fusion the products have less mass than the starting nuclei. The missing mass is turned into energy.
How uranium-235 splits
In wordsA slow neutron is absorbed, so U-235 becomes unstable U-236. It splits into two lighter, radioactive daughter nuclei, 2 or 3 neutrons and γ radiation.

Key facts about fission
Key term · fissile

U-235 is fissile: its nuclei split easily when hit by a neutron.

Mass goes missing

The products have less mass than the nucleus and neutron before. That mass becomes energy.

Where the energy goes

Most is kinetic energy of the two daughter nuclei, which fly apart fast. Some is γ radiation.

What comes out

Two radioactive daughter nuclei and a small number of neutrons (2 or 3).

ExtensionOnly slow neutrons cause fission. Fast neutrons do not.
One neutron starts it all
Key term · chain reaction

Neutrons from one fission strike other U-235 nuclei and cause more fissions.

Steady or runaway?

Steady · reactor

Only one neutron from each fission causes another fission. The rate stays the same. Used in a nuclear power station.

Runaway · explosion

More than one neutron from each fission causes another. It gets faster and faster, like an avalanche, and ends in an explosion.

How much uranium is enough?
Key term · critical mass

The minimum mass of U-235 needed for a chain reaction.

Atomic bomb

Two pieces below critical mass are forced together under high pressure. Above it, the chain reaction is uncontrolled.

2The nuclear reactor
Inside a nuclear power station
Fuel rods and enriched uranium
Key term · enriched uranium

Uranium-238 with a higher proportion of U-235 than natural uranium.

Why?

The fuel rods hold enriched uranium. U-235 is the fissile part that splits.

Natural uranium

Mostly U-238, with only a little U-235.

The moderator slows neutrons
Moderator (graphite)

Absorbs some kinetic energy of the neutrons, so they slow down.

Why?Slow neutrons are more easily absorbed by U-235 and cause fission.
Control rods are the brake
Control rods · boron or cadmium

They absorb neutrons, removing them from the chain reaction.

Lower in → fewer fissions (fully in ≈ stopped).
Raise out → more fissions, more heat.

Keeping the radiation in

Two layers

A steel vessel and about 5 m of concrete around the core.

Stops it all

It stops α, β, γ and even neutrons escaping.

Why?

To protect the workers in the power station.

3Nuclear power: pros, cons and waste
Good points and bad points

Advantages

  • No CO₂ or acidic gases, so no global warming or acid rain
  • Only small amounts of fuel needed
  • Fuel lasts hundreds of years (some fossil fuels may run out in about 50 years)
  • Reliable, steady output

Disadvantages

  • Makes radioactive waste, some with very long half-lives
  • Risk of accidents releasing radioactive material (e.g. Chernobyl, Fukushima)
  • Some reactors make plutonium: very radioactive, fissile, usable in weapons
  • Expensive to build and decommission
Short or long half-life?

Short half-life

Decays quickly and soon becomes safe.

Extremely long half-life

Stays dangerous for thousands of years. A serious storage problem.

What happens to the waste?
Choosing the siteThe rock must be water-resistant and stable: not in an earthquake or volcanic zone.
4Nuclear fusion
Fusion: small nuclei join
Key term · fusion

Light nuclei join to form a larger nucleus.

EnergyThe products have less mass than the original nuclei. The lost mass is released as energy.
The fusion equation and the stars
hydrogen-2 + hydrogen-3 → helium-4 + neutron + energy
21H + 31H → 42He + 10n + energy

Check the numbers

Top: 2 + 3 = 5 and 4 + 1 = 5 ✓

Bottom: 1 + 1 = 2 and 2 + 0 = 2 ✓

Fusion powers the stars

It is the energy source of our Sun and all stars. Hydrogen fuses to make helium.

Why fusion is so hard
Key idea · electrostatic repulsion

Nuclei are positively charged, so they repel each other.

So…To get close enough to fuse, nuclei must collide at very high speed.
What fusion needs

Very high temperature

About 100 million °C, so nuclei move fast.

Very high pressure

Squeezes nuclei together for more collisions.

Strong magnetic field

A “magnetic bottle” holds the hot material.

Fusion reactors: being developed

Fuel is easier to get

Hydrogen isotopes are more readily available than uranium.

Hardly any waste

No long-lived radioactive waste from the fusion products.

Huge energy

A huge energy release for each kilogram of fuel.

The catchFusion is extremely hard to achieve and control on Earth. At low temperature or pressure it does not happen.
Why is fusion harder than fission?

Fission

A slow neutron is uncharged, so it is not repelled. It is simply absorbed by U-235.

Fusion

Two positive nuclei must beat their repulsion: about 100 million °C, very high pressure and a magnetic bottle.

Don't fall into these traps

✗ The moderator absorbs neutrons.

It only slows them. Control rods absorb them.

✗ Fusion is splitting a nucleus.

Fusion = joining. Fission = splitting.

✗ The fission products are safe.

The two daughter nuclei are radioactive.

✗ Moderator and control rods do the same job.

Moderator slows. Control rods absorb.

Memory trickfiSSion = Split. fUSion = Unite (join together).
