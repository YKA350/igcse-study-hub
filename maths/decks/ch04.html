4.3

Expanding brackets

Expand

Multiply every term inside

Expand and simplify 3(x + 4) − 2(x − 1)
First bracket3x + 12
Second bracket−2x + 2
Collect3x − 2x + 12 + 2
x + 14
  1. Multiply the outside by each term inside.
  2. Watch the signs: a minus outside flips the signs inside.
  3. Collect like terms.
Exam tip−2 × −1 = +2.

4.4

Multiplying brackets together

Double brackets

Two brackets: FOIL

Expand (x + 3)(x + 5)
Firstx × x = x²
Outer + Inner5x + 3x = 8x
Last3 × 5 = 15
x² + 8x + 15
  1. First × First.
  2. Outer × Outer.
  3. Inner × Inner.
  4. Last × Last. Then collect like terms.
Exam tipAlways four terms before collecting.

Factorising - common factors

Factorise

Factorising is expanding backwards

Factorise 6x² + 9x
Number3 goes into 6 and 9
Letterx is in both
Outside3x(...)
3x(2x + 3)
  1. Find the biggest number that divides every term.
  2. Find the highest power of each letter in every term.
  3. Write them outside a bracket and divide each term.
Exam tipExpand your answer to check.

Factorising - difference of two squares

Idea

Square minus square

Rule

a² − b² = (a + b)(a − b).

Spot it

Two terms, both perfect squares, with a minus.

Number trick

99² − 1 = 98 × 100.

4.10

Changing the subject of a formula

Rearrange

Make the letter the subject

Make x the subject of y = 3x − 5
Add 5y + 5 = 3x
Divide by 3x = (y + 5) ÷ 3
x = (y + 5)/3
  1. Do the same to both sides.
  2. Undo + and − first.
  3. Then undo × and ÷, powers and roots.
  4. The wanted letter ends up alone.
Exam tipWhatever you do to one side, do to the other.
