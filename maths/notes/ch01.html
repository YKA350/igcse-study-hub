Fractions, decimals and rounding

Maths · Unit 1 · Number and proportion · Last-minute notes · Book pages 1–23

Chapter in a nutshell

  • Equivalent fractions: multiply or divide top and bottom equally
  • × fractions: cancel then multiply; ÷: keep, change, flip
  • Recurring decimals: multiply by 10 or 100 and subtract
  • Round only at the end; 3 s.f. by default
  • Bounds: half the rounding unit either side
1Equivalent fractions
Same value, different look

What it means

12 and 24 are equal. Multiply top and bottom by the same number.

Why it helps

To add or compare, use the same bottom number.

6Upper and lower bounds
How big or small could it really be?

Meaning

A length given as 8 cm to the nearest cm could be anything from 7.5 to just under 8.5.

Rule

Lower bound = value − half the rounding unit. Upper bound = value + half the unit.

Notation

7.5 ≤ length < 8.5

A mass is 60 kg to the nearest 10 kg
Rounding unit10 kg, half is 5
Lower60 − 5 = 55
Upper60 + 5 = 65
55 ≤ m < 65
Bounds in a calculation
a = 6 and b = 4, both to the nearest whole number. Find the upper bound of a − b

Bounds of b3.5 to 4.5

  2. Biggest answer: use upper bounds for + and ×; for − and ÷ use upper then lower.

  4. Write both answers clearly.
