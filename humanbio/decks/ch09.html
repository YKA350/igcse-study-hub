The central nervous system

The central nervous system

  1. The nervous system sends electrical impulses along nerves, the endocrine system uses hormones in the blood.
  2. The CNS is the brain and spinal cord, protected by the skull and vertebrae. Spinal nerves link the spinal cord, cranial nerves link the brain. Mixed nerves contain sensory and motor neurones.
  3. Impulses are movements of ions, not an electric current, and travel at about 10 to 100 m per s.

The central nervous system

Synapses

Synapse

A gap between two neurones. Impulses do not cross it electrically.

  1. Impulse arrives, the axon end secretes a neurotransmitter, it diffuses across the gap and binds to the next membrane starting a new impulse. An enzyme then breaks it down.
  2. Synapses let neurones add up inputs from many cells (integration), and allow one-way transmission.
  3. Drugs can mimic or block neurotransmitters.

Reflex action

A rapid, automatic, involuntary response to a stimulus, often protective.

reflex arc
stimulus → receptor → sensory neurone → relay neurone (spinal cord) → motor neurone → effector → response
Spec 5.4, 5.1 Know the structure and functions of the spinal cord and the structure of a reflex arc
Reflex action

Reflex action (2/2)

  1. For a hot object: pain receptors send impulses through the dorsal root (cell bodies in the dorsal root ganglion), a relay neurone in the spinal cord, then the motor neurone leaves by the ventral root to arm muscles, which contract.
  2. Spinal cord has grey matter (cell bodies) inside and white matter (myelinated axons) outside. In the brain it is reversed.
  3. The brain is told through other synapses, which is how pain is felt.
  4. Knee jerk: tapping the tendon stretches stretch receptors, impulses pass through the spinal cord and the thigh muscle contracts, helping avoid falling.
  5. Voluntary action starts in the brain and is under conscious control.

Mental illness

Mental illness (2/2)

Schizophrenia

About 1 in 100 people. Symptoms: loss of interest, confusion, feeling disconnected, avoiding people, hallucinations, delusions, paranoia. Causes: high dopamine, stress, heredity, drug abuse (cannabis, cocaine), others. Treatment: antipsychotic drugs (side effects), talking treatments, cognitive behavioural therapy, family intervention therapy.

Depression

Intense sadness for days or weeks, worthlessness, guilt, no pleasure, may become suicidal. Causes: other mental health problems, poor health, bad childhood experiences, life events, drug or alcohol abuse, genetics. Treatment: CBT, antidepressants, or both.

Drugs

Drugs (2/4)

Paracetamol

A legal painkiller for aches and fever. It may inhibit an enzyme making prostaglandins that cause pain and fever. Safe at the recommended dose but an overdose causes permanent liver damage and death.

Alcohol

Legal depressant that slows the nervous system and raises reaction time, so driving is dangerous. Large amounts affect balance, vision and speech, and may cause coma and death. The liver detoxifies it, but heavy drinking causes cirrhosis, which is usually fatal. Also damages brain and stomach lining and can be addictive.

Drugs (3/4)

Cannabis

From Cannabis sativa, smoked, with the psychoactive THC. Relaxation and heightened senses, but confusion, hallucinations, anxiety, depression and lung damage.

Drugs

Drugs (4/4)

Heroin

Modified morphine, strong painkiller (diamorphine) in medicine. A narcotic depressant giving a high. Quick tolerance and addiction, withdrawal (sweats, chills, aches, vomiting, anxiety), crime, infections, overdose, and needle sharing risks HIV and hepatitis.

Cocaine

From the coca plant. Local anaesthetic use. A stimulant acting on brain synapses giving energy, then paranoia and violence. Raises heart rate and blood pressure, seizures, addiction and bowel damage.

The endocrine system (1/3)

  1. Endocrine glands are ductless and secrete hormones into blood, which acts on target organs with the right receptors (in the membrane, cytoplasm or nucleus). Exocrine glands use ducts, for example salivary glands.
  2. The pancreas is both: enzymes through a duct and hormones into blood.

The endocrine system

Thyroid and negative feedback (1/2)

  1. Thyroxine contains iodine and raises basal metabolic rate (BMR), increasing aerobic respiration and protein synthesis, needed for growth. Lack of iodine causes goitre (enlarged thyroid). The thyroid stores about 100 days of thyroxine.
  2. Falling BMR is detected by the hypothalamus, which releases TRH, then the pituitary releases TSH, which makes the thyroid release thyroxine. Thyroxine returns BMR to normal and switches off TRH and TSH.
Negative feedback

A change is detected and triggers a response that returns conditions to the set point, such as body temperature 37 °C.

The endocrine system

Thyroid and negative feedback (2/2)

Growth

Growth hormone from the pituitary stimulates growth, mitosis and cell regeneration. Thyroxine raises growth hormone. At puberty testosterone and oestrogen raise it, giving the growth spurt.

Adrenaline

From the adrenal glands in fear, excitement or anger. Breathing deeper and faster, heart beats faster, blood diverted from the gut to muscles, liver turns glycogen to glucose, pupils dilate, mental awareness increases. Also released before races, interviews and exams.

The endocrine system

Blood glucose (1/2)

  1. Normal level is a little under 1 g per dm3.
  2. Insulin from beta cells of the Islets of Langerhans makes liver cells take up glucose and make glycogen. Glucagon from alpha cells makes the liver break glycogen into glucose. Opposite hormones give finer control by negative feedback.
  3. After a meal glucose is carried by the hepatic portal vein to the liver, so blood in the hepatic vein has less.

The endocrine system

Blood glucose (2/2)

Diabetes

Type 1: pancreas cannot make enough insulin, can occur at any age. Glucose appears in urine, constant thirst (hypothalamus), weight loss, coma if untreated. Treat with insulin injections (a protein, so not tablets) plus limited carbohydrate. Type 2: insulin resistance, common with overweight and sugary diets, in middle age, prevented by diet and exercise. Insulin since 1921, first from pigs and cows, and since the 1970s from GM bacteria.

Looking ahead: nerve impulses

Looking ahead: nerve impulses

  1. Resting potential is about -70 mV inside the axon. When stimulated, sodium ions rush in and the cell depolarises (action potential, a few milliseconds). Potassium ions leave to repolarise. The wave spreads along the axon as the impulse. Pumps restore ions using ATP.
