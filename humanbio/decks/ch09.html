The central nervous system

The central nervous system

The nervous system sends electrical impulses along nerves, the endocrine system uses hormones in the blood.

The CNS is the brain and spinal cord, protected by the skull and vertebrae. Spinal nerves link the spinal cord, cranial nerves link the brain. Mixed nerves contain sensory and motor neurones.

Impulses are movements of ions, not an electric current, and travel at about 10 to 100 m per s.

The central nervous system

Neurones (1/2)

Sensory

Job: Receptor to CNS; Structure: Cell body on a side branch just outside the CNS. Long dendron from the receptor, short axon to the CNS

Relay

Job: Link sensory and motor neurones in the CNS; Structure: Short, inside the spinal cord

The central nervous system

Neurones (2/2)

Motor

Job: CNS to effector (muscle or gland); Structure: Cell body in the CNS with dendrons and dendrites, one long axon with nerve endings

Myelin sheath is fatty and wrapped around the axon, insulating it and speeding up impulses.

Neuromuscular junction is the synapse between a motor neurone and a muscle fibre.

The central nervous system

Synapses (1/2)

Synapse

A gap between two neurones. Impulses do not cross it electrically.

Impulse arrives, the axon end secretes a neurotransmitter, it diffuses across the gap and binds to the next membrane starting a new impulse. An enzyme then breaks it down.

The central nervous system

Synapses (2/2)

Synapses let neurones add up inputs from many cells (integration), and allow one-way transmission.

Drugs can mimic or block neurotransmitters.

Reflex action

Reflex action (1/3)

Reflex action

A rapid, automatic, involuntary response to a stimulus, often protective.

For a hot object

pain receptors send impulses through the dorsal root (cell bodies in the dorsal root ganglion), a relay neurone in the spinal cord, then the motor neurone leaves by the ventral root to arm muscles, which contract.

Reflex action

Reflex action (2/3)

Spinal cord has grey matter (cell bodies) inside and white matter (myelinated axons) outside. In the brain it is reversed.

The brain is told through other synapses, which is how pain is felt.

Knee jerk

tapping the tendon stretches stretch receptors, impulses pass through the spinal cord and the thigh muscle contracts, helping avoid falling.

Reflex action

Reflex action (3/3)

Voluntary action starts in the brain and is under conscious control.

Mental illness

Mental illness (1/2)

Schizophrenia About 1 in 100 people. Symptoms: loss of interest, confusion, feeling disconnected, avoiding people, hallucinations, delusions, paranoia.

Causes

high dopamine, stress, heredity, drug abuse (cannabis, cocaine), others.

Treatment

antipsychotic drugs (side effects), talking treatments, cognitive behavioural therapy, family intervention therapy.

Mental illness

Mental illness (2/2)

Depression Intense sadness for days or weeks, worthlessness, guilt, no pleasure, may become suicidal.

Causes

other mental health problems, poor health, bad childhood experiences, life events, drug or alcohol abuse, genetics. Treatment: CBT, antidepressants, or both.

Changes in thoughts, emotions or behaviour that cause distress. About 19% of US adults have some form.

Drugs

Drugs

Drugs (1/3)

Drug

A chemical that affects the normal chemical reactions in the body.

Paracetamol A legal painkiller for aches and fever. It may inhibit an enzyme making prostaglandins that cause pain and fever. Safe at the recommended dose but an overdose causes permanent liver damage and death.

Drugs

Drugs (2/3)

Alcohol Legal depressant that slows the nervous system and raises reaction time, so driving is dangerous. Large amounts affect balance, vision and speech, and may cause coma and death.

The liver detoxifies it, but heavy drinking causes cirrhosis, which is usually fatal. Also damages brain and stomach lining and can be addictive.

Cannabis From Cannabis sativa, smoked, with the psychoactive THC. Relaxation and heightened senses, but confusion, hallucinations, anxiety, depression and lung damage.

Drugs

Drugs (3/3)

Heroin Modified morphine, strong painkiller (diamorphine) in medicine. A narcotic depressant giving a high.

Quick tolerance and addiction, withdrawal (sweats, chills, aches, vomiting, anxiety), crime, infections, overdose, and needle sharing risks HIV and hepatitis.

Cocaine From the coca plant. Local anaesthetic use. A stimulant acting on brain synapses giving energy, then paranoia and violence. Raises heart rate and blood pressure, seizures, addiction and bowel damage.

The endocrine system

The endocrine system (5/5)

The pancreas is both

enzymes through a duct and hormones into blood.

ADH is made in hypothalamus neurones and stored in the pituitary. Receptors in the hypothalamus monitor blood water potential. Target organ is the kidney.

The endocrine system

Thyroid and negative feedback (1/3)

Negative feedback

A change is detected and triggers a response that returns conditions to the set point, such as body temperature 37 °C.

Growth Growth hormone from the pituitary stimulates growth, mitosis and cell regeneration. Thyroxine raises growth hormone. At puberty testosterone and oestrogen raise it, giving the growth spurt.

Adrenaline From the adrenal glands in fear, excitement or anger.

The endocrine system

Thyroid and negative feedback (2/3)

Breathing deeper and faster, heart beats faster, blood diverted from the gut to muscles, liver turns glycogen to glucose, pupils dilate, mental awareness increases.

Also released before races, interviews and exams.

Thyroxine contains iodine and raises basal metabolic rate (BMR), increasing aerobic respiration and protein synthesis, needed for growth. Lack of iodine causes goitre (enlarged thyroid).

The endocrine system

Thyroid and negative feedback (3/3)

The thyroid stores about 100 days of thyroxine.

Falling BMR is detected by the hypothalamus, which releases TRH, then the pituitary releases TSH, which makes the thyroid release thyroxine. Thyroxine returns BMR to normal and switches off TRH and TSH.

The endocrine system

Blood glucose (1/2)

Diabetes Type 1

pancreas cannot make enough insulin, can occur at any age. Glucose appears in urine, constant thirst (hypothalamus), weight loss, coma if untreated.

Treat with insulin injections (a protein, so not tablets) plus limited carbohydrate. Type 2: insulin resistance, common with overweight and sugary diets, in middle age, prevented by diet and exercise.

Insulin since 1921, first from pigs and cows, and since the 1970s from GM bacteria.

The endocrine system

Blood glucose (2/2)

Normal level is a little under 1 g per dm3.

Insulin from beta cells of the Islets of Langerhans makes liver cells take up glucose and make glycogen. Glucagon from alpha cells makes the liver break glycogen into glucose.

Opposite hormones give finer control by negative feedback.

After a meal glucose is carried by the hepatic portal vein to the liver, so blood in the hepatic vein has less.

Looking ahead: nerve impulses

Looking ahead: nerve impulses

Resting potential is about -70 mV inside the axon. When stimulated, sodium ions rush in and the cell depolarises (action potential, a few milliseconds). Potassium ions leave to repolarise.

The wave spreads along the axon as the impulse. Pumps restore ions using ATP.
