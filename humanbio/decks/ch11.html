Sexual reproduction

Reproduction

Sexual reproduction

Sexual reproduction

  1. Humans make gametes: a mobile sperm and a stationary egg cell (ovum). Fertilisation is fusion of sperm and egg to make a zygote, which divides by mitosis into all cells.
  2. Stages: gametes made, male gamete transferred, fertilisation, zygote develops. Offspring show genetic variation from both gamete production and fertilisation.

Diploid

Two sets of chromosomes in homologous pairs, as in body cells (46 in humans).

Haploid

One set of chromosomes, as in gametes (23 in humans).

Meiosis

Meiosis (2/4)

  1. Meiosis in testes and ovaries makes four genetically different haploid cells from one diploid cell.
  2. First each chromosome copies itself (DNA replication) into two chromatids.
  3. Meiosis I: one chromosome from each homologous pair goes into each of two daughter cells. Meiosis II: chromatids separate, one into each of four cells.
  4. Homologous chromosomes carry the same genes in the same order, but may have different alleles.

Variation from meiosis

Pairs are shared out independently of each other. Number of combinations = 2 to the power n, where n is the number of chromosome pairs. For three pairs that is 8, for 23 pairs about 8.4 million.

Gametes and fertilisation

Reproduction

Gametes and fertilisation

Gametes and fertilisation

  1. Sperm: flagellum (tail) for swimming, mitochondria in the mid-piece for ATP, head with the nucleus and an enzyme sac (acrosome) to digest into the egg. Much smaller than the egg.
  2. Egg: about 0.1 mm, one of the largest cells, large cytoplasm with energy stores for the early zygote, nucleus of mother's chromosomes. A fertilisation membrane stops other sperm entering.
  3. Sperm nucleus fuses with egg nucleus giving a zygote with 46 chromosomes.
  4. Random fertilisation: any sperm could fertilise any egg, giving even more variation.

Reproductive systems

Reproduction

Reproductive systems

Reproductive systems

  1. Female: ovaries (make eggs, hormones), oviducts (fallopian tubes, funnel catches egg, fertilisation site), uterus (womb, implantation), cervix, vagina.
  2. Male: testes in the scrotum (sperm best at about 34 °C), sperm ducts, seminal vesicles (fluid), prostate, urethra, penis (erectile tissue). Semen is sperm plus fluid, released by ejaculation.
  3. Ovulation: monthly release of an egg from an ovary. The zygote becomes an embryo and implants in the uterus lining. It becomes a fetus when recognisably human.

Pregnancy

Reproduction

Pregnancy

Pregnancy (1/2)

The placenta

Embryo forms it for exchange with the mother. Maternal and fetal blood do not mix. Fetal blood goes through two umbilical arteries to villi capillaries and returns by the umbilical vein. Oxygen, glucose, amino acids and antibodies diffuse from mother to fetus. Carbon dioxide and urea diffuse the other way. It also secretes progesterone to maintain pregnancy.

Amnion

The amnion membrane makes amniotic fluid that protects the fetus from bumps and sudden movements.

Pregnancy

Pregnancy (2/2)

Birth

1 Cervix dilates, uterine contractions, amnion tears (waters break). 2 Strong contractions push the baby through the cervix and vagina. 3 The placenta detaches and is expelled as the afterbirth. A hormonal signal from the fetus starts it.

Breastfeeding

Prolactin from the pituitary makes milk, oxytocin releases it. Milk is the perfect food, contains antibodies, and builds an emotional bond. Mother needs about 25% more energy, plus protein, calcium and vitamins. Formula milk is the alternative.

Hormones and puberty

Reproduction

Hormones and puberty

Hormones and puberty

  1. Primary sex characteristics are sex organs at birth. Puberty (girls 11 to 14, boys 13 to 16) gives sexual maturity: gametes mature and bodies adapt. Triggered by FSH and LH from the pituitary (gonadotrophic hormones).
  2. Boys: FSH makes sperm, LH makes testes secrete testosterone. Girls: ovaries secrete oestrogen. Testosterone and oestrogen also raise growth hormone for the growth spurt.
  3. Secondary sexual characteristics: Male: penis and testes grow, facial and body hair, muscle, voice breaks. Female: breasts, wider hips, menstruation, body hair.

The menstrual cycle

Reproduction

The menstrual cycle

The menstrual cycle

  1. About a month. Day 1 is first day of period.
  2. FSH from the pituitary makes a follicle grow in the ovary. The follicle makes oestrogen, which repairs and thickens the uterus lining, slows FSH and stimulates an LH peak, causing ovulation at about day 14.
  3. The remains of the follicle form the corpus luteum, making progesterone, which maintains the lining and inhibits FSH and LH so no more ovulation.
  4. If no fertilisation the corpus luteum breaks down, progesterone falls and the lining is shed (period). If fertilised, progesterone continues, the placenta takes over later, and periods stop.

Fertility treatment

Reproduction

Fertility treatment

Fertility treatment

  1. Infertility: up to 40% due to low sperm count (needs over 20 million per cm3 and mostly normal sperm). In women tests check ovulation, surgery for blocked oviducts or ovary cysts.
  2. FSH injections stimulate ovulation but too much causes multiple ovulation and multiple births, which are often premature and low mass.
  3. Artificial insemination puts healthy sperm in the uterus at ovulation.
  4. IVF (in vitro fertilisation): egg and sperm combined in a dish, or a sperm injected into an egg, embryo grown a few days then implanted in the uterus.

Growth and development

Reproduction

Growth and development

Growth and development

  1. Over 99% of genes are the same in all humans. A small fraction cause uniqueness. Genes act directly or via hormones: growth hormone raises protein synthesis and bone growth, thyroxine controls metabolic rate, testosterone and oestrogen drive secondary characteristics.
  2. Growth curve: infant growth spurt is fastest just after birth, rate falls till about 4, slower fall to 10, adolescent growth spurt at puberty (earlier in girls), finished by late teens.
  3. A baby's head is large relative to the body. Organs grow at different rates: kidney and liver follow body growth, reproductive organs only catch up after puberty, the brain reaches 90% of full size by age 6 (13% of baby body weight, 2% of adult). By age 2 the brain has its adult cell number.
