Genes and alleles

Genes and alleles

Genes and alleles (1/4)

Allele

An alternative form of a gene, giving different versions of a characteristic.

Dominant allele

One that is expressed in the phenotype whether or not another allele is present. Shown with a capital letter.

Recessive allele

One that is expressed only when no dominant allele is present. Shown with a small letter.

Genes and alleles

Genes and alleles (2/4)

Genotype

The genetic make-up of an organism, for example RR, Rr or rr.

Phenotype

The appearance of an organism.

Homozygous

Two identical alleles of a gene (RR or rr).

Genes and alleles

Genes and alleles (3/4)

Heterozygous

Two different alleles (Rr).

Genes are sections of DNA coding for proteins. Homologous chromosomes carry the same genes in the same order but may have different alleles.

Example

free earlobe is dominant and attached earlobe is recessive. For guinea pigs, rosettes R is dominant to smooth r.

Genes and alleles

Genes and alleles (4/4)

The heterozygote and dominant homozygote have the same phenotype. A recessive allele is only expressed in the homozygous recessive (rr).

Genetic crosses

Heredity

Genetic crosses

Genetic crosses (1/2)

Monohybrid cross

A cross dealing with one gene.

Meiosis separates each pair so a gamete gets one allele. Fertilisation brings them together.

RR × rr gives all Rr (F1), all with rosettes. F1 × F1 (Rr × Rr) gives F2 genotypes 1 RR: 2 Rr: 1 rr, phenotypes 3 rosettes: 1 smooth. A Punnett square shows this.

Genetic crosses

Genetic crosses (2/2)

Ratios are expected only

with many offspring (400 would be about 300 and 100), small families can differ by chance because fertilisation is random. Ratios can be written as percentages or decimals.

Parents' sex does not matter in non-sex-linked crosses.

Pedigrees

Heredity

Pedigrees

Pedigrees (1/2)

Pedigree

A family tree showing the inheritance of a feature.

Lines join parents to children, first-born is on the left, rows are generations.

To find which allele is dominant

look for two parents with the same feature and a child with the contrasting one. The feature in the parents is dominant.

Pedigrees

Pedigrees (2/2)

Polydactyly (extra digits) is a dominant allele D, so DD or Dd have it, dd are normal. Letters P and p look too alike so are not used.

A person with the dominant trait who has a normal child or normal parent must be heterozygous. Some individuals may be DD or Dd and cannot be told apart.

Codominance

Both alleles are expressed in the phenotype of a heterozygote.

Chestnut RR × white WW horses give all red roan RW. Different letters used as neither is dominant. Red roan × red roan gives chestnut, red roan, white in ratio 1: 2: 1. Most genes show some range of dominance.

Codominance and multiple alleles

Codominance and multiple alleles (3/3)

ABO blood groups

three alleles (multiple alleles), IA, IB and IO. IA and IB are codominant and IO is recessive to both.

Heterozygous A (IA IO) × heterozygous B (IB IO) can give all four groups, A, B, AB and O.

Sex determination

Heredity

Sex determination

Sex determination

Sex chromosomes

females XX, males XY. Humans have 44 non-sex chromosomes plus two sex chromosomes. The presence of the Y decides male. Karyotype is a picture of all chromosomes.

XY × XX

all eggs carry X, half of sperm carry X and half Y, so 50% male and 50% female. Overall male to female births are 1: 1, but individual families can differ.

Sex-linked genes

Heredity

Sex-linked genes

Sex-linked genes (1/3)

Sex-linked gene

A gene carried on a sex chromosome, usually the X, since the Y is smaller and carries few genes.

A male has only one allele of an X-linked gene, so a single recessive allele shows. Females need two, and heterozygous females are carriers.

Haemophilia

blood fails to clot because a clotting protein is missing. Recessive allele h, normal H.

Sex-linked genes

Sex-linked genes (2/3)

Genotypes

XH Y normal male, Xh Y haemophiliac male, XH XH normal female, XH Xh carrier, Xh Xh haemophiliac female. Two healthy parents (carrier mother, normal father) can have an affected son.

A girl needs a haemophiliac father and a carrier or affected mother, so it is much rarer in girls. Treatment is clotting factor injections.

Sex-linked genes

Sex-linked genes (3/3)

Red-green colour blindness

recessive allele g for the pigment in cones that detects green light. About 6% of boys but 0.4% of girls. Inherited like haemophilia.

Normal male × carrier female gives 50% of sons colour blind and 50% of daughters carriers.

Cystic fibrosis

Heredity

Cystic fibrosis

Cystic fibrosis (1/3)

Gene therapy Put a working gene into the patient's DNA using a vector. A harmless virus was tried but few cells took it up, as lung defences blocked it.

In 2015 sprayed lipid droplets carrying the gene gave small but significant improvement in lung function. Research continues.

Cystic fibrosis

Cystic fibrosis (2/3)

Recessive allele n, normal N. Two carriers (Nn × Nn) give 25% with CF (nn), 50% carriers and 25% normal, so a 3: 1 phenotype ratio. High mutation rate in the gene, and over 1500 mutations known.

Cystic fibrosis

Cystic fibrosis (3/3)

Thick, sticky mucus

blocks airways so cilia cannot clear it, causing breathing problems and chest infections, blocks the intestine (poor absorption), blocks the pancreatic duct (poor digestion), blocks tubes from the testes (males mostly infertile).

The faulty gene affects a membrane protein carrying chloride ions.

Treated with drugs, medications and physiotherapy, extending life.

Genes and environment

Heredity

Genes and environment

Genes and environment (1/2)

Identical twins come from one zygote that splits, so are genetically identical, but differ in appearance because of environment, especially if brought up apart.

Height and weight depend on many genes and on diet. Skin colour is inherited but sunlight raises melanin.

Intelligence has both genetic and environmental causes, the nature versus nurture argument.

Genes and environment

Genes and environment (2/2)

Variation in a species is genetic, environmental or both.

Looking ahead: dihybrid inheritance

Two genes at once, for example short hair H dominant to long h and dark eyes E dominant to pink e. HHEE × hhee gives all HhEe, with short hair and dark eyes.
