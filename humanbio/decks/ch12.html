Genes and alleles

Genes and alleles (1/3)

Allele

An alternative form of a gene, giving different versions of a characteristic.

Dominant allele

One that is expressed in the phenotype whether or not another allele is present. Shown with a capital letter.

Recessive allele

One that is expressed only when no dominant allele is present. Shown with a small letter.

Genes and alleles (2/3)

Genetic crosses

Genetic crosses (1/2)

Monohybrid cross

A cross dealing with one gene.

  1. Meiosis separates each pair so a gamete gets one allele. Fertilisation brings them together.
  2. RR × rr gives all Rr (F1), all with rosettes. F1 × F1 (Rr × Rr) gives F2 genotypes 1 RR : 2 Rr : 1 rr, phenotypes 3 rosettes : 1 smooth. A Punnett square shows this.

Genetic crosses (2/2)

Cross two heterozygous (Rr × Rr) guinea pigs. What fraction have smooth coats?

OffspringRR, Rr, Rr, rr = 3 with rosettes and 1 smooth
1 in 4, which is 25%

  2. Parents' sex does not matter in non-sex-linked crosses.

Pedigrees

Heredity

Pedigrees

Pedigrees

Pedigree

A family tree showing the inheritance of a feature.

  1. Lines join parents to children, first-born is on the left, rows are generations.
  2. To find which allele is dominant: look for two parents with the same feature and a child with the contrasting one. The feature in the parents is dominant.
  3. Polydactyly (extra digits) is a dominant allele D, so DD or Dd have it, dd are normal. Letters P and p look too alike so are not used. A person with the dominant trait who has a normal child or normal parent must be heterozygous. Some individuals may be DD or Dd and cannot be told apart.

Pedigrees · diagram

Reading a pedigree

Key

Square male, circle female. Filled = affected. Horizontal line: parents. Vertical line: children.

Clue

Two unaffected parents, affected child: both are carriers (Ff). Next child affected: 1 in 4.

Codominance and multiple alleles

Heredity

Codominance and multiple alleles

Codominance and multiple alleles (1/2)

Codominance

Both alleles are expressed in the phenotype of a heterozygote.

  1. Chestnut RR × white WW horses give all red roan RW. Different letters used as neither is dominant. Red roan × red roan gives chestnut, red roan, white in ratio 1 : 2 : 1. Most genes show some range of dominance.
  2. ABO blood groups: three alleles (multiple alleles), IA, IB and IO. IA and IB are codominant and IO is recessive to both.

Sex determination

Heredity

Sex determination

Sex determination (1/2)

  1. Sex chromosomes: females XX, males XY. Humans have 44 non-sex chromosomes plus two sex chromosomes. The presence of the Y decides male. Karyotype is a picture of all chromosomes.
  2. XY × XX: all eggs carry X, half of sperm carry X and half Y, so 50% male and 50% female. Overall male to female births are 1 : 1, but individual families can differ.

Sex-linked genes

Sex-linked genes (2/2)

  1. A male has only one allele of an X-linked gene, so a single recessive allele shows. Females need two, and heterozygous females are carriers.
  2. Haemophilia: blood fails to clot because a clotting protein is missing. Recessive allele h, normal H. Genotypes: XH Y normal male, Xh Y haemophiliac male, XH XH normal female, XH Xh carrier, Xh Xh haemophiliac female. Two healthy parents (carrier mother, normal father) can have an affected son. A girl needs a haemophiliac father and a carrier or affected mother, so it is much rarer in girls. Treatment is clotting factor injections.
  3. Red-green colour blindness: recessive allele g for the pigment in cones that detects green light. About 6% of boys but 0.4% of girls. Inherited like haemophilia. Normal male × carrier female gives 50% of sons colour blind and 50% of daughters carriers.

Cystic fibrosis

Heredity

Cystic fibrosis

Cystic fibrosis (1/2)

  1. Recessive allele n, normal N. Two carriers (Nn × Nn) give 25% with CF (nn), 50% carriers and 25% normal, so a 3 : 1 phenotype ratio. High mutation rate in the gene, and over 1500 mutations known.
  2. Thick, sticky mucus: blocks airways so cilia cannot clear it, causing breathing problems and chest infections, blocks the intestine (poor absorption), blocks the pancreatic duct (poor digestion), blocks tubes from the testes (males mostly infertile). The faulty gene affects a membrane protein carrying chloride ions.
  3. Treated with drugs, medications and physiotherapy, extending life.
Spec 11.22 Describe how gene therapy with viruses can be used to treat cystic fibrosis
Cystic fibrosis

Cystic fibrosis (2/2)

Gene therapy

Put a working gene into the patient's DNA using a vector. A harmless virus was tried but few cells took it up, as lung defences blocked it. In 2015 sprayed lipid droplets carrying the gene gave small but significant improvement in lung function. Research continues.

Genes and environment

Heredity

Genes and environment

Genes and environment

  1. Identical twins come from one zygote that splits, so are genetically identical, but differ in appearance because of environment, especially if brought up apart.
  2. Height and weight depend on many genes and on diet. Skin colour is inherited but sunlight raises melanin.
  3. Intelligence has both genetic and environmental causes, the nature versus nurture argument.
  4. Variation in a species is genetic, environmental or both.

Looking ahead: dihybrid inheritance

  1. Two genes at once, for example short hair H dominant to long h and dark eyes E dominant to pink e. HHEE × hhee gives all HhEe, with short hair and dark eyes.
