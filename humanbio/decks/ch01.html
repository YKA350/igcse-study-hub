Cell structure

Cells

Cell structure

Cell structure (1/3)

Cytoplasm

The jelly-like living material of a cell, between a solid and a liquid, containing organelles.

Organelle

A structure inside the cytoplasm with a particular job. Most are too small for a light microscope but show with an electron microscope.

Exam tip Electron microscope = higher magnification and resolution. Light microscope resolution is about 200 nm, electron microscope below 1 nm. Ribosomes (about 25 nm) show only with an electron microscope.
Cell structure

Cell structure (2/3)

Nucleus The largest organelle. Controls the cell. Holds chromosomes (46 in human body cells) carrying genes. Red blood cells have none.

Cell membrane A thin skin around the cell. Partially (selectively) permeable: some chemicals pass, others are controlled.

Endoplasmic reticulum A network of membranes. The spaces transport protein. Ribosomes sit on it.

Cell structure

Cell structure (3/3)

Ribosomes Tiny granules where proteins are made.

Mitochondria Do the reactions of respiration and release energy. Many in muscle and nerve cells.

DNA, genes and chromosomes

Cells

DNA, genes and chromosomes

DNA, genes and chromosomes (1/2)

Gene

A section of DNA that codes for a specific protein.

DNA is found in the nucleus in chromosomes. Genes control a cell by deciding which proteins it makes, including enzymes.

DNA is two strands of nucleotides coiled in a double helix.

Each nucleotide has deoxyribose sugar, a phosphate and a base.

DNA, genes and chromosomes

DNA, genes and chromosomes (2/2)

Bases

adenine (A), thymine (T), cytosine (C), guanine (G).

Base-pairing rule

A with T, C with G (complementary bases).

Strands are held by weak hydrogen bonds, easily broken so strands separate.

DNA, genes and chromosomes

DNA replication

Before a cell divides it copies each DNA molecule.

The strands separate. Each acts as a template for a new strand.

Enzyme DNA polymerase assembles the new strand from nucleotides.

Two identical molecules form, each with one old and one new strand. DNA is the only chemical that can copy itself.

DNA, genes and chromosomes

The genetic code (1/2)

Only one strand, the template strand, codes for protein.

Proteins are chains of amino acids. Three bases code for one amino acid, so it is a triplet code. TGT codes for cysteine.

A gene is the base sequence for all the amino acids of a protein.

DNA, genes and chromosomes

The genetic code (2/2)

The code is universal across organisms.

Proteins can be enzymes, structural (keratin, myosin) or functional (haemoglobin, some hormones).

Protein synthesis

Protein synthesis (2/3)

RNA

A second nucleic acid. Single stranded, contains ribose and uracil (U) instead of thymine.

Transcription In the nucleus the DNA unzips. RNA nucleotides pair with the template strand (G-C, C-G, T-A, A-U) to form mRNA. mRNA leaves through nuclear pores. DNA zips up.

Translation At a ribosome, mRNA codons (three bases) are matched by the anticodons of tRNA, each carrying its amino acid. Start codon AUG codes for methionine. Amino acids join in a polypeptide until a stop codon releases it.

Protein synthesis

Protein synthesis (3/3)

mRNA copies the DNA code. tRNA carries amino acids to ribosomes. A third type is rRNA in ribosomes.

20 amino acids but more than 20 codons, because some use more than one, e.g. GGU, GGC, GGA, GGG all code for glycine.

Mutations

Cells

Mutations

Mutations (1/2)

Mutation

A random change in the DNA, usually a mistake in replication, that changes the base sequence and may change the amino acid sequence and protein.

Duplication and deletion A nucleotide inserted twice or lost shifts every following triplet, so the whole gene gives a different protein.

Substitution One nucleotide replaced. One triplet changes and may or may not change the amino acid.

Mutations

Mutations (2/2)

Inversion The order of bases in a triplet is reversed. Effects like substitution.

Body-cell mutations affect only that cell and are lost when the person dies. Only mutations in gametes or the cells forming them are passed on.

Cell division

Cells

Cell division

Cell division

Human body cells have 46 chromosomes in 23 homologous pairs: diploid. Sex cells have 23: haploid.

Mitosis makes all cells except sex cells, for growth and replacing damaged cells. Meiosis makes gametes (Chapter 11).

A zygote is the cell formed when gametes join at fertilisation.

Cell division

Mitosis (1/2)

Exam tip Root tip cells of an onion are used to see mitosis because plant cells are large and clear.

DNA replicates first, forming two chromatids per chromosome.

Prophase

chromatids visible, joined at a centromere, nuclear membrane breaks down.

Metaphase

spindle forms, chromosomes line up at the equator.

Cell division

Mitosis (2/2)

Anaphase

spindle fibres shorten and pull chromatids to opposite poles.

Telophase

two new nuclei form and the cytoplasm divides.

Result

two genetically identical diploid daughter cells, a clone. Used for growth, repair, cloning and asexual reproduction.

Differentiation, tissues and organs

Cells

Differentiation, tissues and organs

Differentiation, tissues and organs (1/4)

Voluntary (skeletal)

Features: Striped, many nuclei per cell; Function: Rapid contraction to move bones, conscious control

Involuntary (smooth)

Features: Not striped, one nucleus per cell; Function: Slow rhythmic contraction in gut, bladder and blood vessels

Cardiac

Features: Branched, many nuclei, striped; Function: Rhythmic contraction in the heart without tiring

Differentiation, tissues and organs

Differentiation, tissues and organs (2/4)

Tissue

A group of similar cells with one function. An organ is a group of different tissues working together.

A zygote divides by mitosis into an embryo. Cells become specialised under gene control: differentiation. All cells hold the same genes but switch different genes on and off to make different proteins.

Differentiation, tissues and organs

Differentiation, tissues and organs (3/4)

Examples

nerve cell (long axon), smooth muscle, ciliated epithelium (cilia move mucus), white blood cell (changes shape), red blood cell (haemoglobin), sperm (tail and head with genes).

An adaptation is a structure suited to a function.

Epithelium lines organs. Ciliated (trachea) has cilia. Squamous is flattened, e.g. lining the cheek.

Differentiation, tissues and organs

Differentiation, tissues and organs (4/4)

Bone

cells secrete calcium salts. Blood: red and white cells in plasma. Nervous tissue: brain, spinal cord, nerves.

Stem cells

Cells

Stem cells

Stem cells (1/2)

Stem cell

A cell that can divide many times by mitosis without differentiating and later become specialised.

Embryonic stem cells From early embryos, can become any cell type. Possible treatments for diabetes, Parkinson's and nerve damage but still experimental. Ethical objections because embryos are used.

Stem cells

Stem cells (2/2)

Adult stem cells Found in bone marrow, skin and intestine lining. Limited to a few cell types. Bone marrow transplants treat leukaemia and replace cells lost in chemotherapy, used for over 30 years.

Genetic engineering

Cells

Genetic engineering

Genetic engineering (1/2)

Recombinant DNA

DNA made by cutting a gene from one species and inserting it into another. The receiving organism is transgenic.

Bacteria have a chromosome plus small circular plasmids that can be swapped.

Restriction enzymes cut DNA at a specific base sequence (e.g. GAATTC on both strands). Staggered cuts give sticky ends.

Genetic engineering

Genetic engineering (2/2)

Ligase joins cut ends.

The plasmid is a vector. Steps

isolate plasmids, cut the gene from donor DNA, open plasmid with the same enzyme, mix with ligase, bacteria take up the plasmid, culture in fermenters.

Genetic engineering

Products

Human insulin from bacteria, better than animal insulin for diabetics.

Human growth hormone, replacing a source from dead bodies that spread Creutzfeldt-Jakob disease.

Hepatitis B vaccine proteins made by yeast, so the body makes antibodies.

Genetic engineering

Genetically modified plants (1/2)

Every cell must receive the gene, then whole plants are grown from a few cells.

Agrobacterium is a soil bacterium vector that inserts plasmids into plant cells. It does not infect cereals, so a gene gun fires DNA-coated gold pellets.

Golden rice has three genes (two from daffodil, one from a bacterium) to make beta-carotene, converted to vitamin A, to prevent blindness. Ethical and environmental objections exist.

Genetic engineering

Genetically modified plants (2/2)

Potatoes, bananas, lettuce, carrots and tobacco have been engineered to make hepatitis B proteins for vaccines.

Genetic engineering

Membranes

Membranes separate reactions into compartments. Mitochondria have an outer membrane and an inner membrane folded into cristae where aerobic respiration happens.
