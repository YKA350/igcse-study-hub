Chapter in a nutshell

  • Computers use binary because transistors are on/off switches (digital, not analogue).
  • A bit is 0 or 1; a byte is 8 bits.
  • Place values 128 64 32 16 8 4 2 1.
  • Signed numbers: sign and magnitude, two's complement.
  • Binary addition, logical and arithmetic shifts, overflow.
  • Hexadecimal is shorthand for binary: 1 hex digit = 4 bits.
1Why binary
Why binary
Binary

A base-2 number system using only the digits 0 and 1. Information is represented by only two values, e.g. voltage or no voltage.

Digital

Information represented by certain fixed values. Systems with separate states are digital.

Analogue

Signals represented by a continuously variable quantity, like a dimmer switch. Needs very accurate sending and receiving systems.

  1. The processor holds billions of transistors connected to form circuits.
  2. Transistors act like switches with two states, on or off, so there are no communication errors from in-between values.
  3. Numbers, text, graphics, sound and program instructions are all streams of 1s and 0s. The program decides how to interpret them.
  4. Human nerves are digital too: impulses have the same intensity, and the frequency signals strength.
2Bits and combinations
Bits and combinations
  1. A bit (binary digit) is the smallest unit of data, 1 or 0.
  2. 1 bit gives 2 combinations, 2 bits give 4, 3 bits give 8, 4 bits give 16.
  3. The number of combinations is 2 to the power of the number of bits.
  4. Representing 52 letters plus punctuation needs more bits, so bits are grouped.
  5. A byte is the basic group, typically 8 bits.

Overflow

An error when a computer tries to handle a number that is too large for the bits available, e.g. a result needing 9 bits in an 8-bit register.

5Signed numbers
Sign and magnitude
  1. Unsigned integers are zero or positive numbers only.
  2. In sign and magnitude the left-most bit is the sign (0 positive, 1 negative) and the other bits are the magnitude.
  3. Example: +28 is 00011100 and −28 is 10011100.
  4. It wastes a pattern on −0 and adding signed numbers needs extra rules.
Two's complement
  1. The left-most bit has a negative place value: −128 64 32 16 8 4 2 1.
  2. To find the two's complement (the negative) of a number: write the positive number in binary, invert every bit, then add 1.
  3. Example: +28 is 00011100. Invert to 11100011. Add 1 to get 11100100, which is −28.
  4. Convert back by inverting and adding 1, or by using place values with −128.

6Binary shifts
Binary shifts

Logical shift

Bits move left or right and empty places are filled with 0. A left shift multiplies an unsigned number by 2 for each place. A right shift divides by 2 for each place and may lose bits.

Arithmetic shift

Used on signed (two's complement) numbers. The sign bit is kept so the sign stays correct. A right arithmetic shift copies the sign bit into the new left-most place.

7Hexadecimal
Hexadecimal
Hexadecimal

A base-16 number system using 0 to 9 and A to F, where A = 10 up to F = 15.

  1. One hex digit represents 4 bits (a nibble).
  2. Hex is shorter and easier for humans to read, remember and type than binary.
  3. It is used for memory addresses, colours and error codes.
