Ethics and privacy

Privacy

Ethics and privacy

Ethics and privacy (1/3)

Ethics

Principles about what is morally right or wrong that govern behaviour. An action can be legal but not ethical.

Privacy

The right to be left alone and free from unwanted scrutiny and intrusion.

Personal data

Information that is personal and unique to an individual.

Ethics and privacy

Ethics and privacy (2/3)

Collecting and analysing personally identifiable information can be a massive invasion of privacy.

Posting on social media, signing up for accounts, web email and search engines all add to data held about you, such as where you live, your friends, bank details and places you visit.

It is stored on the company's servers, not yours.

Schools and other organisations also keep records.

Ethics and privacy (3/3)

Concerns

Privacy

Concerns

Concerns (1/2)

Cookies Small data files recording your browsing history, sites visited and products you look at, so shops learn about you. European law lets consumers opt out.

Spyware A program stored on your drive, usually without your knowledge, that collects information and sends it to a third party.

Many people have little or no control over who has the data, how it is used, how secure and how accurate it is.

Concerns

Concerns (2/2)

Weak security leads to phishing, scams and identity theft, which is stealing personal details such as bank details for purchases and debts.

Inaccurate information can follow a person for life and is hard to correct.

Data protection laws usually say data must be processed fairly and lawfully, used only for the lawful purpose collected, be adequate, relevant and not excessive, be accurate and up to date, be kept no longer than necessary and be kept secure.

Benefits and drawbacks

Privacy

Benefits and drawbacks

Benefits and drawbacks (1/3)

Big data Analysis of huge amounts of data gives insights, e.g. finding drug side effects, optimising city energy use, tracing the spread of disease.

Separate harmless facts combined build a very detailed profile, which risks identity theft or social engineering.

Surveillance CCTV, drones, number plate recognition, bugging and tracking devices monitor people, often without their knowledge.

Benefits and drawbacks

Benefits and drawbacks (2/3)

Accepted for security but also used by employers and companies, and criminals. Edward Snowden (2013), a whistle-blower, exposed the scale of government monitoring.

Police track vehicles in real time with number plate recognition and CCTV, keep records for two years, and use facial recognition.

Location-based services Let people share their location, meet friends and find places, but also allow others to track where you live and what you do.

Benefits and drawbacks

Benefits and drawbacks (3/3)

Giving information allows a more personalised service, e.g. faster purchases from an account.

But targeting adverts at financially vulnerable people at products they cannot afford is questionable.

Privacy-enhancing tools

Privacy

Privacy-enhancing tools

Privacy-enhancing tools (1/3)

Encryption

Stops unauthorised people reading your data

Cookie cleaners, anti-spyware and ad blockers: Detect and remove cookies, spyware and adware

Identity management service

A trusted third party issues an identifier so you can transact without revealing personal information

Privacy-enhancing tools

Privacy-enhancing tools (2/3)

Password manager

Stores logins in an encrypted database behind one master password

These tools help but are not 100 per cent effective.

Data breaches steal personal and financial details held by companies.

Privacy-enhancing tools

Privacy-enhancing tools (3/3)

Ransomware blocks access to a system until money is paid. WannaCry (2017) infected computers in 150 countries including the NHS.
