Ethics and privacy

Ethics and privacy (1/2)

Ethics

Principles about what is morally right or wrong that govern behaviour. An action can be legal but not ethical.

Privacy

The right to be left alone and free from unwanted scrutiny and intrusion.

Personal data

Information that is personal and unique to an individual.

Spec 6.1.2 Understand the ethical impact of using technology
Ethics and privacy

Ethics and privacy (2/2)

  1. Collecting and analysing personally identifiable information can be a massive invasion of privacy.
  2. Posting on social media, signing up for accounts, web email and search engines all add to data held about you, such as where you live, your friends, bank details and places you visit. It is stored on the company's servers, not yours.
  3. Schools and other organisations also keep records.
  4. Facebook holds the biggest database of faces in the world.

Concerns

Privacy

Concerns

Concerns (1/2)

  1. Many people have little or no control over who has the data, how it is used, how secure and how accurate it is.
  2. Weak security leads to phishing, scams and identity theft, which is stealing personal details such as bank details for purchases and debts.
  3. Inaccurate information can follow a person for life and is hard to correct.

Concerns

Concerns (2/2)

Cookies

Small data files recording your browsing history, sites visited and products you look at, so shops learn about you. European law lets consumers opt out.

Spyware

A program stored on your drive, usually without your knowledge, that collects information and sends it to a third party.

  1. Data protection laws usually say data must be processed fairly and lawfully, used only for the lawful purpose collected, be adequate, relevant and not excessive, be accurate and up to date, be kept no longer than necessary and be kept secure.

Benefits and drawbacks

Benefits and drawbacks (1/3)

  1. Giving information allows a more personalised service, e.g. faster purchases from an account.
  2. But targeting adverts at financially vulnerable people at products they cannot afford is questionable.

Benefits and drawbacks

Benefits and drawbacks (2/3)

Big data

Analysis of huge amounts of data gives insights, e.g. finding drug side effects, optimising city energy use, tracing the spread of disease. Separate harmless facts combined build a very detailed profile, which risks identity theft or social engineering.

Surveillance

CCTV, drones, number plate recognition, bugging and tracking devices monitor people, often without their knowledge. Accepted for security but also used by employers and companies, and criminals. Edward Snowden (2013), a whistle-blower, exposed the scale of government monitoring. Police track vehicles in real time with number plate recognition and CCTV, keep records for two years, and use facial recognition.

Benefits and drawbacks (3/3)

Location-based services

Let people share their location, meet friends and find places, but also allow others to track where you live and what you do.
