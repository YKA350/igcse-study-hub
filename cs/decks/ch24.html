E-waste

Any form of discarded electronic equipment, including computing technology.

Non-renewable materials

Resources that cannot be replaced naturally. Only a certain amount exists.

The problem

The problem (2/2)

Demand for devices is growing and the lifespan of a smartphone is under two years, so more is thrown away.

Effects

using up non-renewable materials, harmful e-waste, vast energy use and damage to people's health.

Use

Computing and the environmental impact of technology

Use

Use (1/3)

About 2 billion connected PCs and laptops and over 6 billion mobile devices together use huge amounts of electricity, more than the energy used making them.

Efficiency has improved but use depends on the task: 3D modelling, complex calculations and games use a lot.

Sleep mode can cut consumption by over 50 per cent when idle.

Use

Use (2/3)

Data centres use vast power to run and cool equipment, more than aviation. Small private or government centres are least efficient.

Facebook built a centre near the Arctic Circle in Sweden to use hydroelectric power and cold air for cooling.

Carbon footprint is the carbon dioxide an individual or organisation produces from the energy they consume.

Use

Use (3/3)

Green computing is efficient use of resources: energy efficiency, safer materials and eco-friendly systems.

Disposal

Disposal (1/2)

E-waste is growing fast (UN StEP

soon as heavy as eleven great pyramids). It was expected to top 52 million tonnes by 2021 with only about 20 per cent recycled.

Much is sent to developing countries and dumped in landfill. Toxic substances leak into the ground, water supplies, the food chain and the air.

Disposal

Disposal (2/2)

Each million mobile phones contain about 24 kg of gold, 250 kg of silver and 9 tonnes of copper, so poor local people strip them without protection, risking mercury and lead exposure and toxic fumes.

Countries set targets for collection, recycling and recovery, and for safe disposal of dangerous substances. Many manufacturers run recycling programmes.

Ways to extend life

reuse and refurbish pre-owned devices, and repair them.

Helping the environment

Computing and the environmental impact of technology

Helping the environment

Helping the environment (1/2)

Climate change NASA analyses satellite data and sea temperatures. Wireless sensor probes under glaciers measure temperature, pressure and movement. Oxford uses spare home computer time to model whether warming caused UK floods.

Early warning Tsunami warning systems use sensor networks and communications to evacuate coasts.

Conservation GPS and satellites track Malaysian elephants. Transponders on bees study disease and pesticides. Mobile phones listen for illegal logging and alert rangers.

Helping the environment

Helping the environment (2/2)

Energy Sensors in buildings monitor light, temperature, people and electricity so use adjusts automatically. Solar farms track the sun and adjust panels. Battery storage holds surplus power.
