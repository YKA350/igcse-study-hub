This chapter in a nutshell

Digital inclusion is affordable access to technology and the skills to use it.

The digital divide separates the technology-empowered from the excluded.

Exclusion affects information, jobs, democracy, the economy and social contact.

Governments run programmes to reduce it.

The digital divide

The digital divide (2/2)

  1. The divide exists between industrialised and developing countries and between groups in the same country.
  2. Contributing factors include poor or no connectivity (the main one), cost, lack of skills and lack of devices.
  3. Equally, legislation and policy aims to spread benefits to all.

Moving towards inclusion

Moving towards inclusion (1/2)

  1. Colombia: government subsidies for low-income households including fixed Internet, and satellite links for remote schools.
  2. India: an online gateway for public services such as identity, passports, jobs, tax and pensions, plus digital literacy programmes.
  3. Kenya: a digital learning programme in primary education.
  4. Thailand: more than 20 rural Internet centres building ICT skills.

Moving towards inclusion (2/2)

  1. Google's Project Loon used high-altitude balloons to extend connectivity.
