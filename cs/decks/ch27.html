Professionalism in computing

Professionalism

The skill and competence expected of a person in a professional setting.

  1. Computer scientists write software, create mobile apps, design embedded systems, devise security policies and invent products.
  2. They work for big companies such as Google and Apple, start-ups, hospitals, universities, or are self-employed.
  3. Wherever they work they are expected to behave ethically and show professionalism.
  4. A start-up is a company in the first stage of developing a product or service.

Codes of conduct

Codes of conduct

  1. The British Computer Society (BCS) is the Chartered Institute for IT. Its Code of Conduct sets out the standards members must uphold.
  2. It says to respect the wellbeing, privacy and security of the environment and people, and never stop learning and gaining professional knowledge.
  3. Many computer scientists pay for membership of a professional association: access to conferences, training and publications, networking, and a CV that carries weight with employers.

Ethical dilemmas

Ethical dilemmas

  1. A developer of car braking software finds a bug but fixing it delays completion.
  2. She knows hackers could possibly stop the brakes.
  3. A competitor offers to pay for her code.
  4. Her employer makes claims about the system that are not entirely true.
  5. Professionals should put safety and honesty first, because their work could put lives at risk.
