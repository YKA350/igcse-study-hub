Sexual and asexual

Two ways to reproduce.

Reproduction

Sexual vs asexual

Sexual

Two parents. Gametes fuse at fertilisation to form a zygote. Gametes are haploid (23), zygote diploid (46). Offspring differ: variation. Slower.

Asexual

One parent, no gametes. Mitosis makes identical clones. Fast, but no variation. E.g. bacteria, yeast budding, plant runners.

Exam tipFertilisation: fusion of the nuclei of a male and female gamete.

Cycle

Hormones of the cycle

Oestrogen

Ovaries. Repairs and thickens the uterus lining.

Progesterone

Corpus luteum. Maintains the lining. If an embryo implants it stays high, so no periods.

FSH (Biology only)

Pituitary. Egg matures in a follicle; ovary makes oestrogen.

LH (Biology only)

Pituitary surge triggers ovulation and the corpus luteum.

Common mistakeDay 1 is the start of a period, ovulation is around day 14.

Pregnancy and the placenta

Feeding and protecting the fetus.

Pregnancy

The placenta

Exchange

To fetus: O₂, glucose, amino acids, water, ions, antibodies. To mother: CO₂, urea. By diffusion.

Adapted

Large surface area, thin barrier. Bloods do not mix.

Amniotic fluid

Cushions the fetus against bumps, keeps temperature steady.

Harmful substances

Can cross

Puberty

Secondary sexual characteristics.

Puberty

Testosterone and oestrogen

Boys: testosterone

Sperm production starts, voice deepens, face and body hair, more muscle, penis and testes enlarge.

Girls: oestrogen

Ovulation and periods start, breasts develop, hips widen, pubic and underarm hair, fat on hips and thighs.

RememberSecondary sexual characteristics develop at puberty. Primary ones (the sex organs) are present at birth.
