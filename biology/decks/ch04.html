Energy needs

What changes how much we need.

Energy

What affects energy needs

Age

Teenagers need lots for growth; the elderly need less.

Activity

Active people respire more.

Sex and size

Males usually need more; bigger bodies need more.

Pregnancy

Extra energy, iron and calcium for the fetus.

Common mistakeExcess energy is stored as fat: obesity, heart disease, type 2 diabetes.

Required practical

Energy in food

  1. Measure 20 cm³ water into a boiling tube; record the temperature.
  2. Weigh the food sample.
  3. Burn it on a mounted needle under the tube until it stops.
  4. Record the final temperature.
Energy (J)

mass of water (g) × 4.2 × temperature rise (°C)

Exam tipResults are low: heat is lost. Use an insulated calorimeter to improve.
RememberEnergy per gram = energy ÷ mass of food.

Gut

Liver, gall bladder and pancreas

Liver

Makes bile. Stores glycogen. Breaks down excess amino acids.

Gall bladder

Stores bile and releases it into the duodenum.

Pancreas

Secretes amylase, protease and lipase into the duodenum.

Common mistakeBile is made in the liver, not the gall bladder. The large intestine absorbs water, not food.

Bile

Bile and gut pH

Not an enzyme

Bile neutralises stomach acid (it is alkaline) and emulsifies fats into small droplets.

Why emulsify?

Larger surface area for lipase to work on. A physical change, not chemical digestion.

Stomach

pH about 2 from HCl: best for pepsin; kills microbes.

Small intestine

Slightly alkaline: best for amylase, trypsin and lipase.
