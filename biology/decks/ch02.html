Plants and animals

The two multicellular groups.

Plants

Plants

Features

Multicellular, chloroplasts, photosynthesis, cellulose walls, store starch or sucrose.

Exam tipMaize: wind-pollinated cereal. Peas and beans: insect-pollinated legumes.

Animals

Animals

Multicellular

No chloroplasts, no cell walls. Usually a nervous system. Store glycogen. Most move.

Insects

3 body parts, 6 jointed legs, exoskeleton, antennae, compound eyes.

Mammals

Hair or fur, mammary glands, most give live birth, external ears.

Feeding and structure.

Fungi

Fungi

Structure

Hyphae form a mycelium. Chitin walls.

Saprotrophic

Extracellular enzymes digest food; soluble products absorbed.

Exam tipYeast is single-celled and buds.

Bacteria and viruses

Prokaryotes and non-living particles.

Bacteria

Bacteria

Prokaryote

No nucleus: circular DNA and plasmids. Cell wall.

Examples

Lactobacillus (yoghurt) and Pneumococcus (pneumonia).

Spec 1.3 describe the common features shown by prokaryotic organisms such as bacteria
Viruses

Viruses

Not cells

No cytoplasm, membrane or organelles. Genetic material inside a protein coat. About 100× smaller than a bacterium.

Parasitic

Reproduce only inside living cells, using the host’s machinery.

Examples

TMV (mottled leaves), influenza, HIV (causes AIDS).

Spec 1.4 understand the term pathogen and know that pathogens may include fungi, bacteria, protoctists or viruses

An organism or virus that causes disease. Can be a fungus, bacterium, protoctist or virus.

Exam technique

Mark-scheme phrases

Fungi

secrete extracellular enzymes, absorb soluble products

Viruses

reproduce only inside living cells

Bacteria

no nucleus; circular chromosome and plasmids

Common mistakeFungi do not photosynthesise. Viruses are not cells. Bacteria do have DNA, just no nucleus.
Exam tipFungi share cell walls and not moving with plants; no chloroplasts and glycogen with animals.

Recap

Chapter 2 in a nutshell

Eukaryotes have a nucleus; prokaryotes do not
Plants: chloroplasts, cellulose, starch
Animals: no wall or chloroplasts, glycogen
Fungi: hyphae, chitin, saprotrophic
Protoctists: single-celled eukaryotes
Bacteria: circular DNA, plasmids
Viruses: protein coat, reproduce in host cells
Pathogen: causes disease
