Hormones and glands

Chemical messengers.

Hormones

The endocrine system

Hormone

A chemical messenger secreted by an endocrine gland directly into the blood. It travels in the plasma to target organs.

Ductless glands

Release hormones straight into capillaries. Only target cells with the right receptors respond.

Slower, longer-lasting

Than nervous responses.

Common mistakeHormones travel in the plasma, not in red blood cells or nerves.

Adrenaline

Fight or flight.

Adrenaline

Effects of adrenaline

Frightened, excited or stressed
→
Adrenal glands release adrenaline
→
Heart rate and breathing rate rise
→
More blood, O₂ and glucose to muscles
→
More respiration, more energy

Also

Blood glucose rises (from liver glycogen), pupils dilate, blood to gut falls, alertness rises.

Exam tipExplain: ↑ heart rate → more O₂ and glucose to muscles → more respiration → more energy for contraction.

Blood glucose control

Negative feedback.

Glucose

Negative feedback keeps glucose steady

Negative feedback

A change away from normal triggers a response that reverses it.

Pancreas

Monitors glucose; releases insulin or glucagon. The liver is the main target.

Glucose

Diabetes and name check

Type 1

Pancreas makes too little insulin. Treated with insulin injections, diet and exercise.

Type 2

Cells stop responding to insulin; linked to obesity. Controlled by diet and exercise.

Don’t mix up

Glucose = sugar in blood. Glycogen = stored carbohydrate. Glucagon = hormone turning glycogen to glucose.

Common mistakeThe liver and muscles store glycogen, not the pancreas.
