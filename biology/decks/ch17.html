Chromosome numbers

Numbers

Halve, then restore

two sets of chromosomes in pairs; 46 in human body cells

Haploid (n)

one set; 23 in sperm and egg

Exam tipMeiosis halves the number so fertilisation restores 46.

2

Mitosis

Two identical cells.

Uses

Why mitosis?

Growth

Zygote to adult; plant meristems at shoot and root tips.

Repair

Replace damaged cells: skin, gut lining, bone.

Asexual reproduction

Runners, tubers, cuttings, bacteria, cloning.

Key point

Chromosomes are copied exactly first, so daughter cells are genetically identical: no variation.

Variation

Sources of variation

Random assortment

Pairs line up randomly. With 23 pairs there are over 8 million mixes.

Crossing over

Sections of chromosome swap between pairs.

Random fertilisation

Any sperm can fuse with any egg.

Calculation

Counting chromosomes

Cell with 2n = 8
Mitosiseach daughter has 8
Meiosiseach gamete has 4
Fertilisation4 + 4 = 8
2n = 8, n = 4

Rule

Mitosis keeps the number. Meiosis halves it. Fertilisation doubles it back.

Common mistakeHaploid is never 46 in humans: it is 23.

Why meiosis

Why halve the number?

  1. Gametes made by meiosis: n.
  2. Egg and sperm fuse.
  3. Zygote is 2n again.
  4. Mitosis grows the embryo.

Without meiosis

Chromosome number would double every generation.

Exam tipSexual reproduction mixes two parents' genes.

Compare

Same or different?

Mitosis

Daughter cells match the parent and each other: a clone.

Meiosis

Each gamete has a different mix of maternal and paternal chromosomes.

Exam technique

Mark-scheme phrases

Say this

Mitosis: two daughter cells, genetically identical, diploid. Meiosis: four cells, haploid, genetically different. Chromosomes copied first.

Avoid this

Meiosis for growth; mitosis making 4 cells; haploid = 46; human gametes by mitosis.
