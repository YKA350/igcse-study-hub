Two transport systems

Xylem and phloem.

Vascular tissue

Xylem vs phloem

Xylem

Dead hollow cells, no end walls, lignin. Water and mineral ions, upwards only. Supports the plant.

Phloem

Living cells, sieve plates, companion cells. Sucrose and amino acids, both directions: translocation.

Vascular tissue

Where are xylem and phloem?

Root

Xylem in the centre in an X shape.

Stem

Vascular bundles in a ring.

Leaf

Xylem above phloem in the midrib and veins.

Translocation

Movement of sucrose and amino acids in the phloem from sources (leaves) to sinks (roots, growing tips, fruits, stores).

3

Transpiration

Evaporation pulls water up the xylem.

Transpiration

The transpiration stream

Transpiration

Evaporation of water from mesophyll cell surfaces, then diffusion of water vapour out through the stomata.

Why useful

Carries water and minerals up, supplies photosynthesis, keeps cells turgid, cools the leaf.

Transpiration

Stomata and guard cells

Light

Guard cells take in water, become turgid and bend apart: stoma opens, CO₂ enters.

Dark or short of water

Guard cells flaccid: stoma closes, less water lost.

RememberMost stomata are on the lower surface: cooler and shaded.

Required practical

The potometer

  1. Cut the shoot under water.
  2. Joints airtight with Vaseline.
  3. Dry leaves; let in one air bubble.
  4. Measure bubble distance in a set time; repeat ×3.
Exam tipRate = distance ÷ time. Volume = π r² × distance.
