1From nucleus to DNA
Zooming in

Nucleus

Holds 46 chromosomes: 23 pairs.

Chromosome

A long coiled molecule of DNA.

Gene

A section of DNA coding for one protein.

Key terms

Chromosome pairs

Homologous pair

One chromosome from mother, one from father. Same genes in the same places, maybe different alleles.

Diploid and haploid

Body cells have 46 (diploid). Gametes have 23 (haploid).

Sex chromosomes

Pair 23: XX is female, XY is male.

Common mistakeChromosomes are made of DNA. Genes are sections of it, not the other way round.
2Sex determination
Sex inheritance

Result

2 XX girls : 2 XY boys, so 50 % each.

Why

Eggs are always X. Sperm carry X or Y, so the father's sperm decides the sex.

Exam tipDraw gametes, then the square, then state the ratio.
3The structure of DNA
The double helix

Nucleotide

Phosphate + deoxyribose sugar + a base. DNA is a polymer of nucleotides.

Two strands

Twisted into a helix, joined by hydrogen bonds between bases.

Code

The order of bases is the genetic code.

Complementary base pairs
Rule

A always pairs with T. C always pairs with G.

Complementary strand
StrandA T G C C A
PairA→T, T→A, G→C, C→G

From gene to protein

1. Transcription

DNA unzips; a complementary mRNA copy is made (U not T). It leaves through a nuclear pore.

2. Translation

mRNA binds a ribosome; tRNA anticodons match codons; amino acids join into a protein.

Codons and anticodons
Codon

three bases on mRNA coding for one amino acid

Anticodon

three bases on tRNA complementary to a codon

tRNA

Carries one specific amino acid to the ribosome.

  1. Base order sets codon order.
  2. Codon order sets amino acid order.
  3. Amino acid order sets protein shape.
  4. Shape sets function, e.g. an enzyme's active site.
Exam tipmRNA never contains T: it has U.
5Mutations
A substitution
Mutation

a rare, random change in genetic material that can be inherited

Effect

One base changes, so one amino acid changes, the protein may fold differently.

What mutations do

Most: neutral

Same amino acid, or non-coding DNA.

Some: harmful

Protein shape changes: cystic fibrosis, sickle cell, cancer.

Rare: beneficial

New advantage, the basis of evolution.

Increasing the mutation rate

Ionising radiation

X-rays, gamma rays, UV light.

Chemicals

Tar in tobacco smoke, some pesticides.

Cancer

Damaged DNA in body cells can cause cancer.

Exam tipMutations in gametes pass to offspring; in body cells they do not.
Resistance spreads

Why

A random mutation gives resistance. The antibiotic selects for it; it does not cause it.

Control

Do not over-prescribe; finish the course. MRSA is hard to treat.
