Blood and circulation

Biology · Unit 2 · Animal physiology · Last-minute notes · Book pages 70–83

Chapter in a nutshell

  • Large animals need a circulatory system
  • Blood: plasma, red cells, white cells, platelets
  • Phagocytes engulf; lymphocytes make antibodies
  • Vaccines give memory cells
  • Heart: 4 chambers; left ventricle thicker
  • Exercise and adrenaline raise heart rate
  • Arteries thick, veins valves, capillaries one cell thick
  • Double circulation keeps pressure high
1Why a transport system?
Small cells vs large animals

Single-celled

Large SA:V ratio, so diffusion over the surface is fast enough.

Multicellular

Small SA:V and cells far from the surface: diffusion is far too slow.

Circulatory system

Heart, blood vessels and blood carry O₂, CO₂, glucose, amino acids, urea, hormones, heat and antibodies.

2Blood
Composition of blood

Plasma

Carries CO₂, digested food, urea, hormones, heat.

Platelets

Cell fragments that help clotting.

White cells

Defend against pathogens.

Red blood cells are adapted

Haemoglobin

Iron-containing protein: forms oxyhaemoglobin in lungs, releases O₂ in tissues.

Shape

Biconcave disc: large surface area for O₂. Thin: short diffusion distance.

No nucleus

More room for haemoglobin.

Small and flexible

Squeeze through narrow capillaries.

3The immune system
Phagocytes and lymphocytes

Phagocytes

Engulf and digest pathogens (phagocytosis). Non-specific. Lobed nucleus.

Common mistakeSay engulf or ingest, not eat.

Lymphocytes

Make antibodies specific to the pathogen’s antigens. They stick pathogens together, mark them, or neutralise toxins.

RememberAntibody shape fits only one antigen.
Vaccination and memory cells

Vaccine

Dead or weakened pathogen (antigens). Lymphocytes make antibodies and memory cells without causing disease.

Later infection

Memory cells give a faster, larger secondary response.

Common mistakeVaccines contain antigens, not antibodies.

4The heart
Structure of the heart

Chambers

Atria receive blood; ventricles pump it out.

Left ventricle

Thicker wall: pumps at high pressure round the body.

Valves and septum

Valves stop backflow; septum separates the two sides.

Heart rate and exercise

Exercise

Rate and volume per beat increase: more O₂ and glucose to muscles, more CO₂ and heat removed.

Adrenaline

The fight-or-flight hormone increases heart rate.

Practical

Take resting pulse, exercise, then measure every minute until back to resting.

RememberFitter people: lower resting rate, faster recovery.
Coronary heart disease

Cause

Fatty deposits (atheroma) narrow coronary arteries: less O₂ for heart muscle, angina. A clot blocks it: heart attack.

Risk factors

High saturated fat, smoking, lack of exercise, stress, obesity, high blood pressure, high salt, genes, age, being male.

Double circulation

Why double?

Blood loses pressure in lung capillaries, so it returns to the heart to be pumped again at high pressure.

Vessels to learn

Hepatic artery, hepatic vein, hepatic portal vein (gut to liver), renal artery, renal vein.
