Chapter in a nutshell

  • Stimulus → receptor → CNS → effector → response
  • CNS = brain + spinal cord
  • Sensory, relay and motor neurones
  • Neurotransmitter crosses the synapse
  • Reflex: rapid, automatic, via spinal cord
  • Cerebrum, cerebellum, medulla
  • Iris controls pupil; cornea refracts most
  • Near object: ciliary muscle contracts, lens fatter

Receptors

Detect a stimulus and change it into electrical impulses. In sense organs: eyes (light), ears (sound, balance), nose and tongue (chemicals), skin (touch, pain, temperature).

Effectors

Muscles contract; glands secrete hormones, saliva or sweat.

2The nervous system
CNS and nerves
CNS

The brain and spinal cord: the coordinator. It receives impulses from receptors and sends impulses to effectors.

Nerves

Bundles of many neurones linking the CNS to the body.

Impulses

Electrical, up to 120 m/s. Responses are rapid, short-lived and precise.

Common mistakeSay electrical impulses, not messages.
Three types of neurone

Myelin sheath

Insulates the axon and speeds the impulse.

The synapse

Steps

Impulse arrives → neurotransmitter released → diffuses across gap → binds receptors → new impulse.

RememberSynapses make impulses travel one way only.
3The reflex arc
Reflex arc

Reflex action

Rapid, automatic, involuntary. No conscious thought, so it protects: pulling away from a hot object, blinking, the pupil reflex.

Spinal cord

The relay neurone in the spinal cord makes the decision, not the brain.

Regions of the brain

Cerebrum

Conscious thought, memory, language, voluntary movement, senses.

Cerebellum

Balance and coordination of muscles.

Medulla

Unconscious actions: heart rate and breathing rate.
