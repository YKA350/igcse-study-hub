Homeostasis and excretion

Biology · Unit 2 · Animal physiology · Last-minute notes · Book pages 104–117

Chapter in a nutshell

  • Homeostasis uses negative feedback
  • Hot: vasodilation and sweating
  • Cold: vasoconstriction and shivering
  • Lungs, kidneys, skin excrete
  • Ultrafiltration in the Bowman’s capsule
  • All glucose reabsorbed in the proximal tubule
  • ADH controls water reabsorption
  • Urine: water, urea, ions
1Homeostasis
What is homeostasis?
Homeostasis

Keeping the internal environment constant within narrow limits, despite changes inside or outside the body.

Negative feedback

A change from normal is detected and a response reverses it.

Controlled

Body temperature (about 37 °C, best for enzymes), blood water content, blood glucose, CO₂, urea, ions.

2Controlling body temperature
The skin

Control centre

Hypothalamus monitors blood temperature.

Endotherms

Humans keep a constant core temperature using heat from respiration.

Too hot and too cold

Too hot

Vasodilation: arterioles widen, more blood near the surface, more heat radiated. Sweating: evaporation takes heat. Hairs lie flat.

Too cold

Vasoconstriction: arterioles narrow, less heat lost. Shivering: respiration releases heat. Little sweating. Hairs stand up, trapping air.

Common mistakeCapillaries do not move. It is the arterioles supplying them that widen or narrow.

4The kidney
The urinary system

Kidneys

Filter blood, make urine.

Ureter, bladder, urethra

Carry, store, release urine.

The nephron

1. Ultrafiltration

High pressure forces small molecules into the capsule. Cells and proteins stay.

2. Selective reabsorption

All glucose goes back by active transport, plus some ions and water.

3. Water reabsorption

Loop of Henle and collecting duct, controlled by ADH.

ADH and water balance

Negative feedback

Hypothalamus detects blood water; pituitary releases ADH.

RememberADH acts on the collecting duct, making it more permeable to water.
Kidney structure

Cortex

Outer layer: filtration happens here.

Medulla

Inner layer: loops of Henle and collecting ducts.

Pelvis

Collects urine and passes it to the ureter.

RememberEach kidney contains about a million nephrons. Renal artery brings blood in; renal vein takes it away.
