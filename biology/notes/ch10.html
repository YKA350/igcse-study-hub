Plants and food

Biology · Unit 3 · Plant physiology · Last-minute notes · Book pages 136–153

Chapter in a nutshell

  • 6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂
  • Light, CO₂, temperature can limit the rate
  • Palisade: chloroplasts; spongy: air spaces
  • Stomata and guard cells control gas exchange
  • Glucose: respiration, starch, cellulose, sucrose
  • Nitrate for proteins, magnesium for chlorophyll
  • Starch test: boil, ethanol, rinse, iodine

1Photosynthesis
Inputs and outputs
Photosynthesis

Light energy absorbed by chlorophyll in chloroplasts converts carbon dioxide and water into glucose, releasing oxygen.

Equation

6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂

RememberPlants are autotrophs: they make their own food.
2Limiting factors
Effect of light and CO₂

Light

The energy supply. More light, faster rate, until another factor limits.

CO₂

A raw material at only 0.04 % of air. Often limiting.

Exam tipWhere the line rises, the x-axis factor is limiting. Where it levels off, another factor is.
Temperature

Enzymes

Photosynthesis is enzyme-controlled: faster as it warms, then enzymes denature above the optimum.

Limiting factor

The factor in shortest supply that limits the rate: light, CO₂ or temperature.

Common mistakeLight provides energy. It does not make energy.
RememberGreenhouses add CO₂ and light.
3The leaf
Section through a leaf

Palisade

Tall, packed with chloroplasts near the top: main site of photosynthesis.

Spongy

Air spaces give a large surface for gas exchange.

Stomata

Guard cells open and close the pores.

5Practicals
Testing a leaf for starch
Boil in water
→
Warm in ethanol (water bath)
→
Rinse in water
→
Add iodine

Why

Boiling kills and stops reactions; ethanol removes chlorophyll; rinsing softens; blue-black means starch.

Common mistakeNever heat ethanol over a flame. It is flammable.
Is light, chlorophyll or CO₂ needed?

De-starch

Keep the plant in the dark for 48 h so starch is used up.

Light

Cover part of a leaf with foil; only the uncovered part turns blue-black.

Chlorophyll

Variegated leaf: only green parts make starch.

CO₂

Bag with soda lime absorbs CO₂; no starch. Control has none.

Light intensity and pondweed
  1. Pondweed in water with sodium hydrogencarbonate (CO₂).
  2. Lamp at a set distance; wait 5 min.
  3. Count bubbles per minute.
  4. Repeat at 20 to 50 cm.
Exam tipCloser lamp: more bubbles. Use a heat shield to keep temperature constant.
Net gas exchange with indicator

Bright light

Photosynthesis > respiration: CO₂ taken up, indicator turns purple.

Dim light

Rates equal (compensation point): stays red.

Dark

Only respiration: CO₂ released, indicator turns yellow.

RememberPlants respire all the time, day and night. Tube with no leaf is the control.
