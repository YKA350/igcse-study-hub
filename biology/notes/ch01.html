3Enzymes
Lock and key
Enzyme

A protein that acts as a biological catalyst: speeds up reactions without being used up.

Specific

Each enzyme works on only one substrate because of its active site shape.

Metabolism

All the chemical reactions in a cell.

Temperature and pH

Heating

More kinetic energy, more collisions. Too hot: active site changes shape, substrate no longer fits. Denatured, permanently.

pH

Each enzyme has an optimum pH, e.g. pepsin pH 2, amylase pH 7. Wrong pH changes the active site.

Common mistakeCold does not denature enzymes. It only slows them.
Amylase and temperature
  1. Put iodine drops on a spotting tile.
  2. Mix starch and amylase in a water bath at a set temperature (20 to 60 °C).
  3. Every 30 s add a drop to the iodine.
  4. When it stays orange-brown the starch is digested: record the time.
  5. Rate = 1 ÷ time. Repeat at each temperature.

Control variables

Volume and concentration of starch and amylase, pH (use a buffer).

Catalase and pH (Biology only)

Potato breaks down H₂O₂ to water and oxygen. Use buffers and measure O₂ volume in a fixed time.

4Respiration
How cells get energy
Respiration

Enzyme-controlled reactions that release energy from glucose in every living cell, all the time.

ATP

Energy is first transferred to ATP, which provides energy for the cell.

Common mistakeRespiration releases energy. It does not make it.

Aerobic

glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water. C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O. In mitochondria.

Anaerobic (animals)

glucose → lactic acid

Anaerobic (yeast, plants)

glucose → ethanol + CO₂

After hard exercise, extra oxygen is needed to break down lactic acid.

Detecting CO₂

Hydrogencarbonate indicator: red → yellow with more CO₂. Limewater turns milky.

5Moving in and out of cells
Three ways in and out

Diffusion

Net movement from high to low concentration. Passive. O₂ in, CO₂ out.

Osmosis

Water moves from dilute to concentrated solution through a partially permeable membrane.

Active transport

Against the gradient (low to high), using energy from respiration and carrier proteins.

Speeding up diffusion

Larger surface area

Shorter distance

Thin membranes

Exam tipBigger objects have a smaller SA:V, so large organisms need exchange surfaces: alveoli, villi, root hairs.
Agar cubes and diffusion

Method

Cut agar cubes of different sizes containing indicator. Put them in acid and time how long until the colour disappears.

Result

Small cubes go colourless fastest: larger SA:V and shorter distance to the centre.

SA : V

SA = 6 × side², V = side³. The bigger the object, the smaller the ratio.

Exam tipSpec 2.17: diffusion and osmosis in living and non-living systems.
Cells in different solutions
RememberAnimal cells have no cell wall so they can burst. The plant cell wall stops bursting: turgid cells support the plant.
Osmosis in potato cylinders
  1. Cut equal potato cylinders, blot dry and weigh.
  2. Place each in a different sucrose concentration (0 to 1.0 mol/dm³).
  3. Leave the same time at the same temperature.
  4. Blot dry and re-weigh.
  5. Calculate % change and plot against concentration.
% change

(final − initial) ÷ initial × 100

Exam tipWhere the line crosses 0 % is the concentration inside the potato cells.
Visking tubing: osmosis in a non-living system

Method

Fill Visking tubing with concentrated sugar solution, fit a capillary tube and stand it in water. Mark the start level.

Result

Water moves in by osmosis so the level rises. With water inside and sugar solution outside, it falls.

Why

Visking tubing is a partially permeable membrane, like a cell membrane but not alive.

6Specialised cells and organisation
Differentiation and stem cells

Differentiation

A zygote divides by mitosis. Cells become specialised as genes are switched on or off.

Stem cells

Unspecialised cells that divide and differentiate. Embryonic can become any type; adult only a few.

Uses

Could treat diabetes, paralysis and Parkinson’s.

Issues

Ethical objections, risk of rejection, risk of uncontrolled division (cancer).

RememberPlants have stem cells in meristems.

7Levels of organisation
From organelle to organism
Tissue

A group of similar cells with the same function.

Organ

Several tissues working together, e.g. heart or leaf.

Organ system

Organs working together, e.g. circulatory, digestive.
