Fermentation

anaerobic respiration by microorganisms that makes useful products

About yeast

Single-celled fungus

Reproduces by budding. Not a bacterium.

Plants too

Plants also respire anaerobically: glucose → ethanol + CO₂.

Animals

Anaerobic respiration: glucose → lactic acid.

2Bread, beer and wine
Making bread

Why it rises

Yeast respires anaerobically; CO₂ bubbles are trapped in the dough.

Amylase

Enzymes in flour break starch to sugars that yeast uses.

Yeast and CO₂
  1. Yeast and sugar; thin oil layer.
  2. Delivery tube to limewater.
  3. Set the water bath; wait 5 min.
  4. Count bubbles per minute.
  5. Repeat 20–60 °C.
Exam tipOptimum about 35–40 °C, then enzymes denature. Control: boiled yeast.
Brewing drinks

Beer

Barley is germinated (malting): enzymes turn starch to maltose. Boil with hops, cool, add yeast. Anaerobic fermentation gives ethanol + CO₂.

Wine

Crushed grapes already have sugar. Yeast ferments it; an airlock lets CO₂ out. Stops near 15 % ethanol, which kills the yeast.

✓ Say this

Yeast respires anaerobically, CO₂ makes dough rise. Lactobacillus converts lactose to lactic acid. Water jacket removes heat.

✗ Avoid this

Ethanol raises bread; water jacket heats; yeast is a bacterium; no reason for contamination.
