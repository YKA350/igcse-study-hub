Reproduction in plants

Biology · Unit 3 · Plant physiology · Last-minute notes · Book pages 176–183

Chapter in a nutshell

  • Stamen: anther + filament; carpel: stigma, style, ovary
  • Insect flowers: bright petals, nectar, sticky pollen
  • Wind flowers: feathery stigmas, light pollen
  • Pollen tube carries the male nucleus to the egg
  • Ovule → seed; ovary → fruit
  • Germination: water, oxygen, warmth
  • Runners, cuttings, micropropagation give clones
  • No variation in clones

What is pollination?
Pollination

Transfer of pollen from an anther to a stigma.

Self

Same flower or plant.

Cross

Different plant of the same species: more variation.

Common mistakePollination is not fertilisation.
Common mistakeInsects do not eat pollen to pollinate: pollen sticks to their bodies.
3Fertilisation
The pollen tube
  1. Pollen lands on the stigma.
  2. Pollen tube grows down the style.
  3. Enters the ovule.
  4. Male nucleus fuses with the female nucleus: fertilisation, zygote.

After

Zygote → embryo; ovule → seed; ovary → fruit.

Why fruits?

Dispersal

Fruits spread seeds away from the parent: less competition for light, water and minerals.

Common mistakeThe ovule becomes the seed, not the ovary.
4Seeds and germination
Inside a seed

Germination needs

Water (activates enzymes, swells the seed), oxygen (respiration), warmth (enzymes).

Food

Enzymes break down starch and protein in the cotyledons until the seedling can photosynthesise.

RememberLight is not usually needed.
Conditions for germination

Exam tipSame type and number of seeds. Boiling removes dissolved oxygen; oil stops it returning.
5Asexual reproduction
Natural and artificial methods

Natural

Runners (strawberry), tubers (potato), bulbs (onion).

Cuttings

Stem in rooting powder (auxin), in damp compost.

Micropropagation

Tiny pieces on sterile agar with hormones.

RememberFast, cheap, keeps good features.
Common mistakeNo variation: all vulnerable to the same disease.
