Breathing and gas exchange

Biology · Unit 2 · Animal physiology · Last-minute notes · Book pages 40–51

Chapter in a nutshell

  • Breathing moves air; respiration releases energy
  • Air: trachea, bronchi, bronchioles, alveoli
  • Inhale: diaphragm flattens, ribs up, volume up, pressure down
  • Alveoli: large area, thin walls, capillaries
  • Exhaled air: less O₂, more CO₂
  • Smoking: tar, nicotine, CO
  • Emphysema, lung cancer, heart disease
  • Cilia and goblet cells keep airways clean
1The gas exchange system
Breathing vs respiration

Respiration

Chemical reactions in every cell that release energy from glucose.

Breathing (ventilation)

Muscle movements that move air into and out of the lungs.

RememberGas exchange: oxygen diffuses into the blood; carbon dioxide diffuses out.
Common mistakeNever mix up breathing and respiration.

Keeping the airways clean

Goblet cells

Make sticky mucus that traps dust and bacteria.

Ciliated cells

Cilia beat and sweep the mucus to the throat, where it is swallowed.

RememberAir is filtered, warmed and moistened on the way in.
Exam tipPath: nose or mouth → trachea → bronchi → bronchioles → alveoli.
2Ventilation
Inhaling and exhaling

Inhale

Diaphragm contracts and flattens; external intercostals raise ribs. Volume ↑, pressure ↓, air in.

Exhale

Diaphragm relaxes and domes; ribs fall. Volume ↓, pressure ↑, air out.

Exam tipMuscles contract → volume increases → pressure decreases → air moves in.
Common mistakeThe lungs do not suck air in. The diaphragm flattens, it does not move up when inhaling.
RememberForced exhale: internal intercostals pull ribs down.
The bell-jar model

How it works

Pull the sheet down: volume rises, pressure falls, balloons inflate.

Limitation

No ribs or intercostal muscles. The real diaphragm pulls itself down.

3Gas exchange in the alveoli
Alveoli are adapted

Adaptations

Huge surface area, thin walls (one cell thick), dense capillary network, moist lining.

Steep gradient

Ventilation and blood flow keep O₂ high and CO₂ low in the alveolus.

Exam tipSay diffusion down a concentration gradient.
Exhaled vs inhaled air

Limewater test

Breathe through a mouthpiece: inhaled air bubbles through tube A, exhaled through B. B turns milky much faster.

Conclusion

Exhaled air contains more CO₂.

Exercise and breathing rate
  1. Sit still 5 min; count breaths per minute (×3, take the mean).
  2. Exercise for a set time, e.g. 2 min of step-ups.
  3. Count breaths per minute, then every minute until back to resting.
  4. Keep intensity, duration and person the same.

Why it rises

Muscles respire faster, so need more O₂ and must remove more CO₂.

RememberStays high afterwards to repay the oxygen debt.
4Effects of smoking
Harmful substances

Tar

Carcinogens cause cancer; paralyses cilia.

Nicotine

Addictive; narrows arteries.

Carbon monoxide

Binds to haemoglobin, so less O₂ is carried.

Exam tipSmoke paralyses cilia and makes goblet cells produce more mucus: smoker’s cough and chronic bronchitis.
Smoking and disease

Emphysema

Alveolar walls break down, so surface area falls. Always breathless.

Lung cancer

Carcinogens make cells divide uncontrollably into a tumour.

Heart disease

Nicotine and CO damage artery walls: atheroma, narrowed arteries, coronary heart disease.

Smoking and lung cancer

Correlation

The more cigarettes per day, the higher the risk. Risk falls after giving up.

Causation

With lab evidence that tar carcinogens cause cancer, this shows smoking causes lung cancer.
