1What is GM?
Genes across species
Genetic modification

changing an organism's DNA, usually by moving a gene from one species to another

Transgenic

contains a gene from a different species

Why it works

The genetic code is universal.

GM vs selective breeding

Selective breeding

Mixes genes the organism already has. Slow, many generations.

Genetic modification

Adds a gene from any species. Much faster.

2The tools
Cutting and joining

Restriction enzyme

Cuts DNA at a specific base sequence, often leaving sticky ends.

DNA ligase

Joins DNA pieces by sealing the sugar-phosphate backbone.

Vector

Carries the gene into the host cell: plasmid or virus.

Recombinant DNA

DNA made by joining pieces from different sources

Sticky ends

Staggered cut

Leaves unpaired bases that can pair with complementary sticky ends.

Same enzyme

Cut the human gene and the plasmid with one enzyme so ends match.

3Making insulin
Six steps
Recombinant plasmid

Plasmid

Small ring of DNA in bacteria; used as a vector.

Result

Each bacterium copies the plasmid and makes human insulin.

Exam tipInsulin treats type 1 diabetes.
Advantages of GM insulin

The insulin

Identical to human insulin, so fewer reactions. Made in large amounts, quickly and cheaply. No animals needed.

The bacteria

Reproduce very quickly, have plasmids that are easy to modify, grow easily in fermenters.

4GM crops
Types of GM crop

Pest-resistant (Bt)

Bacterial gene makes a toxin that kills insects: less insecticide, higher yield.

Herbicide-resistant

Spray kills weeds, crop survives: less competition.

Golden Rice

Makes beta-carotene for vitamin A, preventing deficiency.

Making a pest-resistant crop
Benefits and concerns

Benefits

Higher yields; less pesticide; grow in poor conditions; more nutritious; medicines; gene therapy.

Concerns

Genes spread by pollen; harm to bees; pests evolve resistance; unknown health effects; less biodiversity; costly seeds.

Transgenic animals

How

A human gene is put in a fertilised egg of a sheep, goat or cow, switched on in milk. The animal is then cloned.

Examples

Sheep: alpha-1-antitrypsin for emphysema. Goats: antithrombin. Cows: human antibodies.

Exam tipCloning uses nuclear transfer (chapter 20).
