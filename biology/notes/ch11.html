Transport in plants

Biology · Unit 3 · Plant physiology · Last-minute notes · Book pages 154–167

Chapter in a nutshell

  • Xylem: dead, lignin, water and minerals up
  • Phloem: living, sucrose and amino acids both ways
  • Root hairs: large surface area
  • Water by osmosis, ions by active transport
  • Transpiration pulls water up the xylem
  • Light, temperature, wind increase; humidity decreases
  • Potometer: rate = distance ÷ time
  • Wilting: cells flaccid
1Two transport systems
Xylem vs phloem

Xylem

Dead hollow cells, no end walls, lignin. Water and mineral ions, upwards only. Supports the plant.

Phloem

Living cells, sieve plates, companion cells. Sucrose and amino acids, both directions: translocation.

Where are xylem and phloem?

Root

Xylem in the centre in an X shape.

Stem

Vascular bundles in a ring.

Leaf

Xylem above phloem in the midrib and veins.

Translocation

Movement of sucrose and amino acids in the phloem from sources (leaves) to sinks (roots, growing tips, fruits, stores).

2The roots
The root hair cell

Large surface area

Long extension of the cell.

Water

Enters by osmosis: soil water is more dilute than cell sap.

Ions

Active transport, using energy from respiration (needs O₂).

Plant cells and water
RememberThe cell wall stops turgid cells bursting.
Common mistakeWilting: more water lost than taken in, cells flaccid, leaves droop.
3Transpiration
The transpiration stream
Transpiration

Evaporation of water from mesophyll cell surfaces, then diffusion of water vapour out through the stomata.

Why useful

Carries water and minerals up, supplies photosynthesis, keeps cells turgid, cools the leaf.

Stomata and guard cells

Light

Guard cells take in water, become turgid and bend apart: stoma opens, CO₂ enters.

Dark or short of water

Guard cells flaccid: stoma closes, less water lost.

RememberMost stomata are on the lower surface: cooler and shaded.
4Factors and potometer
Factors affecting the rate
FactorEffectWhy
Light ↑increasesstomata open wider
Temperature ↑increasesmore kinetic energy: faster evaporation and diffusion
Humidity ↑decreasessmaller concentration gradient of water vapour
Wind ↑increasesmoist air blown away: steep gradient kept
Common mistakeHumidity slows transpiration. It does not speed it up.
The potometer
  1. Cut the shoot under water.
  2. Joints airtight with Vaseline.
  3. Dry leaves; let in one air bubble.
  4. Measure bubble distance in a set time; repeat ×3.
Exam tipRate = distance ÷ time. Volume = π r² × distance.
Changing one factor

Wind

Use a fan.

Light

Use a lamp.

Humidity

Plastic bag over the shoot.

Temperature

Change the room.

RememberA potometer measures water uptake, nearly all of which is lost by transpiration.
