1Producing more food
Controlling limiting factors

Light

Glass lets light in; lamps at night.

Temperature

Glass traps heat; heaters in winter.

CO₂ and water

Paraffin heaters or cylinders add CO₂; automatic watering.

Higher yield
Yield

amount of crop produced

  1. Raise light, temperature, CO₂.
  2. These are limiting factors.
  3. Photosynthesis is faster.
  4. More glucose, so higher yield.
Exam tipA paraffin heater gives heat and CO₂: both raise photosynthesis.

Advantages

Out-of-season crops, faster growth, protection from weather and birds, easier biological control.

Disadvantages

Costly to build and heat; fuel adds CO₂; pests and disease spread fast when warm and humid.

Why use fertiliser?

Harvested crops remove mineral ions from the soil. Fertilisers replace them.

N: nitrate

Makes proteins for growth.

P: phosphate

DNA, membranes, roots.

K: potassium

Enzymes, flowers, fruit.

2Controlling pests
Pesticides vs biological control

Pesticides

Fast and easy. But kill non-target species such as bees, persist in food chains, pests evolve resistance, must be reapplied.

Biological control

Natural predator, parasite or pathogen: ladybirds eat aphids. Specific, no resistance, but slow, and the agent may become a pest.

Bioaccumulation
Bioaccumulation

build-up of a persistent toxin along a food chain

3Air pollution
How acid rain forms

Source

Burning coal and fuels containing sulfur.

Effects

Kills trees, acidifies lakes so fish die, leaches minerals, damages limestone.

Reduce

Remove sulfur, chimney scrubbers, renewables.

Carbon monoxide

Source

Incomplete combustion: car engines, faulty boilers, smoke.

Reduce

Catalytic converters, servicing boilers, CO alarms.

Effect

CO binds to haemoglobin more strongly than oxygen, so the blood carries less oxygen: tiredness, dizziness, death.

Exam tipColourless and odourless, so you cannot detect it yourself.
4The greenhouse effect
The greenhouse effect

Natural

Gases absorb and re-radiate heat, keeping Earth warm. Essential for life.

Enhanced

Human activity adds more gas, trapping more heat: global warming.

Consequences

Sea level

Ice caps and glaciers melt: flooding of low land.

Weather

More storms and droughts; rainfall changes cause crop failure.

Wildlife

Ranges shift, some extinctions, coral bleaching.

5Water pollution
Pollution by sewage

Oxygen loss

Bacteria decompose the organic matter and respire aerobically, using up dissolved oxygen. Fish and invertebrates die.

Nutrients

Add nitrates and phosphates: eutrophication.

Disease

Pathogens such as cholera and typhoid spread in drinking water.

Common mistakeIt is the bacteria's aerobic respiration that uses the oxygen, not the algae.

Effects of cutting forests
Biodiversity
Biodiversity

Exam tipLoss of habitat is the main cause of falling biodiversity.

Threats

Protect
