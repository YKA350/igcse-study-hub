1Addition polymers
What is an addition polymer?
Addition polymer

formed by joining many small molecules called monomers

Monomers

Alkenes: the C=C opens and they add together.

Products

Nothing is lost: the polymer is the only product.

3Repeat units and monomers
Monomer to repeat unit
  1. Draw the alkene with C=C in the middle and four groups above and below.
  2. Change the double bond to single.
  3. Draw a continuation bond from each carbon, through square brackets.
  4. Write n after the bracket. Change nothing else.
Polymer to monomer
  1. Take two neighbouring chain carbons: that is the repeat unit.
  2. Remove brackets, n and continuation bonds.
  3. Put the double bond back between the two carbons.
Find the monomer
Poly(styrene)
Repeat unit–[CH₂–CH(C₆H₅)]ₙ–
RuleKeep C₆H₅ as one group; add C=C
CH₂=CHC₆H₅, styrene
Poly(propene)
Repeat unit–[CH₂–CH(CH₃)]ₙ–
RuleRemove brackets, n, bonds; add C=C
CH₂=CHCH₃, propene
4Disposing of addition polymers
The problems

Inert

Strong covalent bonds: unreactive.

Not biodegradable

Bacteria cannot break them down.

Burning

Toxic gases: CO, HCl (from PVC); also CO₂.

Landfill or incineration?
MethodAdvantagesDisadvantages
Landfillcheap; no toxic or greenhouse gases from plasticugly, smelly; needs land; lasts thousands of years
Incinerationlittle space; heat for homes or electricityexpensive; greenhouse and toxic gases; ash to landfill
5Condensation polymers
Condensation polymerisation
Condensation polymerisation

a dicarboxylic acid reacts with a diol to give a polyester and water (Chemistry only)

Monomers

Ethanedioic acid HOOCCOOH and ethane-1,2-diol HOCH₂CH₂OH.

Two ends

–COOH or –OH at each end, so the chain keeps growing.

Making a polyester
Equation

n HOOC–COOH + n HO–CH₂CH₂–OH → –[OC–CO–O–CH₂CH₂–O]ₙ– + 2n H₂O

Two types of polymerisation

6Biodegradable polyesters
Biopolyesters

Ester link

Polyesters can break down, but may take hundreds of years.

Biopolyesters

Biodegradable: break down much faster.

Example

polylactic acid (PLA) from corn starch lactic acid: biodegradable bags and surgical stitches

✓ Say this

Repeat unit has single C–C. Addition gives one product; condensation also water. Brackets with continuation bonds. Inert and non-biodegradable.

✗ Avoid this

Double bond in repeat unit; missing n; no continuation bonds.
