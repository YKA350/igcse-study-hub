Crude oil

Chemistry · Unit 4 · Organic chemistry · Last-minute notes · Book pages 268–276

Chapter in a nutshell

  • Crude oil: mixture of hydrocarbons
  • Bigger molecules: higher boiling, more viscous, darker
  • Complete: CO₂ + H₂O; incomplete: CO or soot
  • SO₂ and NOₓ cause acid rain
  • Cracking: long alkane to shorter alkane + alkene
1What crude oil is
Where crude oil comes from

Formation

Dead sea plants and animals buried millions of years ago; heat and pressure made crude oil.

Finite

A non-renewable resource.

Crude oil

a mixture of hydrocarbons, from tiny molecules to over 100 atoms

As molecules get bigger

Boiling point

Rises: stronger intermolecular forces.

Viscosity

More viscous: flows less easily; less volatile.

Colour and burning

Darker, and burn less easily.

2Fractional distillation
The fractionating column
  1. Crude oil heated until it boils.
  2. Vapours rise up the column.
  3. Each condenses at its boiling point and is removed.
  4. Gases never condense; bitumen is the residue.
Exam tipEach fraction is still a mixture, with a narrow range of sizes.

4Acid rain
Where the pollutants come from

Sulfur dioxide

Fuels contain sulfur: S + O₂ → SO₂. Power stations, factories, vehicles.

Nitrogen oxides

Hot car engines: N₂ + O₂ → 2NO.

Making the acid
Equation

2SO₂ + 2H₂O + O₂ → 2H₂SO₄

Acid rain facts

pH

Normal rain about 5.6 (dissolved CO₂). Acid rain below 5.6, often about 4.

Damage

CaCO₃ + H₂SO₄ → CaSO₄ + H₂O + CO₂: attacks limestone, marble, iron.

Solutions

Remove sulfur, scrub waste gases, catalytic converters.

SO₂ + water alone

Weaker sulfurous acid, H₂SO₃.

Carbon dioxide and climate

Fossil fuels

Coal, gas and oil: all from living things.

Burning

Releases CO₂, a greenhouse gas.

Heat trapped

Contributes to climate change.

5Cracking
Why cracking is needed

Supply and demand

Too many long chains; not enough short ones for petrol.

Products

More petrol, and alkenes for polymers.

Catalytic cracking

Thermal decomposition

C–C bonds break; new C=C forms.

Without catalyst

Higher temperature: thermal cracking.

Exam tipAlkane → shorter alkane + alkene.
Cracking equations

C₁₄H₃₀

→ C₁₀H₂₂ + C₄H₈ (alkane + alkene)

C₁₃H₂₈

→ C₂H₄ + C₃H₆ + C₈H₁₈: ethene and propene for polymers, octane for petrol.

Exam tipMolecules break fairly randomly, so many other splits happen.

✓ Say this

CO combines with haemoglobin, reducing oxygen transport. Fractions separated by boiling point. Cracking makes alkenes.

✗ Avoid this

Fractions are pure; CO makes you lack carbon; rain acid pH 7; no catalyst details.
