Reversible reactions and equilibria

Chemistry · Unit 3 · Physical chemistry · Last-minute notes · Book pages 240–247

Chapter in a nutshell

  • ⇌ means reversible
  • Dynamic equilibrium: equal rates, constant amounts
  • Pressure: side with fewer gas molecules
  • Temperature: endothermic when raised
  • Catalyst: no shift
1Reversible reactions
Reversible reactions
Reversible reaction

products can react to form the reactants again; shown with ⇌

Change conditions

Hot to cool can make the reaction go the other way.

Heat reversed too

If forward takes in heat, reverse gives out exactly the same amount.

Dehydrating copper(II) sulfate
Equation

CuSO₄·5H₂O(s) ⇌ CuSO₄(s) + 5H₂O(l)

Heating

Loses water of crystallisation: blue to white.

Adding water

White to blue, giving out heat.

Heating ammonium chloride
Equation

NH₄Cl(s) ⇌ NH₃(g) + HCl(g)

Observation

White crystals vanish at the hot end; recombine as white solid higher up where cooler.

2Dynamic equilibrium
Why sealed?

Sealed container

Nothing added, nothing escapes (heat can).

Example

CaCO₃(s) ⇌ CaO(s) + CO₂(g). Open container: CO₂ escapes, no reverse reaction.

Result

A reversible reaction in a sealed container reaches equilibrium.

Reaching equilibrium
Dynamic equilibrium

both reactions still happen at the same rate; concentrations stay constant

Common mistakeConstant, not equal.
3The position of equilibrium
Position of equilibrium

Position

How much of each substance is present at equilibrium.

Golden rule

If conditions change, the reaction counteracts the change.

Catalyst

No effect on position.

A ⇌ 2B

Raising T moves the position right: 90 % to 80 % A.

4Changing pressure
Pressure and gas molecules
EquilibriumGas molecules L : RHigher pressure
A + 2B ⇌ C + D3 : 2shifts right
N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃4 : 2more ammonia
H₂ + I₂ ⇌ 2HI2 : 2no effect

Increase pressure

Shifts to the side with fewer gas molecules.

Decrease pressure

Shifts to the side with more gas molecules.

5Changing temperature
Temperature and ΔH

ΔH given

Is for the forward reaction. Reverse has the same size, opposite sign.

Increase T

Shifts to the endothermic direction.

Decrease T

Shifts to the exothermic direction.

ΔH = −100

Lower T from 500 to 100 °C: more products.

Catalyst and equilibrium

Both faster

A catalyst speeds forward and reverse reactions by the same proportion.

No shift

So the position of equilibrium is unchanged.

Exam tipIt is added so equilibrium is reached faster.
6Worked examples
Nitrogen dioxide mixture
Equation

2NO₂(g) ⇌ N₂O₄(g), ΔH = −57

Colours

NO₂ dark brown; N₂O₄ colourless.

Hot

Endothermic reverse: more NO₂, darker.

Predicting shifts
  1. More pressure: 2 → 1 molecule, shifts right, paler (more N₂O₄).
  2. Lower temperature: shifts exothermic (forward), colour fades.
  3. Higher temperature: shifts endothermic (reverse), darkens.
Making ammonia
Equation

N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g), ΔH = −92 kJ/mol. Iron catalyst, 200 atm, 450 °C

iron catalystno changeequilibrium reached faster

✓ Say this

Rates equal, concentrations constant. Shift to the side with fewer gas molecules. Catalyst: no change in position.

✗ Avoid this

Concentrations equal; reactions stop at equilibrium; pressure shifts when moles equal.
