Reactivity series

Chemistry · Unit 2 · Inorganic chemistry · Last-minute notes · Book pages 145–159

Chapter in a nutshell

  • K, Na, Li, Ca, Mg, Al, Zn, Fe, Cu, Ag, Au
  • Water: hydroxide + H₂; steam: oxide + H₂
  • Acid: salt + hydrogen
  • More reactive displaces less reactive
  • Oxidation: loss of electrons; reduction: gain
  • Rusting needs water and oxygen
1The reactivity series
Order of reactivity

Above hydrogen

React with water, steam or acid to give hydrogen.

Carbon

Heating an oxide with carbon extracts metals below carbon, such as iron.

Below hydrogen

Cu, Ag, Au do not react with water, steam or dilute acid.

2Water and steam
Water and steam: the pattern
Cold water

metal + cold water → metal hydroxide + hydrogen

Steam

metal + steam → metal oxide + hydrogen

Copper

Does not react, so it is used for water pipes.

Suck-back

3Metals and dilute acids
Metals with dilute acid
  1. About 2 cm³ dilute HCl in four tubes.
  2. Add Mg, Zn, Fe, Cu.
  3. Test any gas with a lighted splint: squeaky pop is hydrogen.
  4. Repeat with dilute sulfuric acid.

Temperature rise

Same acid

50 cm³ of 1 mol/dm³ HCl, in excess.

Same moles

0.010 mol of each metal, not the same mass.

Insulate

Powders, polystyrene cup, record the maximum temperature.

Metal + acid → salt + hydrogen

Magnesium

Mg + H₂SO₄ → MgSO₄ + H₂. Vigorous, hot.

Aluminium

2Al + 6HCl → 2AlCl₃ + 3H₂. Slow start (oxide layer), then vigorous.

Iron

Fe + 2HCl → FeCl₂ + H₂. Iron(II) salts form.

Exam tipK, Na, Li and Ca are too reactive to add to acid safely. Cu, Ag, Au do not react.
4Displacement
Displacement reactions
Rule

a more reactive metal displaces a less reactive metal from its oxide or from a solution of its salt

Mg + CuO

Mg + CuO → MgO + Cu on heating.

Zn + CuSO₄

Blue fades; pink-brown copper forms.

Cu + AgNO₃

Silver forms; solution turns blue.

6Predicting: manganese
Will manganese react?

Position

Between aluminium and zinc; forms Mn²⁺; salts very pale pink.

With CuSO₄

Above copper, so it displaces it: Mn + CuSO₄ → MnSO₄ + Cu. Blue fades to pale pink.

With steam

Above hydrogen: Mn + H₂O(g) → MnO + H₂.

7Rusting
What makes iron rust?

Needs both

Oxygen and water.

Rust

Hydrated iron(III) oxide, Fe₂O₃·xH₂O. Redox: iron is oxidised.

Faster

Salty water speeds rusting.

Preventing rust

Barrier

Paint, oil, grease, plastic, tin. Cheap, but rust starts when broken.

Galvanising

Zinc coat: barrier, and zinc corrodes instead if scratched.

Sacrificial

Blocks of Zn or Mg attached; they lose electrons instead of iron.

✓ Say this

Rusting needs water and oxygen. Oxidation is loss of electrons. The oxidising agent is reduced.

✗ Avoid this

Rust needs only one of them; copper reacts with acid; oxidising agent is oxidised.
