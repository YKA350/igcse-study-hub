1The alkene family
What are alkenes?
Alkenes

a homologous series of hydrocarbons containing a carbon–carbon double bond; C=C is the functional group

Unsaturated

Contains a C=C double (or triple) bond.

Saturated

Only C–C single bonds: alkanes.

3Reactions of alkenes
Combustion and addition

Combustion

C₂H₄ + 3O₂ → 2CO₂ + 2H₂O. Too useful to waste as fuel.

Addition reaction

part of the double bond breaks, and the electrons join other atoms onto the two carbons

Ethene and bromine
Equation

CH₂=CH₂ + Br₂ → CH₂BrCH₂Br: colourless liquid; no heat, light or catalyst needed

Propene and bromine
Predicting products
Chlorine + propene
Step 1C=C opens
Step 2One Cl on each of C1 and C2
CH₂ClCHClCH₃: 1,2-dichloropropane

Rule

Halogen atoms add one to each carbon of the C=C.

Origin

Alkenes come from alkanes by cracking, and make polymers (Chapter 29).

5Other addition reactions
Adding water and hydrogen

Hydration (Chemistry only)

CH₂=CH₂ + H₂O → CH₃CH₂OH: steam, 300 °C, 60–70 atm, phosphoric acid catalyst.

Hydrogenation

CH₂=CH₂ + H₂ → CH₃CH₃. Used to make margarine from plant oils.

✓ Say this

Bromine water decolourised (orange to colourless). Unsaturated: contains C=C. Bromine atoms on different carbons. Every carbon has 4 bonds.

✗ Avoid this

Clear instead of colourless; alkenes need UV; both Br on one carbon.
