1Making an ester
What are esters?
Esters

formed when an alcohol reacts with a carboxylic acid; functional group –COO–

Reactants

Ethanoic acid and ethanol.

Product

Ethyl ethanoate and water.

Esterification
Equation

CH₃COOH + CH₃CH₂OH ⇌ CH₃COOCH₂CH₃ + H₂O

Conditions

A few drops of concentrated sulfuric acid; heat.

Reversible

Use pure ethanol and glacial ethanoic acid: water shifts it left.

Ethyl ethanoate

Condensation

Water forms when two molecules join.

Catalyst

Not written in the equation: not used up.

2Drawing and naming esters
Working out the ester
  1. Draw alcohol and acid with their –OH groups next to each other.
  2. Remove H₂O.
  3. Join the O to the C of the acid.
  4. Check: a C=O beside a C–O–C link.

3Working backwards
Splitting an ester
  1. Break between the C and the O of the C–O–C link.
  2. Add OH to one side, H to the other.
  3. Side with the C=O is the acid; the other is the alcohol.
Equation

CH₃COOCH₂CH₃ + H₂O ⇌ CH₃COOH + CH₃CH₂OH

4Properties and the practical
Properties and uses

Volatile

Turn to vapour easily: distinctive, sweet, fruity smells.

Uses

Food flavourings, perfumes; small esters are solvents.

Nature

Banana, raspberry and pear smells come partly from esters.

Making an ester
Why each step?

Catalyst and heat

Concentrated sulfuric acid is the catalyst. Use a water bath: contents are flammable.

Sodium carbonate

Neutralises leftover acid (fizzes), so its smell does not mask the ester.

Exam tipThe ester does not dissolve, so it floats. Waft the smell.

✓ Say this

Alcohol gives the first part of the name; acid the second. Water is the other product. Catalyst: concentrated sulfuric acid. Group is –COO–.

✗ Avoid this

Reversing the name; writing –COOH; forgetting water.
