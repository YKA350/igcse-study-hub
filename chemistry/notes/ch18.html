Chemical tests

Chemistry · Unit 2 · Inorganic chemistry · Last-minute notes · Book pages 190–197

Chapter in a nutshell

  • H₂ pop, O₂ relights, CO₂ milky
  • NH₃ turns damp red litmus blue; Cl₂ bleaches
  • Flames: Li red, Na yellow, K lilac, Ca orange-red, Cu blue-green
  • NaOH: Cu²⁺ blue, Fe²⁺ green, Fe³⁺ orange-brown
  • Halides white, cream, yellow; sulfate white
  • Anhydrous CuSO₄ turns blue with water

Why limewater goes milky
Equation

Ca(OH)₂(aq) + CO₂(g) → CaCO₃(s) + H₂O(l)

Milky

White precipitate of calcium carbonate.

Excess CO₂

The milkiness clears: soluble calcium hydrogencarbonate forms.

Exam tipAlways give the test and the result.
Details that earn marks

Hydrogen

Burns explosively with oxygen: 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O.

Chlorine

Green gas; blue litmus goes red, then white.

Ammonia

The only alkaline gas you meet. Never sniff Cl₂ or NH₃.

2Testing for water
Is water present?
Test

CuSO₄(s) + 5H₂O(l) → CuSO₄·5H₂O(s)

Limit

Shows water is present, not that it is pure.

Is the water pure?

Pure water

Freezes at exactly 0 °C; boils at exactly 100 °C at 1 atm.

Impure water

Freezes lower; boils higher.

Measure

Use a thermometer for freezing or boiling point.

4Tests for cations
Coloured precipitates

Method

Dissolve in distilled water; add about the same volume of dilute NaOH.

Look for

A precipitate: the solution goes cloudy. Test a solution, not a solid.

5Tests for anions
Precipitates to recognise

Halides

Dilute nitric acid, then silver nitrate.

Sulfate

Dilute HCl, then barium chloride.

Precipitate equations

Silver halides

Ag⁺(aq) + Cl⁻(aq) → AgCl(s). Same for Br⁻ and I⁻. They darken in light.

Barium sulfate

Ba²⁺(aq) + SO₄²⁻(aq) → BaSO₄(s)

Why add acid first?

Removes carbonate

Acid removes CO₃²⁻ and OH⁻, which also give precipitates.

Use nitric

HCl would add chloride ions.

Not sulfuric

It would add sulfate ions.

✓ Say this

Say precipitate, not only a colour. Fe³⁺ orange-brown, Fe²⁺ green. Anhydrous CuSO₄ white to blue.

✗ Avoid this

Squeaky pop without the lighted splint; chlorine turns litmus blue; ammonia bleaches.
