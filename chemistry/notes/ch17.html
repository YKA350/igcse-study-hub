What you see

With metals

Mg and acid: fizzing, squeaky pop, very warm. Zn slower. Never Na or K.

With oxides

Black CuO + hot acid gives blue solution. Most oxides need heating.

With carbonates

Fizzing; gas turns limewater milky.

3Proton transfer
Acid: donor, base: acceptor
Acid

a proton (H⁺) donor

Base

a proton (H⁺) acceptor

Proton

H⁺ is a hydrogen atom without its electron: a proton.

Bases

Metal oxides, hydroxides and ammonia. An alkali is a soluble base.

Proton transfer equations

HCl in water

H₂O + HCl → H₃O⁺ + Cl⁻. HCl donates a proton to water: HCl acid, water base.

HCl and ammonia

NH₃ + HCl → NH₄⁺ + Cl⁻. HCl is the acid; NH₃ the base. Gases give white smoke of NH₄Cl.

Exam tipWater can be a base or an acid, yet is neutral.

5Copper(II) sulfate crystals
Making CuSO₄ crystals
Equation

CuO(s) + H₂SO₄(aq) → CuSO₄(aq) + H₂O(l)

Crystals

Hydrated CuSO₄·5H₂O; black powder gives blue solution.

Why each step?

Excess oxide

Shows all the acid is neutralised.

Filter

Removes unreacted solid.

Saturated

Crystals form on a cold glass rod dipped in.

Do not dry out

Evaporating to dryness ruins hydrated crystals.

Do you need to heat?

Cold is fine

Carbonates and magnesium react with dilute acid in the cold.

Heat needed

Most other metals and oxides. Use a water bath; wear eye protection.

6Na, K and NH₄ salts
Why use a titration?

Problem

Na, K and NH₄ compounds are all soluble: excess solid would dissolve and cannot be filtered.

Solution

Titrate to find the exact acid volume that neutralises the alkali.

Exam tipRepeat without indicator, then crystallise.
Sodium sulfate crystals
  1. Pipette 25 cm³ NaOH into a flask; add methyl orange.
  2. Add H₂SO₄ from a burette until yellow turns orange.
  3. Note the volume. Mix the same volumes without indicator.
  4. Heat to saturation, cool to crystallise.
  5. Filter and dry the crystals.
Equation

2NaOH + H₂SO₄ → Na₂SO₄ + 2H₂O

Others

NaCl with HCl. (NH₄)₂SO₄: crystallise slowly, do not overheat.

7Precipitation
Making an insoluble salt
Precipitate

a solid formed by a reaction in solution

Silver chloride

AgNO₃ + NaCl → AgCl(s) + NaNO₃. White precipitate.

Ionic

Ag⁺ + Cl⁻ → AgCl(s); Na⁺ and NO₃⁻ are spectators.

What to mix

The metal's nitrate with the sodium or potassium salt of the other ion.

Examples

PbI₂ yellow; BaSO₄ white.

Pure dry PbSO₄
Equation

Pb(NO₃)₂ + Na₂SO₄ → PbSO₄(s) + 2NaNO₃

Safety and washing

Tap water leaves impurities. Lead is toxic: avoid skin contact.

Is the solid in excess?

Carbonate or metal

Bubbles stop.

Oxide

Solid stays undissolved after stirring and warming.

Then

Remove the excess by filtration.

✓ Say this

Add solid in excess, filter. Wash precipitate with distilled water. Alkali is a soluble base.

✗ Avoid this

Evaporating to dryness; unwashed precipitate; every base is an alkali.
