The alkali metals

Chemistry · Unit 2 · Inorganic chemistry · Last-minute notes · Book pages 123–129

Chapter in a nutshell

  • One outer electron, form M⁺
  • Soft, low density, low melting points
  • 2M + 2H₂O → 2MOH + H₂
  • Flames: Li red, Na yellow, K lilac
  • Reactivity increases down the group
1The Group 1 family
The alkali metals

Members

Lithium, sodium, potassium, rubidium, caesium, francium.

Why one family

One outer electron and similar chemical properties.

Melting points fall down the group

Low for metals

Very low melting and boiling points, falling down the group.

Density

Generally rises, but K is less dense than Na.

Float

Li, Na and K are less dense than water.

Soft and shiny

Soft

Cut easily with a knife; softer down the group.

Tarnish

Shiny silver when cut, tarnishing quickly in air.

2Storage and structure
Storage and handling

Under oil

Li, Na and K are kept under oil to stop reaction with air and water vapour.

Sealed tubes

Rb and Cs are so reactive they are sealed in glass.

No bare fingers

Sweat reacts, forming heat and corrosive hydroxide.

3With water
Metal + water: the general reaction
Word equation

alkali metal + water → alkali metal hydroxide + hydrogen

General

2M + 2H₂O → 2MOH + H₂

Sodium

2Na(s) + 2H₂O(l) → 2NaOH(aq) + H₂(g)

Exam tipThe solution turns universal indicator blue: it is alkaline, containing OH⁻ ions.
Sodium in water

Floats

Less dense than water.

Melts to a ball

Low melting point; heat.

Fizzes and darts

Hydrogen gas pushes it about.

Disappears

The sodium is used up.

5Explaining the trend
Bigger atoms, weaker attraction

More shells

Atoms get bigger down the group.

Further away

Outer electron is further from the nucleus.

Easier to lose

Weaker attraction, so Na → Na⁺ + e⁻ is easier.
