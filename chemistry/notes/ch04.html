The Periodic Table

Chemistry · Unit 1 · Principles of chemistry · Last-minute notes · Book pages 30–37

Chapter in a nutshell

  • Order of atomic number; groups are columns, periods are rows
  • Shells fill 2, 8, 8, then the fourth shell
  • Group number = outer electrons
  • Period number = number of shells
  • Same group, same outer electrons, similar reactions
  • Noble gases: full outer shell, unreactive
  • Metals conduct and have basic oxides
  • Non-metals do not conduct and have acidic oxides

How the table is arranged
Key term · atomic number

The number of protons in the nucleus. Elements are arranged in order of atomic number.

Mendeleev

The modern table was first presented in 1869 by the Russian chemist Dmitri Mendeleev.

Groups and periods

Vertical columns are groups (1 to 7, then 0). Horizontal rows are periods. Period 1 is just hydrogen and helium.

Named groups

Group 1 = alkali metals
Group 7 = halogens
Group 0 = noble gases

RememberMetals are on the left, non-metals on the right. Transition metals sit in the middle.
Exam tipHydrogen is not in Group 1: it is a non-metal.
Two numbers on each symbol

Atomic number

Always the smaller number. It is the number of protons.

The other number

The mass number of the commonest isotope, or the relative atomic mass.

Lanthanoids and actinoids

Written separately at the bottom so the table can be drawn bigger.

2Electronic configurations
Filling the shells
Key idea

Electrons move around the nucleus in energy levels (shells). The lowest levels, closest to the nucleus, fill first.

1st shellholds 2
→
2nd shellholds 8
→
3rd shellholds 8
Common mistakeThe circles show energy levels, not paths. The electrons do not orbit along them.
  1. Look up the atomic number: chlorine is 17, so 17 electrons.
  2. Fill from the inside: 2, then 8, then 7.
  3. Write 2, 8, 7.
  4. Check: 2 + 8 + 7 = 17.
Exam tipAlways check that the electrons add up to the atomic number.
Drawing electron shells
RememberCircles are shells. Dots or crosses are electrons.
Exam tipElectrons can be drawn singly or in pairs.
The fourth shell

After argon

The third shell is full (8), so potassium’s last electron goes into the fourth shell.

Common mistakeCalcium is 2, 8, 8, 2, not 2, 8, 10.

3Position and electrons
Two important facts

Group = outer electrons

Elements in a group have the same number of outer electrons. For Groups 1 to 7, outer electrons = group number.

Period = shells

The period number is the number of occupied shells. Calcium (2, 8, 8, 2) is Period 4.

Exam tipBarium (Group 2) has 2 outer electrons, iodine (Group 7) has 7.
Which element is 2, 8, 5?
An atom has the electronic configuration 2, 8, 5. Name the element.
Add up2 + 8 + 5 = 15, so the atomic number is 15.
Outer electrons5 outer electrons: Group 5.
Shells3 shells: Period 3.
Phosphorus
Similar chemical properties

Group 1

React vigorously with water, making hydrogen and hydroxides: LiOH, NaOH, KOH.

Group 7

React with hydrogen: HF, HCl, HBr.

Group 2

Form chlorides: MgCl₂, CaCl₂.

Why?

Reactions depend on the outer electrons, the ones used in bonding.

RememberSame group → same outer electrons → similar reactions.
4The noble gases
Full outer shells

Unreactive

Outer shell is full: no tendency to lose, gain or share electrons.

Why Group 0?

Once thought to have zero combining power.

Remember8 outer electrons (helium 2).
5Metals and non-metals
Where are they?

Left and right

Metals are on the left of the table. Non-metals are on the right.

The dividing line

Near it, elements such as arsenic have properties of both.

Common mistakeHydrogen is not in Group 1: it is a non-metal.
