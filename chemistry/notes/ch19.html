1Exothermic and endothermic
Heat out or heat in

Exothermic

Gives out heat to the surroundings. Products have less energy; temperature goes up.

Endothermic

Takes in heat from the surroundings. Products have more energy; temperature goes down.

Exam tipAny reaction with a flame is exothermic.
Examples of each
Exothermic: temperature upEndothermic: temperature down
Combustion: 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂OThermal decomposition of carbonates
Metals + acidsZnCO₃ → ZnO + CO₂ (heat all the time)
NeutralisationDissolving NH₄Cl or NH₄NO₃
Thermite: 2Al + Fe₂O₃ → 2Fe + Al₂O₃Baking soda + vinegar; cold packs
CaO + H₂O → Ca(OH)₂Heating does not always mean endothermic
Take care

Thermite

So much heat it welds railway lines.

Heating a reaction

Sometimes just to make it faster, not because it is endothermic.

2Enthalpy change, ΔH
What ΔH means
Enthalpy change, ΔH

heat energy taken in or given out in a reaction: energy of products minus energy of reactants

Units

kJ/mol

Exothermic

ΔH negative

Endothermic

ΔH positive

Reading a ΔH value

Mg + H₂SO₄ → MgSO₄ + H₂

ΔH = −466.9 kJ/mol: 466.9 kJ given out per mole of Mg.

CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂

ΔH = +178 kJ/mol: 178 kJ absorbed per mole decomposed.

4Q = mcΔT
Specific heat capacity
Specific heat capacity

heat needed to raise the temperature of 1 g of a substance by 1 °C. Water: 4.18 J/g/°C

Q = m × c × ΔT

Q in J, m in g (mass of water or solution), c = 4.18, ΔT in °C

Check

2 °C on 1 g of water needs 4.18 × 2 = 8.36 J.

Units

1 cm³ of dilute solution is about 1 g. Divide J by 1000 for kJ.

Molar enthalpy change
ΔH = Q ÷ n

Q in kJ, n = moles reacted. Add − if temperature rose, + if it fell

5Burning an alcohol
Copper can calorimeter
  1. 100 cm³ water in the can; note T.
  2. Weigh the burner with lid.
  3. Shield from draughts; burn until T rises about 40 °C.
  4. Stir; record maximum T.
  5. Reweigh the burner.
Ethanol worked example
Calculate ΔH
ΔT62.8 − 21.5 = 41.3 °C
Q100 × 4.18 × 41.3 = 17.26 kJ
Ethanol burned137.36 − 136.58 = 0.78 g
n0.78 ÷ 46 = 0.01696 mol
ΔH17.26 ÷ 0.01696 = 1020

Data-book

−1370 kJ/mol: our answer is too small.

Why the answer is too small

Heat loss

Warm water, flame and can lose heat to the air.

Heats the can

Copper can and thermometer also take heat.

Incomplete combustion

Yellow flame and soot: less heat released.

Exam tipA bomb calorimeter burns fuel in pure oxygen for better values.
Longer alcohols release more heat

Pattern

Each extra CH₂ adds about 650 kJ/mol more exothermic.

Why

More bonds to make as products form.

7Bond energies
Breaking and making bonds

Breaking bonds

Needs energy: endothermic.

Making bonds

Releases energy: exothermic.

Bond energy

energy to break 1 mole of covalent bonds in gaseous molecules, kJ/mol; the same is released when they form

✓ Say this

Exothermic: ΔH negative, always give the sign. Convert J to kJ. m is the mass of water heated. Breaking bonds is endothermic.

✗ Avoid this

m as mass of fuel; J not kJ; no sign on ΔH; bond making endothermic.
