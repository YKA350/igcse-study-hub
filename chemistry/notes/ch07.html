Ionic bonding

Chemistry · Unit 1 · Principles of chemistry · Last-minute notes · Book pages 75–84

Chapter in a nutshell

  • Ion: charged particle; cation +, anion −
  • Ionic bonding: electrostatic attraction between ions
  • Groups 1, 2, 3 form 1+, 2+, 3+; Groups 5, 6, 7 form 3−, 2−, 1−
  • Formulae: total positive = total negative
  • Brackets for compound ions
  • Giant lattice: high melting point, crystalline, brittle
  • Conduct only when molten or dissolved
  • Never say molecules for ionic compounds
1How ions form
Ions and ionic bonding
Ion

A charged particle formed when an atom (or group of atoms) loses or gains electrons.

Ionic bonding

The strong electrostatic attraction between positive and negative ions.

Cation: positive ion

Formed when an atom loses electrons. Example: Na⁺.

Anion: negative ion

Formed when an atom gains electrons. Example: Cl⁻.

Dot-and-cross: NaCl
RememberDots and crosses are identical electrons. They only show which atom each came from.
Exam tipDraw square brackets and the charge on each ion.
Dot-and-cross: MgO
RememberTwo electrons move from magnesium to oxygen: Mg²⁺ and O²⁻.
Common mistakeDon’t forget the charges.
Dot-and-cross: LiF

Calcium chloride

Ca has 2 outer electrons but each Cl takes only one, so two Cl are needed: Ca²⁺ and 2Cl⁻, CaCl₂.

2Noble gas configurations
Getting a full outer shell

Noble gas structure

For the first 20 elements, atoms lose or gain electrons to get 2, 8 (like neon) or 2, 8, 8 (like argon).

Groups 1, 2, 3 lose

They form 1+, 2+, 3+ ions.

Groups 5, 6, 7 gain

They form 3−, 2−, 1− ions.

Isoelectronic

Having the same number of electrons. Na⁺ (2, 8) is isoelectronic with neon.

Larger ions

Rb⁺ has the same electrons as krypton; I⁻ the same as xenon.

Common mistakeFe²⁺, Fe³⁺, Cu²⁺, Zn²⁺, Ag⁺ and Pb²⁺ do not have noble gas structures.

When the name tells you

Roman numerals

Give the charge: lead(II) oxide has Pb²⁺; iron(III) chloride has Fe³⁺; copper(II) sulfate has Cu²⁺.

-ide or -ate?

-ide: just the two elements, copper(II) sulfide is CuS. -ate: oxygen too, copper(II) sulfate is CuSO₄.

RememberAmmonium chloride, NH₄Cl, is ionic but has no metal.

5Giant ionic lattices
The sodium chloride lattice
Giant ionic lattice

A regular array of ions held by strong electrostatic attractions in all directions. In NaCl each ion has 6 oppositely charged neighbours.

Common mistakeNever talk about molecules of an ionic compound.
High melting points

Why high?

A lot of energy is needed to break the strong electrostatic attractions between oppositely charged ions.

Crystalline and soluble

Regular arrangement of ions. Tend to dissolve in water but not in ethanol or hexane. MgO is an exception: insoluble in water.

CompoundIonsAttractionm.p.
NaCl1+ and 1−weaker801 °C

Exam tipHigher charges mean stronger attraction and a higher melting point.
Brittle crystals

Brittle

A small shift puts like charges side by side. They repel and the crystal splits.

Conduction

Solid: no (ions fixed). Molten or dissolved: yes, the ions move.

Common mistakeNever say electrons move. It is the ions.
