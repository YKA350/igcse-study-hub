1Minerals and ores
Minerals, ores and native metals

Mineral

A compound of a metal found in the Earth's crust.

Ore

A rock with enough mineral to be worth extracting.

Native

Unreactive metals such as gold occur uncombined; sometimes Ag and Cu.

Roasting and reduction
Roasting a sulfide

2ZnS(s) + 3O₂(g) → 2ZnO(s) + 2SO₂(g)

Why

Many ores are oxides or are converted to oxides first.

Extraction is reduction

Remove oxygen, or add electrons to metal ions.

Costs

Energy and the reducing agent: manufacturers want the cheapest method.

2Extraction and the series
Extraction method and reactivity

Above carbon

Cannot be reduced by carbon: electrolysis (needs lots of electricity, costly).

Below carbon

Heat the oxide with carbon: cheap.

Iron: reduction with carbon
Equation

Fe₂O₃(s) + 3C(s) → 2Fe(l) + 3CO(g)

Redox

Fe₂O₃ is reduced; carbon is oxidised: the reducing agent.

In the blast furnace

Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂: carbon monoxide is the main reducing agent.

Exam tipNo blast furnace detail needed.
Metals above carbon

Electrolysis

Used for metals above zinc; very costly in electricity.

Other metals

Titanium: heat its compound with a more reactive metal. Also costly.

3Extracting aluminium
Electrolysis cell

Cathode: reduction

Al³⁺ + 3e⁻ → Al

Anode: oxidation

2O²⁻ → O₂ + 4e⁻

Cryolite

Al₂O₃ melts above 2000 °C; in cryolite about 1000 °C, saving energy.

Aluminium: cost and exam skill

Why expensive

More abundant than iron but electrolysis is costly.

Exam skill

You must comment on an extraction when given information; no detail to learn.

Why alloys are harder

Pure metal

Regular layers of ions slide easily.

Alloy

Different-sized atoms stop layers sliding.

5Uses of aluminium and copper
Uses of aluminium

Alloyed

Pure Al is weak; alloys with Si, Cu or Mg are used.

Uses

Aircraft, overhead cables (steel core), pots and pans.

Properties

Low density, strong when alloyed, conducts heat and electricity.

Corrosion

Thin, strong aluminium oxide layer protects it.
