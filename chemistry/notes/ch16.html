Acids, alkalis and titrations

Chemistry · Unit 2 · Inorganic chemistry · Last-minute notes · Book pages 167–172

Chapter in a nutshell

  • pH 0–3 strong acid, 7 neutral, 11–14 strong alkali
  • Litmus red/blue; methyl orange red/yellow
  • Acids give H⁺; alkalis give OH⁻
  • H⁺ + OH⁻ → H₂O
  • Titration: rough, then dropwise; read to 2 d.p.

Dissociation

pH

Measures the concentration of H⁺ ions.

Bases and alkalis
Base

neutralises acids: metal oxides, hydroxides and ammonia

Alkali

a base that dissolves in water, a source of OH⁻ ions

Examples

NaOH, KOH, all Group 1 hydroxides, ammonia solution; soluble carbonates weakly.

Ammonia

NH₃(aq) + H₂O(l) ⇌ NH₄⁺(aq) + OH⁻(aq)

4Neutralisation
Acid + base or alkali

Acid + base

CuO + H₂SO₄ → CuSO₄ + H₂O. H⁺ combines with O²⁻ to give water.

Acid + alkali

NaOH + HCl → NaCl + H₂O: salt + water.

Ionic equation

H⁺(aq) + OH⁻(aq) → H₂O(l). Every acid–alkali neutralisation is this; Na⁺ and Cl⁻ are spectators.

5Titration
Titration apparatus

Pipette

Measures 25.0 cm³ into the flask.

White tile

Makes the colour change easy to see.

Drop by drop

Near the end-point add dropwise.

Carrying out a titration
  1. Pipette 25 cm³ of hydrochloric acid into a conical flask.
  2. Add a few drops of phenolphthalein.
  3. Fill the burette with sodium hydroxide; read the start.
  4. Add alkali, swirling, to a permanent pale pink.
  5. Read the end; volume = final − initial.
Getting a precise result

Rough first

Do a rough titration, e.g. 22.50 cm³.

Then accurate

Run in about 20 cm³, then add dropwise; one drop gives the end-point.

Exam tipAlkali is added to the acid: easier to see colourless turn pink than pink fade.

Rules

Numbers rise down the burette. Record to the nearest 0.05 cm³: 2 d.p.

Indicator

Methyl orange works; universal indicator does not: no sharp end-point.

The end-point
End-point

where the indicator changes colour

Why drop by drop

Near the end-point pH changes very rapidly: one drop can take it from about 3 to 11.

✓ Say this

Acids give H⁺; alkalis give OH⁻. pH 7 neutral. Burette to 2 d.p.

✗ Avoid this

'Clear' for colourless; below 7 alkaline; 1 d.p. burette readings.
