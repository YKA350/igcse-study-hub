1Organic compounds and hydrocarbons
What is organic chemistry?
Organic chemistry

the study of carbon compounds, except the simplest: CO₂, CO, carbonates, hydrogencarbonates

Hydrocarbon

Hydrogen and carbon only.

Saturated

Only C–C single bonds.

Unsaturated

Contains C=C (or triple) bonds.

Two hydrocarbons

Only H and C

Nothing else may be present.

Exam tipAlmost all organic compounds contain C and H; a hydrocarbon has nothing else.

Formula facts

Empirical

A molecular formula is a multiple of it: CH₂ could be C₂H₄, C₃H₆, C₄H₈.

Twisting

Bending a chain does not make a new molecule.

Combustion equation

2C₄H₁₀ + 13O₂ → 8CO₂ + 10H₂O: molecular formulae are used when structure does not matter

3Homologous series and functional groups
Two key ideas
Homologous series

compounds with similar chemical properties because they share a functional group; each member differs by –CH₂–

Functional group

an atom or group of atoms that decides the chemical properties

Numbers in names

Rule

Number from the end that gives the smaller numbers.

but-1-ene

CH₂=CHCH₂CH₃: double bond starts at carbon 1.

but-2-ene

CH₃CH=CHCH₃: starts at carbon 2.

Longest chain

Base the name on the longest carbon chain.

Two methyls (di), both on carbon 2.

2,3-dimethylbut-2-ene

5Structural isomers
What are isomers?
Structural isomers

same molecular formula, different structural formulae; you cannot convert one to another by bending

Not isotopes

Isotopes: same atomic number, different mass number.

Tip

Draw the longest chain horizontally to avoid counting an isomer twice.

More combustion

Butene

C₄H₈ + 6O₂ → 4CO₂ + 4H₂O

Ethanol

C₂H₅OH + 3O₂ → 2CO₂ + 3H₂O. Count the O in the alcohol when balancing.

✓ Say this

Every carbon has four bonds. Hydrocarbon: H and C only. Longest chain gives the name. Combustion in excess oxygen: CO₂ and water.

✗ Avoid this

Isomers vs isotopes; five bonds on carbon; addition with two products.
