  • Balance with big numbers; never change a formula

1Writing and balancing equations
What the numbers mean

Big number in front

A coefficient multiplies the whole formula. 2H₂SO₄ has 4 H, 2 S and 8 O.

Small number (subscript)

Applies only to the atom just before it. The 3 in CaCO₃ means 3 oxygens.

Number after a bracket

Applies to everything inside. Ca(OH)₂ has 1 Ca, 2 O and 2 H.

2Cl or Cl₂?

2Cl is two separate chlorine atoms. Cl₂ is two atoms joined in one molecule.

How to balance an equation
  1. Work across the equation from left to right, one element at a time.
  2. Leave an element that appears in several places until the end.
  3. Count an unchanged group, like SO₄, as a whole.
  4. Only write big numbers in front of formulae.
  5. Check everything again at the end.
Common mistakeNever change a formula to balance. H₂Cl does not exist.
Exam tipCount the atoms on each side. They must match.
Zinc and hydrochloric acid
  1. Zn + HCl → ZnCl₂ + H₂
  2. Zinc: 1 on each side.
  3. Hydrogen: 1 left, 2 right, so write 2HCl.

2Relative atomic and formula mass
Aᵣ and Mᵣ
Relative atomic mass, Aᵣ

The weighted average mass of the isotopes of the element, on a scale on which a carbon-12 atom has a mass of exactly 12.

Relative formula mass, Mᵣ

The sum of the Aᵣ values of all the atoms in the formula. Also called relative molecular mass for covalent molecules.

No units

Relative masses have no units.

Use whole numbers

Except Cl = 35.5 and Cu = 63.5.

Exam tipSay “relative formula mass” for everything. NaCl is ionic, so it has no molecules.

3The mole
What is a mole?
Key term · mole (mol)

The unit for the amount of a substance. The mass of 1 mole is the Mᵣ (or Aᵣ) in grams.

Water

1 mol = 18 g

Sulfur

1 mol = 32 g

MgO

1 mol = 40 g

Extension

1 mole of anything has about 6 × 10²³ particles: the Avogadro constant.

Common mistake“1 mole of oxygen” could be O atoms (16 g) or O₂ molecules (32 g). Quote the formula.
The mole triangle
moles = mass ÷ mass of 1 mole
n = m ÷ Mᵣ
mass = moles × mass of 1 mole
m = n × Mᵣ

5Practicals: finding formulae
Formula of magnesium oxide (combustion)
  1. Weigh a crucible with its lid.
  2. Add 10 cm of Mg ribbon and reweigh.
  3. Heat strongly, lifting the lid every few seconds.
  4. Cool, then weigh.
  5. Reheat and reweigh to constant mass.

Why the lid?

It stops MgO escaping. Lifting it lets oxygen in.

Reading the equation

CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O: 16 g of CH₄ (1 mol) reacts with 64 g of O₂ (2 mol) to give 44 g of CO₂ and 36 g of H₂O.

Step 3moles to mass
  1. Work out the moles from the mass you are given.
  2. Use the equation to find the moles of the substance you want.
  3. Convert those moles to a mass.
Exam tipThe ratio works in any units, grams or tonnes.

7Percentage yield and excess
Actual compared with theoretical
% yield = actual yield ÷ theoretical yield × 100
% yield = actual ÷ theoretical × 100
Theoretical yield

The most product possible, from a moles calculation. Actual yield is what the experiment gives.

Copper(II) sulfate crystals
2.40 g of CuO with hot sulfuric acid gave 5.21 g of CuSO₄·5H₂O.
Moles CuO2.40 ÷ 79.5 = 0.0302 mol
Ratio1 : 1, so 0.0302 mol crystals
Theoretical0.0302 × 249.5 = 7.53 g
% yield5.21 ÷ 7.53 × 100
69.2%

Why under 100%?

Spillages, losses when moving liquids, and side reactions.

Common mistakeA yield over 100% means a mistake, for example the salt was not dry.
