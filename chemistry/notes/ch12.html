The halogens

Chemistry · Unit 2 · Inorganic chemistry · Last-minute notes · Book pages 130–136

Chapter in a nutshell

  • 7 outer electrons, diatomic, form X⁻
  • F₂ yellow, Cl₂ green, Br₂ red-brown, I₂ grey
  • Boiling points rise down the group
  • Reactivity falls: Cl₂ displaces Br and I
  • Displacement is redox

What they have in common

7 outer electrons

So they react in a similar way and form X⁻ ions.

Diatomic non-metals

F₂, Cl₂, Br₂, I₂: poor conductors of heat and electricity.

Halogen

means salt-producing: with metals they form salts such as NaCl and KI

2Trends down the group
Boiling points rise down the group

Covalent molecules

Small molecules held by intermolecular forces.

Heavier molecules

Stronger forces, so more energy to separate them.

Exam tipNo covalent bonds break on boiling.
Handling halogens

Fluorine

Too dangerously reactive for school labs.

Poisonous vapours

Use a fume cupboard.

Liquid bromine

Corrosive: keep off skin.

Rule

a more reactive halogen displaces a less reactive one from its salt solution

Displacement equations

Chlorine + potassium bromide

2KBr(aq) + Cl₂(aq) → 2KCl(aq) + Br₂(aq): colourless turns orange.

Chlorine + potassium iodide

2KI(aq) + Cl₂(aq) → 2KCl(aq) + I₂(aq): brown.

Bromine + potassium iodide

2KI(aq) + Br₂(aq) → 2KBr(aq) + I₂(aq): brown.

Exam tipWrite 'chlorine displaces bromine', not 'bromide'.

5Ionic equations and redox
Ionic equation
Ionic equation

2Br⁻(aq) + Cl₂(aq) → 2Cl⁻(aq) + Br₂(aq)

Spectator ions

K⁺ does not change, so it is left out.

Observation

Colourless solution turns orange.

Why it is redox

Oxidation

Bromide ions lose electrons: oxidised.

Reduction

Chlorine gains electrons: reduced to Cl⁻.

Oxidising agent

takes electrons from another species: Cl₂ here

Exam tipBr₂ + 2I⁻ → 2Br⁻ + I₂: bromine is the oxidising agent.
