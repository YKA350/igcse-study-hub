1Gas volumes
Two key ideas
Avogadro’s law

Equal volumes of gases at the same temperature and pressure contain equal numbers of molecules.

Molar volume

The volume of 1 mole of any gas: about 24 dm³ (24 000 cm³) at room temperature and pressure, rtp (20–25 °C, 1 atmosphere).

Volume ratio

For gases, the mole ratio in an equation is also the volume ratio. CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O: 100 cm³ of CH₄ reacts with 200 cm³ of O₂.

Units

1 dm³ = 1000 cm³. cm³ → dm³: ÷ 1000.

Same volume, same number
RememberEqual volumes of gases at the same temperature and pressure hold equal numbers of molecules.
Exam tipUse 24 for dm³ and 24 000 for cm³.

CaCO₃(s) + 2HCl(aq) → CaCl₂(aq) + CO₂(g) + H₂O(l). Volume of CO₂ from 1.00 g CaCO₃?

3Concentration of solutions
Units of concentration
Concentration

The amount of solute in 1 dm³ of solution. It can be in g/dm³ or in mol/dm³.

Molar

2 mol/dm³ can be written 2 M (“2 molar”). Also written mol dm⁻³.

The solution triangle
moles = concentration × volume
n = c × V
concentration = moles ÷ volume
c = n ÷ V
Common mistakeVolume must be in dm³. cm³ ÷ 1000.
RememberConcentration in mol/dm³, volume in dm³, moles in mol.

4Titration calculations
Acid–alkali titration
  1. Measure the alkali with a pipette into a conical flask.
  2. Add a few drops of indicator.
  3. Run acid from the burette until the indicator just changes colour.
  4. Add drop by drop near the end.

White tile

Shows the colour change clearly.

The standard calculation
25.00 cm³ of 0.100 mol/dm³ NaOH needs 23.50 cm³ of HCl. NaOH + HCl → NaCl + H₂O
Moles NaOH25.00 ÷ 1000 × 0.100 = 0.00250 mol
Ratio1 : 1, so 0.00250 mol HCl in 0.02350 dm³
Concentration0.00250 ÷ 0.02350
0.106 mol/dm³ HCl

Steps

Moles of the one you know, ratio, moles of the other, then concentration.
