Covalent bonding

Chemistry · Unit 1 · Principles of chemistry · Last-minute notes · Book pages 85–97

Chapter in a nutshell

  • Covalent bond = shared pair of electrons
  • Learn H₂, Cl₂, HCl, CH₄, NH₃, H₂O, O₂, N₂, CO₂, C₂H₆, C₂H₄
  • Simple molecular: weak forces, low m.p., no conduction
  • Boiling points rise with Mᵣ
  • Giant covalent: many strong bonds, very high m.p.
  • Diamond hard and non-conducting
  • Graphite soft and conducting
  • C₆₀ is simple molecular
1What a covalent bond is
The shared pair
Covalent bond

A pair of electrons shared between two atoms. It is the strong electrostatic attraction between the nuclei and the shared pair.

RememberDots and crosses only show which atom each electron came from.

Full outer shells

Atoms share so H has 2 outer electrons and other atoms have 8 (the octet rule).

More stable

Making a bond releases energy, so H₂ is more stable than two separate H atoms.

Common mistakeH does not become helium: its protons are unchanged.
H₂, Cl₂ and HCl
RememberOne shared pair = a single covalent bond.
Exam tipIn HCl: Cl has 2, 8, 8 like argon and H has 2 like helium.
2Dot-and-cross diagrams
CH₄, NH₃ and H₂O
RememberC shares 4 electrons, N shares 3, O shares 2. Each ends with 8.
Exam tipWater has 2 bonding pairs and 2 lone pairs, so it is bent (V-shaped).
Double and triple bonds

O₂

Each O shares 2 electrons: a double bond (2 pairs).

N₂

Each N shares 3: a triple bond. Very strong, so nitrogen is unreactive.

CO₂ and C₂H₄

CO₂ has two C=O double bonds. Ethene has C=C and four C–H.

3Simple molecular structures
Strong inside, weak between

Properties

Low melting and boiling points: weak intermolecular forces.

Covalent bonds stay

Melting or boiling does not break the covalent bonds.

No conduction

No ions, and electrons are held in atoms or bonds. Often insoluble in water.

4Giant covalent structures
A continuous network
Giant covalent structure

Atoms joined by strong covalent bonds in a continuous network. It is not a molecule.

Examples

Diamond, graphite and silicon dioxide (SiO₂).

High melting points

Solids with very high m.p. and b.p.: a lot of energy is needed to break all the strong bonds.

Allotropes

Diamond, graphite and C₆₀ are different forms of the same element, carbon.

Diamond

Structure

Each C bonded to 4 others in a tetrahedral arrangement.

Very hard, very high m.p.

Strong C–C bonds throughout must be broken. Used on drill bits.

No conduction

All outer electrons are held in covalent bonds.

Graphite

Soft and slippery

Each C bonded to 3 in layers. Weak forces between layers, so they slide: pencils and lubricant.

Conducts

The fourth outer electron is delocalised and moves through the layer. Used for electrodes.

High m.p.

Strong covalent bonds must be broken.

C₆₀ fullerene

Simple molecular

Molecules of 60 carbon atoms with weak forces between them.

Lower m.p., softer

Only weak intermolecular forces are broken.

No conduction

Electrons move within a molecule but cannot jump to the next.
