Practical: oxygen in air

Copper, iron and phosphorus.

Copper

Oxygen with hot copper

Equation

2Cu(s) + O₂(g) → 2CuO(s)

Observation

Pink-brown copper turns black.

Exam tipCopper must be in excess.
Method

Copper method

  1. Set the syringes to hold 100 cm³ of air.
  2. Heat the tube of copper strongly.
  3. Pass the air back and forth over the copper.
  4. Move the Bunsen to fresh copper; continue until the volume is steady.
  5. Cool to room temperature before reading.
Common mistakeHot gas has expanded: always cool before reading.
Calculation

Worked example: copper

Percentage oxygen
Start100 cm³ air
End79 cm³ left
Used100 − 79 = 21 cm³
Percentage21 ÷ 100 × 100
21 % oxygen

Gas left

Mostly nitrogen: it puts out a lighted splint.

Common mistakeToo little copper: not all O₂ removed, answer too low.
Iron

Using rusting iron

  1. Find the air volume in flask and tube using water.
  2. Wet iron filings in the flask, syringe attached.
  3. Record the start reading.
  4. Leave about a week until steady; record the end.
Iron result
Start130 + 12 + 92 = 234 cm³
End130 + 12 + 43 = 185 cm³
Used234 − 185 = 49 cm³
49 ÷ 234 × 100 = 21 %

If the answer is below 21 %

Not left long enough, or not enough iron. The metal must be in excess.

Equations

Combustion equations

Magnesium

2Mg(s) + O₂(g) → 2MgO(s). Then MgO + H₂O → Mg(OH)₂ (alkaline).

Sulfur

S(s) + O₂(g) → SO₂(g). Then SO₂ + H₂O → H₂SO₃ (sulfurous acid).

Hydrogen

2H₂(g) + O₂(g) → 2H₂O(l). Explodes: squeaky pop.

Properties of oxides

Metal oxides

Ionic, contain O²⁻, usually basic: react with acids to form salts. Soluble ones give alkaline solutions.

Non-metal oxides

SO₂ and CO₂ are usually acidic.

Climate change

Carbon dioxide as a greenhouse gas.

Greenhouse

The greenhouse effect

In

UV and visible light pass through and warm the surface.

Out

Warm Earth gives out infrared; CO₂ absorbs it and emits in all directions, warming the air.

Climate

Rising CO₂

Cause

Fossil fuels and deforestation over about 200 years.

Effect

Global warming and climate change.

Evidence

Melting ice caps, rising seas, extreme weather.
