Inside an atom

Where is the mass?

Nearly all in the nucleus

Virtually all the mass of an atom is in the nucleus, because electrons have a much smaller mass.

A tiny nucleus

Scale a helium atom up to the size of a sports hall and the nucleus would be no bigger than a grain of sand.

Inside an atom

Relative values and electrons

Why “relative”?

Real masses and charges are tiny, so we give them relative to each other.

It takes about 6 × 10²³ protons to weigh just 1 g.

Electrons don’t orbit like planets

We only know their energy and the region they are likely to be found in.

Atomic number and mass number

Two numbers that tell you what an atom is made of.

Atomic number and mass number

Two definitions to learn

Key term · atomic number

The number of protons in an atom’s nucleus (also called the proton number).

Key term · mass number

The total number of protons + neutrons in the nucleus (also called the nucleon number).

Counting protons, neutrons and electrons

Three simple rules turn two numbers into a full count of the particles.

Counting particles

The three rules

  1. Number of protons = atomic number.
  2. Number of electrons = number of protons, because atoms are electrically neutral (+1 and −1 charges cancel).
  3. Number of neutrons = mass number − atomic number.
number of neutrons = mass number − atomic number
neutrons = A − Z
A = mass numberZ = atomic number
Why protons?The atomic number is defined by protons, not electrons, because electrons can change in reactions (when ions form).

Worked example

Uranium from the Periodic Table

The Periodic Table shows uranium with the numbers 238 and 92. How many protons, neutrons and electrons?
ProtonsThe atomic number is the smaller number: 92, so 92 protons.
ElectronsElectrons = protons = 92.
NeutronsNeutrons = 238 − 92 = 146
92 protons, 146 neutrons, 92 electrons

Isotopes

Atoms of the same element do not all have the same mass.

Isotopes

What are isotopes?

Key term · isotopes

Atoms of the same element which have the same atomic number but different mass numbers.

In other wordsSame number of protons, different numbers of neutrons.

The same

Protons, atomic number, electrons — so the same chemical reactions.

Different

Number of neutrons, so a different mass number.

Why the same chemistry?

Chemical properties are controlled by the number and arrangement of electrons.

Relative atomic mass

Why the Periodic Table says chlorine is 35.5 — and how to calculate it.

Relative atomic mass

What is Aᵣ?

Key term · relative atomic mass (Aᵣ)

The average mass of an atom, taking into account the amount of each isotope present in a naturally occurring sample of an element.

35.5 can’t be a mass number

If it were, chlorine would have 35.5 − 17 = 18.5 neutrons. You can’t have half a neutron!

No single chlorine atom has a mass of 35.5.

It is an average

Chlorine is a mixture of ³⁵Cl and ³⁷Cl. Aᵣ is the average mass of all its atoms.

So the answer, 35.5, lies between 35 and 37.

Relative atomic mass

Why chlorine is 35.5

Closer to 35

35.5 is closer to 35 than to 37, so there is more chlorine-35.

Periodic Table

The top number is Aᵣ. It equals the mass number of the commonest isotope — except for chlorine (35.5) and copper (63.5).

Worked example

Aᵣ of boron

Boron is 20% ¹⁰B and 80% ¹¹B. Calculate the Aᵣ of boron.
Imagine100 atoms: 20 have mass 10, 80 have mass 11.
Total mass20 × 10 + 80 × 11 = 200 + 880 = 1080
Averagetotal mass ÷ 100 atoms = 1080 ÷ 100
Aᵣ of boron = 10.8

Relative atomic mass

The Aᵣ formula

relative atomic mass = weighted average of the isotope masses
Aᵣ = (% × mass of isotope 1 + % × mass of isotope 2 + …) ÷ 100
% = abundance of each isotopemass = its mass number

A weighted average

Each isotope counts as much as there is of it. With three or four isotopes, do it the same way.

Exam tip · check your answerAᵣ must lie between the isotope masses and be closest to the most common isotope.

Worked example

Aᵣ of chlorine

Chlorine is 75% chlorine-35 and 25% chlorine-37. Calculate its Aᵣ.
FormulaAᵣ = (% × mass 1 + % × mass 2) ÷ 100
SubstituteAᵣ = (75 × 35 + 25 × 37) ÷ 100
Work out= (2625 + 925) ÷ 100 = 3550 ÷ 100
Aᵣ = 35.5

Don’t lose marks

Common mistakes

Mixing up the numbersThe atomic number is the smaller of the two numbers.
Isotopes and reactionsIsotopes do not react differently: same electrons, same chemistry.
Subtracting the wrong wayNeutrons = mass number − atomic number, not the other way round.
The Periodic TableThe top number in the Periodic Table is Aᵣ, not always a mass number.

Chapter 3 in a nutshell

Atom = smallest piece of an element; molecule = 2 or more atoms bonded
Nucleus: protons (+1, mass 1) and neutrons (0, mass 1)
Electrons (−1, mass 1/1836) in shells around the nucleus
Atomic number = protons; mass number = protons + neutrons
Neutrons = mass number − atomic number
Electrons = protons in a neutral atom
Isotopes: same protons, different neutrons, same chemistry
