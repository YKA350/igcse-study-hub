The Periodic Table

How the table is arranged

Key term · atomic number

The number of protons in the nucleus. Elements are arranged in order of atomic number.

Mendeleev

The modern table was first presented in 1869 by the Russian chemist Dmitri Mendeleev.

Groups and periods

Vertical columns are groups (1 to 7, then 0). Horizontal rows are periods. Period 1 is just hydrogen and helium.

Named groups

Group 1 = alkali metals
Group 7 = halogens
Group 0 = noble gases

RememberMetals are on the left, non-metals on the right. Transition metals sit in the middle.
Exam tipHydrogen is not in Group 1: it is a non-metal.

Electronic configurations

How electrons fill the shells.

Electron shells

Filling the shells

Key idea

Electrons move around the nucleus in energy levels (shells). The lowest levels, closest to the nucleus, fill first.

1st shellholds 2
→
2nd shellholds 8
→
3rd shellholds 8
Common mistakeThe circles show energy levels, not paths. The electrons do not orbit along them.
  1. Look up the atomic number: chlorine is 17, so 17 electrons.
  2. Fill from the inside: 2, then 8, then 7.
  3. Write 2, 8, 7.
  4. Check: 2 + 8 + 7 = 17.
Exam tipAlways check that the electrons add up to the atomic number.

Position and electrons

The table tells you the electron arrangement.

Position and electrons

Two important facts

Group = outer electrons

Elements in a group have the same number of outer electrons. For Groups 1 to 7, outer electrons = group number.

Period = shells

The period number is the number of occupied shells. Calcium (2, 8, 8, 2) is Period 4.

Exam tipBarium (Group 2) has 2 outer electrons, iodine (Group 7) has 7.

Position and electrons

Similar chemical properties

Group 1

React vigorously with water, making hydrogen and hydroxides: LiOH, NaOH, KOH.

Group 7

React with hydrogen: HF, HCl, HBr.

Group 2

Form chlorides: MgCl₂, CaCl₂.

Why?

Reactions depend on the outer electrons, the ones used in bonding.

RememberSame group → same outer electrons → similar reactions.
