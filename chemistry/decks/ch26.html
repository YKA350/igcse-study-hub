3

The dichromate(VI) practical

Orange to green.

Method

Oxidising ethanol in the lab

  1. Few drops of ethanol into orange potassium dichromate(VI) and dilute sulfuric acid.
  2. Heat in a hot water bath: ethanol is flammable.
  3. Solution turns green: Cr³⁺ ions form.
  4. Tube holds dilute ethanoic acid.

Details

Why the details matter

Acid needed

Without H⁺ the dichromate(VI) cannot oxidise.

Safety

Eye protection; add slowly; don't point tube at anyone.

A sample

Heat under reflux with excess oxidising agent, then distil.

Conditions

Conditions and why

No air

Anaerobic: with air, yeast makes CO₂ and water instead.

About 30 °C

Optimum 30–40 °C. Too cold: slow. Above about 40 °C: enzymes denatured.

Exam tipIt takes several days.

Exam technique

Don't lose marks

Say this

–OH is covalent. Fermentation needs no air. Dichromate(VI): orange to green. Hydration: steam, phosphoric acid, 300 °C, 60–70 atm.

Avoid this

Calling alcohols alkalis; fermenting with air; saying dichromate goes clear.
