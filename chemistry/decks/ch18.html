Limewater

Why limewater goes milky

Equation

Ca(OH)₂(aq) + CO₂(g) → CaCO₃(s) + H₂O(l)

Milky

White precipitate of calcium carbonate.

Excess CO₂

The milkiness clears: soluble calcium hydrogencarbonate forms.

Exam tipAlways give the test and the result.

Testing for water

Chemical and physical.

Water

Is water present?

Test

CuSO₄(s) + 5H₂O(l) → CuSO₄·5H₂O(s)

Limit

Shows water is present, not that it is pure.

Spec 2.49 describe a test for the presence of water using anhydrous copper(II) sulfate
Purity

Is the water pure?

Pure water

Freezes at exactly 0 °C; boils at exactly 100 °C at 1 atm.

Impure water

Freezes lower; boils higher.

Measure

Use a thermometer for freezing or boiling point.

Tests for cations

Sodium hydroxide.

Cations

Coloured precipitates

Method

Dissolve in distilled water; add about the same volume of dilute NaOH.

Look for

A precipitate: the solution goes cloudy. Test a solution, not a solid.

Why acid

Why add acid first?

Removes carbonate

Acid removes CO₃²⁻ and OH⁻, which also give precipitates.

Use nitric

HCl would add chloride ions.

Not sulfuric

It would add sulfate ions.

Exam technique

Don't lose marks

Say this

Say precipitate, not only a colour. Fe³⁺ orange-brown, Fe²⁺ green. Anhydrous CuSO₄ white to blue.

Avoid this

Squeaky pop without the lighted splint; chlorine turns litmus blue; ammonia bleaches.
